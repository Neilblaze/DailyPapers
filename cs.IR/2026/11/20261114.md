### TokenFormer V2: Breaking the Resolution-Complexity Dilemma in Unified Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19074v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19074v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 01:24:46+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 01:24:46+00:00
- **Authors**: Kaihui Wu, Guoming Li, Liang Guo, Yifeng Zhou, Yuehong Hu, Junwei Pan, Zhixiang Feng, Chengguo Yin, Wenqi Fan, Haijie Gu, Jie Jiang
- **Comment**: Technical Preprint. 12 pages, 5 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Industrial recommendation faces a resolution--complexity dilemma: dense interactions make detailed modeling of long behavior histories expensive, while compact history summaries restrict the evidence available to prediction. A unified backbone must preserve rich behavioral details within strict serving budgets. We introduce \textbf{TokenFormer V2}, a unified architecture that explicitly decouples \emph{representation resolution} from \emph{interaction density}. Its group-wise token layout jointly encodes behavior segments with non-sequential context, expands interaction ranges via hierarchical group merging, and coordinates distributed groups through \emph{NS Fusion} and \emph{Global Sync} modules. Through this \emph{local--global decomposition} paradigm, computation is concentrated on structured interactions while preserving fine-grained evidence, ensuring that every encoded event remains directly accessible to the \emph{Full-Resolution Readout} module. On the Tencent Advertising Algorithm Competition 2026 (TAAC 2026) benchmark, TokenFormer V2 achieves top-ranking accuracy among unified recommendation baselines while using \textbf{5.1$\times$ fewer FLOPs} than leading baselines, sustaining its advantage across long histories and imbalanced behavior domains. Furthermore, in billion-parameter evaluations on WeChat Channels advertising, it consistently outperforms state-of-the-art industrial baselines, yielding up to a \textbf{+0.07 pp} overall AUC gain. In live online A/B testing against the production model, TokenFormer V2 delivers a statistically significant \textbf{2.33\% increase in GMV}. These results establish TokenFormer V2 as a battle-tested blueprint for scalable, full-resolution behavior modeling in industrial recommendation.



### Knowledge-Guided Local-to-Global Anomaly Learning for Longitudinal Medication Trajectories
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19106v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19106v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 02:30:55+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 02:30:55+00:00
- **Authors**: Wendi Ji
- **Comment**: 16 pages, 3 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Detecting suspicious deviations in longitudinal medication trajectories requires distinguishing knowledge-consistent treatment variations from anomalous changes and linking local deviations to account-level decisions. We propose a knowledge-guided local-to-global (L2G) anomaly learning framework that addresses both challenges. It constructs proxy supervision at three granularities: medication occurrences, treatment episodes, and contiguous visit segments. Knowledge-consistent medication and episode replacements provide plausible negatives, while cross-category and cross-account perturbations provide suspicious proxies. A medication-multiset encoder and causal visit-sequence encoder feed a shared scoring head that produces visit-level anomaly logits. Top-\(α\) aggregation derives account-level scores from these logits, while account-level classification and class-balanced visit-localization objectives jointly optimize the same visit-level evidence. Experiments on two city cohorts compare L2G with native and proxy-adapted anomaly methods. On City-A, L2G outperforms the strongest comparators. Cross-city results show more consistent gains in localization than in account-level detection.



### CLEAR: Legible and Steerable Recommendation via Grounded Natural-Language Interests
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19110v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19110v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 02:44:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 02:44:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Qi Guo, Reza Mirghaderi, Iordanis Fostiropoulos, David Fan, Yuchen Liu, Shiliang Tang, Lukasz Korycki, Golnaz Ghasemiesfeh, Xinzhu Bei, Sasha Vorontsov, Hanchao Yu, Brendan Quinlivan, Khoi Pham, Jigar Gada, Hao Yu, Felix Li, Hang Gao, Boyu Fang, Yang Gao, Yale Thomas, Xiaomeng Jin, Avishek Dutta, Hong Hong, Janet Xia, Ethan Zhu, Ellie Pan, Xiang Shen, Jawwad Zakaria, Jinfeng Lin, Jonathan Rush, Ruixue Lian, Noah Kennedy, Brian Vanover, Jas Pyneni, Daniel Thiyagu, Apoorv Saxena, Sanyogeeta Lawande, Huayu Ding, Hima Cherukuru, Yang Liu, Chen Kovacs, Qunshu Zhang, Lizhu Zhang, Benyu Zhang, Kate Chen, Ashish Singal, Danny Kanoff, Zhiyu Liu, Jiayi Liu, Jianyu Wang, Fei Liu, Xiangjun Fan, Abhishek Kumar, Hong Yan
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Modern recommenders keep learned preferences in representations people cannot inspect or change. We present CLEAR (Controllable and Legible End-to-end Architecture for Recommendation), an end-to-end system deployed at Meta and built around an editable natural-language interest layer. CLEAR makes a ranked subset of inferred interests readable and stores accepted additions and suppressions as authoritative preference state. Behavioral grounding maps each inferred interest back to supporting events in the user's own history. The resulting interest object is also a serving address: its label and profile-conditioned rewrites drive lexical retrieval, its supporting events seed behavioral retrieval, its projection queries an embedding index, and its stable identity supports ranking, attribution, and exposure control. A fresh user declaration may precede behavioral support, so it enters through the same serving identity and initially draws candidates from topical retrieval. Ranking makes those candidates competitive, while a delivery controller observes attributable exposure and replenishes underexposed interests. Direct user review found that users rated about 88.5% of displayed interests accurate. In an offline benchmark using a shared evaluation protocol, the Specialized 3B generator reached 0.3967 mean-user F1@10, compared with 0.3633 for Gemini 2.5 Pro and 0.1911 for the prior generator. For a newly declared interest, a user-randomized cumulative ablation measured same-day delivery losses of 8.7 percentage points for added-interest ranking terms, 11.1 for reserved top-slot insertion, and 13.7 for interest-conditioned candidate sourcing, conditional on the tested removal sequence. CLEAR components have operated in production for over one year; the system spans four surfaces on Instagram and Facebook, with an interest layer people can inspect, correct, and use to direct recommendations.



### LABEL: Literature Asymmetry between Efficacy and Limits in Drug Knowledge and Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19166v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19166v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 05:14:50+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 05:14:50+00:00
- **Authors**: S M Rayeed, Charles V. Stewart, Yao Ma
- **Comment**: 27 pages (9 main + supplementary material), 8 figures, 7 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Every medication comes with two types of medical facts: indications, which describe what conditions the drug treats (benefits), and contraindications, which describe who must avoid it to prevent serious harm (safety limits). Although contraindications are critical for patient safety and FDA regulations require listing them as rigorously as indications, we find that contraindications are far less documented than indications in general prose. In this paper, we measure how often these texts contain both facts across 574 drugs from PrimeKG by comparing a drug's indications against its own contraindications. Across Wikipedia articles and PubMed abstracts, we find that reference prose names a drug's benefits about twice as often as its safety limits (within-drug odds ratio 2.19). We call this documentation gap LABEL (Literature Asymmetry between Efficacy and Limits). Language models mirror this gap. As we scale Qwen2.5 from 0.5B to 14B, accuracy on indications rises by 18.3 points, whereas accuracy on contraindications remains flat near random guessing. However, when we provide models with a retrieved passage that states the answer, they use safety facts just as effectively as benefits. Furthermore, routing safety questions to official FDA labels boosts accuracy by 15.8 points whenever the label states the fact. Overall, our findings show that models fail on medication safety not because they cannot reason about risk, but because free-text corpora lack safety facts. Clinical AI systems should therefore route medication-safety questions to official regulatory labels and structured databases rather than relying on general public records. Code and data are available at https://github.com/SMRayeed/LABEL.



### Revisiting What Makes a Good Context Subset: A Sparse Latent Basis Perspective
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19220v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19220v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 07:26:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 07:26:16+00:00
- **Authors**: Cen-Jhih Li, Siddhant Chaudhary, Aditya Bhaskara
- **Comment**: 17 pages, 2 figures. The format of ACL rolling review
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Selecting context subsets is increasingly important in modern LLM inference, yet what makes a subset effective remains unclear. We study this question in the controlled setting of few-shot demonstration selection. We examine a hypothesis that demonstrations should collectively provide task-relevant information needed by a target query. Assuming that the query representation provides a useful proxy for these requirements, we formulate selection as sparse reconstruction and instantiate it with Residual Explanation Search (RES), a Matching-Pursuit-style diagnostic procedure. Across seven benchmarks and eight open-weight LLMs, no retrieval objective is universally best. RES outperforms MMR in 34 of 56 model--dataset pairs, with its clearest advantage on sentiment tasks, suggesting that query-conditioned collective reconstruction may capture useful complementarity that pairwise dissimilarity misses. Relevance-based retrieval remains strong on template-heavy reasoning benchmarks, while all retrieval methods underperform zero-shot prediction on average on StrategyQA, where a solution-reconstruction analysis is consistent with the query not exposing the required external knowledge. Target-model hidden states further show that retrieval objectives interact differently with representation spaces: middle-layer representations generally favor similarity retrieval, whereas last-layer representations are often stronger for residual- and diversity-based selection. These findings support a conditional, representation-dependent account of context subset quality rather than a universally superior selector.



### Keep it Simple! A Multiple-Choice Task can Benefit Generative Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19320v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19320v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 10:32:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 10:32:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Wei-Hsiang Huang, Jyun-Yu Jiang, Pu-Jen Cheng
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Sequential recommendation has recently witnessed growing interest in generative retrieval. One of the most popular approaches is to autoregressively generate the semantic identifiers (SIDs) of items as recommendations. Existing efforts mainly rely on semantic alignment, sophisticated architectural designs, and extensive hyperparameter tuning. However, the conventional learning framework for generative models could have foundational limitations. Specifically, most generative models directly predict target items without explicitly learning negative compositions and relative ranking signals. This is not well aligned with the philosophy of Bayesian personalized ranking (BPR), which emphasizes the importance of relative rankings between positive and negative samples.   In this paper, we show that adding a simple multiple-choice auxiliary task into the learning objective can effectively address this issue. With minimal adaptation, we introduce Task Identifiers and Candidate Constructions to synthesize training data for the multiple-choice auxiliary task. In addition, we provide theoretical analysis of the fundamental benefits of the multiple-choice auxiliary task from the perspectives of information theory and compositional generalization. Extensive experiments on four publicly available benchmark datasets show that our approach can improve TIGER, a conventional generative recommendation model, by 4.9% to 79% in NDCG@10.



### User First Intelligence: Three Measurements of Search and AI-Answer Analytics (SEO and GEO/AEO), and a Knowledge-Graph Framework for Query Discovery Beyond Search Volume
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19655v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19655v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 22:51:48+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 22:51:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Venkata Pagadala
- **Comment**: 43 pages, 10 figures. Preprint, not peer reviewed. Data and scripts in the ancillary files; implementation at https://github.com/VenkataPagadalaGIT/ufi
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: People say what they want to know in many places besides a search box, and the tools that search and AI-answer optimization (SEO and GEO/AEO) rely on see only part of that demand, through models reported as counts. This paper reports three measurements of such instruments and a framework. On the area-code section of a large US telecom provider's site, two commercial analytics platforms differed on estimated monthly visits by 7.5x to 27.1x (8.6x to 29.9x worldwide); across twelve sections of the domain the ratio ran from 0.20x to 7.24x with no detectable relation to section age, while they agreed on which section is larger in 60 of 66 pairs. On 37,193 reviews of 378 telecom stores linked to a 42,994-node keyword taxonomy, review demand and search volume agreed on branches to a varying degree (Spearman 0.47 to 0.75; 0.91 post hoc) and barely on topics (0.18), on links two blind AI annotators judged two-thirds correct; on the 372 area codes, a public count of Wikipedia requests for each code and vendor volume agreed roughly on ranking (0.45 over 240 articles, gone once keywords matched per code are held constant) and not on magnitudes, predictions fixed first. In one vendor's index of AI answers naming or citing the largest companies of each S&P 500 sector, of four predictions written first, one held, one in direction only and two failed; video and community sites took a median 21% of citation incidences on Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode against 0.6% to 9.2% on four other surfaces, in every sector, and in education answers in each of 13 countries read later. Estimates and sources need measuring per instrument and surface. User First Intelligence (UFI), built on that premise, collects what people ask beyond the website, links it to an industry ontology and scores where an organization does not answer; an open implementation is released, its score not evaluated. No causal claim is made.
