### The abc index: Exact Retrieval for Learned Sparse Representations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28555v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28555v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 04:21:29+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 04:21:29+00:00
- **Authors**: Rossano Venturini
- **Comment**: 13 pages, 2 figures, 5 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In learned sparse retrieval (LSR), queries have tens of terms with learned weights, on which classic dynamic pruning is believed to fail, and approximate indexes such as Seismic and HNSW are considered faster than exact ones. We challenge this view with abc, an exact index built only from standard IR techniques: term-at-a-time (TAAT) evaluation, Block-Max MaxScore, binary packing and doc-id reordering. They are combined in a new design and engineered for modern CPUs. On MS MARCO encoded with four LSR models, the abc index is the fastest exact index at every k: 6.5-84x faster than the exact search of Qdrant, 13-55x faster than that of Lucene, and 2.9-18x faster than BMP with an index 5-7x smaller. Against approximate indexes, it takes 1.5-3.6x less space, and its latency grows much more slowly with k: they are up to 4.3x faster at k=10, but at k=1000 it overtakes them, by up to 7.4x. Exactness comes with an extra benefit: there are no parameters to tune.



### MADRec: Reliable Multi-Agent Reasoning with Evidence-Grounded Debate for Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28604v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28604v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 06:31:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 06:31:16+00:00
- **Authors**: Zunlong Liu, Weijie Feng, Jingzhi Li, Lei Zhu, Zhiyong Cheng, Meng Wang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are transforming recommender systems from conventional preference matching toward reasoning-driven and explainable decision-making. Multi-agent collaboration further advances this paradigm by distributing recommendation reasoning across specialized agents. However, existing frameworks rarely assess the reliability of intermediate reasoning during collaboration, allowing erroneous judgments to propagate across agents and ultimately degrade recommendation quality. To address this issue, we propose Multi-Agent Debate-based Recommendation (MADRec), an evidence-grounded multi-agent framework that enhances the reliability of multi-agent recommendation reasoning. MADRec consists of three coordinated modules. First, the Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) module enables an Interaction Agent and a Semantic Agent to analyze interaction signals and content-level semantic signals from complementary perspectives, generating diverse recommendation judgments through structured debate. Second, the Evidence-Grounded Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning (EG-RAR) module grounds the Semantic Agent's reasoning in item-level evidence and refines candidate scores by assessing how well the evidence supports the corresponding semantic claims. Third, the Judge-based Ranking Calibration (JRC) module leverages debate-derived reliability cues to adaptively calibrate interaction-based and semantic ranking results for the final recommendation. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate that MADRec consistently outperforms competitive baselines and enhances the reliability of multi-agent recommendation reasoning.



### PROSPER: LLMs as Sparse Retrievers for First-Stage Product Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28645v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28645v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 08:14:41+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 08:14:41+00:00
- **Authors**: Hongru Song, Yu-An Liu, Ruqing Zhang, Jiafeng Guo, Maarten de Rijke, Sen Li, Wenjun Peng, Fuyu Lv, Xueqi Cheng
- **Comment**: Accepted at WSDM 2027(oral)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Product search is a crucial component of large-scale e-commerce platforms, where first-stage retrieval should achieve high recall while ensuring efficient online deployment. Sparse retrieval remains attractive for this setting due to its interpretability and deployability, yet it suffers from severe vocabulary mismatch challenges, especially for short queries and product titles. Large language models (LLMs) offer strong semantic capabilities, but directly applying them to sparse retrieval in product search introduces new challenges: (i) semantic expansion may distort critical literal signals such as brand names and model identifiers; and (ii) learning meaningful sparse representations is difficult in the high-dimensional vocabulary space induced by LLMs.To address these challenges, we propose PROSPER, a framework that uses LLMs as sparse retrievers for product search while explicitly preserving lexical fidelity. PROSPER combines (i) a literal residual network that alleviates lexical expansion hallucination by reinforcing underweighted literal terms through a residual compensation mechanism; and (ii) a lexical focusing window that uses top-k selection for rapid early sparsification and effective training initialization. Extensive offline evaluations and large-scale online A/B tests demonstrate that PROSPER consistently improves over existing sparse retrievers, and provides revenue gains for industrial hybrid retrieval systems.



### ChatGPT Can't Sell (And That's Probably a Good Thing): Buyer-Aligned AI Advice, the Sales-Representative Gap, and Evidence from Commercial Conversations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28946v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28946v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 17:28:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 17:28:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Benjamin Tannenbaum
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: AI assistants and merchant sales agents have different objectives: assistants should help buyers compare options and preserve the choice not to purchase, while sales agents also guide qualified buyers toward an authorized next step. We study this sales-representative gap with three evidence streams. In 12,178 historical English GPT-family conversations screened for commercial or transactional intent, a 279-request sample contained recommendation language in 63 first replies (22.6%) and no narrow transactional action wording. In a 56-conversation explicit-buying sample, 24 replies (42.9%) contained recommendation markers, 32 (57.1%) qualification phrases, and none transaction-specific phrases. These lexical measures do not estimate successful selling. A 2026 brand-monitoring cohort of 1,050 GPT-5.4--Gemini answer pairs over 383 purchase-related prompts showed transaction-specific phrases in 0.0% of recommendation-worded prompts, 0.21% of price/comparison prompts, and 9.52% of explicitly transaction-worded prompts. The prompts are selected, not organic shopping sessions. In a prospective pilot across five matched hypothetical product scenarios, sales instructions produced more next-step language than general-assistant instructions (5/5 vs. 2/5), but no increase in clean product choice (3/5 in both); unsupported offers and fit errors also occurred. This pilot cannot establish a population effect. A separate company-affiliated randomized field study reports higher engaged-visitor conversion for Javo, a commercially trained sales agent, than for a frontier-model agent in shared tooling, but does not identify the mechanism. We argue that apparent reluctance to sell can be appropriate for buyer-aligned assistants. Merchant agents should optimize qualified decision progress through catalog grounding, truthful authorization, and safeguards against unsuitable sales.



### Who Gets Re-ranked When a Clinical Model Gains New Data?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28968v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28968v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 18:05:00+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 18:05:00+00:00
- **Authors**: Priyanka Siddappa, Muntaqim Ahmed Raju, Shifat Haider Al Amin, Ruizhe Ma
- **Comment**: Accepted at the 6th International Workshop on Multi-Modal Medical Data Analysis (MMMDA), IEEE BigData 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Hospitals often update a deployed risk model by adding a data source, such as laboratory results, that only some patients have. Work on safe model updates assumes fixed inputs and so ignores patients who lack the new data. The update has no new measurements for them, yet their rank can change, and a sick patient not yet tested can drop off an alert list. We split rank-based compatibility by data availability and pair it with error correction and alert-list effects. We compared each update with ordinary retraining on the old inputs, predicting ICU admission or death and hospital admission, on 78,475 emergency department visits from MC-MED and, in a pre-registered replication, 100,000 stays from MIMIC-IV-ED at a second hospital. Radiograph report text gave no detectable gain and almost no extra re-ranking. Laboratory results improved prediction but, at both hospitals and with linear or tree models, pushed patients without labs down alert lists; linear updates also reshuffled them more than retraining did, without ranking them better. A top-20% admission list caught 4.2% of admitted patients without labs after the update, against 18.8% after retraining (second hospital: 4.9% against 22.6%), while catching more patients overall. This held for list sizes from 5% to 50% and in every patient group we checked, despite better measured calibration. Separate alert thresholds for patients with and without labs restored most or all of their catch rate at both hospitals at almost no cost. Teams should check these patients before deploying such an update.



### HME-PFD: Heterogeneous Mixture-of-Experts Privileged Feature Distillation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29094v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29094v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 23:32:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 23:32:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Wen Xu, Xiaomin Zhang, Yucheng Yang, Danny Liu, Xunlong Gui, Fei Huang, Sha Meng
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Knowledge distillation (KD) is widely used to transfer knowledge from large teacher models to smaller student models, facilitating efficient serving of machine learning systems. In the context of ads recommendation, privileged features distillation (PFD) extends KD by allowing the teacher model to leverage privileged features, i.e., features available only during training, to enhance distillation performance. However, in real-world scenarios, privileged features often have limited coverage and exhibit heterogeneous distributions, resulting in training examples of varying importance for distillation and model learning. We propose Heterogeneous Mixture-of-Experts Privileged Feature Distillation (HME-PFD), a novel approach that incorporates privileged features into the routing module of a Mixture-of-Experts-based student model. Our method introduces both hard-coded and learnable routing modules, enabling the student to dynamically determine expert assignment based on the availability and values of privileged features. This design allows the student model to better handle the heterogeneous data scenario, where privileged features are partially observed, by differentiating between high- and low-signal samples. Extensive experiments on two post-click conversion prediction tasks demonstrate that HME-PFD consistently outperforms baseline methods. Our results show that leveraging privileged features for expert routing in the student model leads to improved model performance, even when privileged features are incomplete or sparse.
