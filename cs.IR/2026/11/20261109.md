### Proactive Dimension-Adaptive Denoising for Graph Collaborative Filtering via Representation Coherence
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11801v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11801v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 02:55:44+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 02:55:44+00:00
- **Authors**: Wenjun Li, Dong Zhao, Jiaying Shi, Juan Zhang, Zhaoming Pan, Zhimin Zhang
- **Comment**: code available at https://github.com/GordonDongZHAO/NDL
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Implicit feedback in recommender systems contains noisy interactions (e.g., accidental clicks, impulse purchases) that propagate through user-item graphs and contaminate learned representations. Existing explicit denoising methods uniformly rely on training loss to estimate interaction reliability. However, loss is a fundamentally passive signal: it detects noise only after it has caused prediction errors, and it is heavily confounded by training stage, negative sampling, and hard positives. We propose Noise-aware Dimension Learning (NDL), a proactive dimension-adaptive denoising framework. NDL introduces Representation Coherence Detection (RCD), which evaluates the coherence between an interaction representation and the user's historical preference centroid in embedding space, providing an active noise signal independent of loss. RCD and the loss signal are fused via activity-aware weighting (active users rely more on coherence while cold-start users automatically fall back to loss), and the fused estimate drives an interaction-level dimension-adaptive mask that limits the effective representation capacity of unreliable interactions. A lightweight contrastive regularizer further improves embedding uniformity, stabilizing the geometry on which RCD discriminates. On three public benchmarks NDL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art denoising methods, lifting Recall@5 by +17.95% on Amazon-book, +12.33% on Yelp, and +5.63% on MovieLens-1M over the strongest baseline DAR; the core variant without contrastive learning already surpasses DAR on sparser datasets, confirming representation coherence as the primary driver of these gains. NDL is confined to the training loop and is serving-compatible with LightGCN-based production pipelines, adding no online latency or parameters at inference time. Code: https://github.com/GordonDongZHAO/NDL

- **Summary**: Agricultural documents, such as prefectural technical reports and crop calendars, are critical knowledge resources. To address these issues, the NTCIR-19 DAGRI (Data Analytics for aGRicultural Information) task introduced three subtasks focusing on different dimensions of document intelligence. In the Table Information Extraction (TableIE) task, we developed a multi-stage pipeline combining structure-preserving parsing via MinerU, region-adaptive segmentation for long documents, and a dual-modal (vision-text) extraction strategy for complex work schedules. ### CHO-Bench: How Do Context Hints Affect Ad Rankings in LLM Advertising?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11955v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11955v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 06:19:53+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 06:19:53+00:00
- **Authors**: Shiqi Zhang, Yang Fei, Xiaokui Xiao
- **Comment**: 11 pages, 6 figures, 4 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLM advertising selects advertisements based on user conversations, where user needs may extend beyond explicit product searches. ChatGPT Ads allows advertisers to provide natural-language context hints describing what they offer, who may find it useful, and when it may be useful, as additional information for ranking advertisements across diverse user conversations. However, how to effectively construct these hints remains unclear. We formulate Context Hint Optimization (CHO) as the problem of constructing context hints to improve an advertisement's ranking under a given budget. Drawing on established marketing frameworks, we develop seven context hint generation strategies covering What, Who, and When, along with a Unified strategy that combines all seven dimensions. We introduce CHO-Bench and conduct over 1.19 million paired evaluations across five datasets using embedding-based and LLM-based rankers. Our results show that context hints improve advertisement rankings under the evaluated offline rankers. While individual strategies achieve similar average gains and Unified offers no consistent advantage, strategy differences become more pronounced under specific generation-ranking configurations, with embedding-based and LLM-based rankers favoring different information dimensions. Increasing the hint budget further improves rankings with diminishing marginal gains. These findings establish an offline benchmark for studying ad-side ranking optimization in LLM advertising and highlight opportunities for model-specific hint selection and budget-aware generation and aggregation.

- **Summary**: Late Chunking encodes an entire document before pooling at chunk boundaries, preserving document context in local retrieval units. Chunk-level post-training improves context-dependent retrieval, but shared context can make semantically similar same-document negatives difficult to distinguish. We study the local decision boundary between a gold chunk and its hardest sibling. Starting from InSeNT, we analyze InSeNT's within-document gradients and show that the entropy of the conditional negative allocation decreases monotonically with the scaling factor at fixed logits. ### TS-GEAR: Taobao Shangou GEnerative Ad Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12180v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12180v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 10:30:44+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 10:30:44+00:00
- **Authors**: Guofeng Tong, Xu Zhang, Guangliang Yu, Haiqiang Wang, Guannan Cong, Xing Guo, Yang Zhou, Jingjing Duan, Shuang Li, Feng Shi, Hualei Liu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: On-demand delivery platforms such as DoorDash and Uber Eats impose a constraint absent in traditional e-commerce: merchants beyond the user's delivery range are invalid candidates rather than merely low-ranked. Generative retrieval has recently shown strong potential in large-scale retrieval systems. Adapting it to this setting, however, poses three challenges. First, existing semantic IDs encode geography only as a soft signal, so reachability can only rely on a non-differentiable post-generation hard truncation. Second, user preferences vary substantially across both regions and queries, so aggregating historical behaviors without conditioning on geography and the current query yields biased interest estimates. Third, the retrieved candidate set is evaluated by its collective value, yet autoregressive training scores each sequence independently, with no set-level signal to discourage redundant near-duplicates. To address the challenges, we propose TS-GEAR, a unified generative retrieval framework for on-demand delivery: (1) Geo-Semantic ID splits merchant identifiers into geographic prefixes and semantic suffixes, making reachability a prediction target rather than a post-hoc filter; (2) Query-Geo Target Attention aggregates historical behaviors using the real-time query and location as dual targets, while encoding each behavior's own location into its attention key; (3) GMPO (Group Marginal Policy Optimization) rewrites advantages using a deduplicated group reward and marginal advantage within GRPO, avoiding auxiliary diversity objectives. Deployed online with a lightweight 0.1B backbone, TS-GEAR serves over 150 million queries per day in the search advertising pipeline of Taobao Shangou, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art generative retrieval methods on both HitRate and geographic validity, and achieving 3.8% gains in ad impressions and 4.2% in ad revenue.



### RecoGEM: A Serving System for Production-Scale Generative Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12296v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DC**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12296v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 12:20:07+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 12:20:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Xiangrui Yang, Yunfeng Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Fangyu Zhang, Enrui Zhao, Liang Zeng, Cheng Peng, Minhui Xie, Jiaqiang Liu, Xiao Liang, Yiming Qiu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative recommendation (GR) is moving industrial recommender systems (RecSys) from scoring candidate items to autoregressively generating item identifiers. This shift promises higher recommendation quality, but makes serving substantially harder: each request executes a short sequence of dependent decoding steps that explore a large item space and select promising candidates on the latency-critical path. GR not only creates structural mismatches with conventional RecSys pipelines, but also challenges the architectural and optimization assumptions of LLM serving stacks, making direct adoption of existing systems difficult. We present RecoGEM, our production GR serving system. RecoGEM uses a runtime--engine decoupling architecture to move GR-specific serving responsibilities, such as candidate-state transitions and live artifact updates, to a dedicated runtime. Its execution engine exploits GR's short token sequences, large candidate populations, and predictable step shapes to optimize self-attention, candidate selection, and execution planning. RecoGEM has been deployed for over nine months across our production lines, serving applications with hundreds of millions of users and supporting more than ten GR model families. On our OneModel production workloads, RecoGEM reaches over 1,000 QPS per serving instance with P99 latency below 35 ms and a 2.88$\times$ throughput improvement over representative baselines.



### Hidden Distances: How Likert-Scale Spacing Shapes Algorithmic Voting Advice
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12313v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CY**, cs.IR, physics.soc-ph
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12313v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 12:36:10+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 12:36:10+00:00
- **Authors**: Thomas Broholt Knudsen, Christian Høst-Madsen, Vedran Sekara, Jonas L. Juul
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Voting Advice Applications (VAAs) are algorithmic recommenders that match voters with political candidates on policy preferences. They are widely adopted across many multi-party democracies; in Denmark, 62\% of eligible voters used a VAA ahead of the 2022 general election, and 45\% of VAA users reported following its advice. VAAs typically ask voters and candidates to respond to policy statements on a Likert scale, from ``strongly disagree'' to ``strongly agree'', and recommend candidates whose responses are closest to the voter's. Computing these distances requires assigning numbers to response options, a design choice without an empirical basis, since it is unknown how people perceive the distances between options. Here we show that this seemingly technical choice substantially changes whom voters are matched with. We parameterize the distance between ``(dis)agree'' and ``strongly (dis)agree'' relative to the distance between ``neutral'' and ``(dis)agree'' as $α$, and quantify how it changes recommendations for a popular Danish VAA. We find that moving from equidistant options to doubling this distance can change $37.7\%$ of top candidate matches and $21.9\%$ of top party matches, although no answers change. Low $α$-values favor candidates with strong positions, with some parties receiving up to $ 19.6\%$ more recommendations, while high $α$-values favor moderates. Similarly, female candidates receive fewer recommendations at low $α$ and more at high $α$-values. Our findings demonstrate that undisclosed quantification choices in algorithmic recommenders can carry considerable electoral consequences, and they call for transparency and sensitivity analysis in VAA design. As Likert-based matching also underpins dating platforms and roommate allocation systems, these implications extend well beyond elections.

### Multi-Agent Screening: What Agreement and Acceptance Cannot Tell You
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12705v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.MA**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12705v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-09 17:00:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-09 17:00:16+00:00
- **Authors**: Benjamin Chang, Dell Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: When AI agents screen items for a costly human assessor, as in agentic search, e-discovery and AI reviewing of papers, only what they pass gets judged. We show what each kind of evidence can establish about their errors, even with unlimited data. Agreement shows that agents concur, not that they are right, however many there are. Acceptance by the assessor gives precision but only a lower bound on recall. Knowing which agents found which relevant items gives an upper bound on recall if agents' finds are not negatively correlated, and caps what one more agent can add if agents are interchangeable. Without assumptions about the agents, recall can be pinned down only by judging items they did not pass: a random sample of these estimates how many relevant items were missed, to any chosen accuracy, and the sample needed does not grow with the number of agents.

- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate how people seek and consume information, including accounts that differ across languages and regions, especially on contested topics. The pipeline, the cutoff rule, and the per-cell user counts are publicly available.
