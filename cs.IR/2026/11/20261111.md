### When Does a Recommendation Spark Curiosity? Theory, Measurement, and Field Evidence
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14938v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **econ.GN**, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14938v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 02:39:23+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 02:39:23+00:00
- **Authors**: Pan Li, Alexander Tuzhilin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Curiosity, the drive to close the gap between what one knows and what one wants to know, pulls consumers beyond their familiar content, sustains engagement, and breaks them out of the filter bubbles of repetitive, relevance-only recommendations. Despite its importance, curiosity is hardly incorporated into modern recommender systems for lack of a formal measure. The information-gap theory of curiosity provides no formal quantitative model that a recommender system can operate on, and existing proxy metrics do not accurately measure the curiosity that a recommended item evokes. To fill this research gap, we formalize the information-gap theory into five axioms of curiosity and construct the canonical formulation of a computational measure of the curiosity that a recommended item evokes in a consumer. We validate our curiosity measure through a survey-based study, in which it matches consumers' self-reported curiosity and outperforms alternative proxy metrics. We subsequently build a curiosity-aware recommender system that incorporates this measure into the utility function design, and we show through offline experiments on three real-world datasets that it significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, we conduct a large-scale online controlled experiment at a major video-streaming platform in Asia, where we show that our method significantly increases click-through rate, video views, and time spent over the latest production system, leading to tangible economic impact for the company. Our method has since been deployed on the platform and remains in operation. Our findings establish consumer curiosity as a measurable and actionable objective for recommender systems, with important managerial implications for digital platforms to recommend what consumers wonder about.



### Measuring, Revealing and Mitigating Harmful Content Propagation in Recommender Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15160v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15160v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 07:57:00+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 07:57:00+00:00
- **Authors**: Xiaokun Zhang, Youlin Wu, Junyu Lu, Jiamin Chen, Zhaochun Ren, Hongfei Lin, Fenglong Ma, Xue Liu, Chen Ma
- **Comment**: This work has been acceptted by IEEE TPAMI
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recommender systems help users navigate vast amounts of information by filtering content according to their historical behaviors. As these systems increasingly shape everyday information access, they largely determine the content users are exposed to. However, current research typically prioritizes catering to user interest, while overlooking the existence and propagation of harmful content, such as violence, hate speech, and pornography. In this work, we take a systematic step toward investigating harmful content in recommender systems from three aspects: measuring, revealing, and mitigating. First, we construct datasets with harmfulness-related annotations and present new evaluation metrics to quantitatively measure the harmfulness of recommendation results. Second, our empirical analysis reveals that users tend to engage with harmful content and prevailing recommendation methods often contribute to the proliferation of such content. Third, to address this issue, we introduce a generic framework, MitiHarm, which integrates harmfulness-related signals into both item and user representation learning. Experiments show that MitiHarm can significantly mitigate the propagation of harmful content while preserving user satisfaction. Resources of this work are available at https://github.com/Zhang-xiaokun/MitiHarm.



### Vision-Grounded Semantic Learning and Alignment for Scalable Multilingual Text-Based Person Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15328v1
- **DOI**: 10.1145/3767308.3836472
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.CL, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15328v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 11:07:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 11:07:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Xinyu Zhou, Fan Li, Shasha Chen, Min Cao, Ahtamjan Ahmat, Qiaoming Zhu, Mang Ye
- **Comment**: Accepted by ACM MM 2026 (Oral). 10 pages
- **Journal**: Proceedings of the 34th ACM International Conference on Multimedia (MM '26), 2026
- **Summary**: Multilingual Text-Based Person Retrieval (TBPR) aims to retrieve individuals with texts in multiple languages. Despite its practical importance, it has been overlooked by existing research, which mainly focuses on English-only retrieval. The only prior attempt at multilingual TBPR suffers from (1) the absence of a large-scale, multilingual pretraining dataset and (2) a suboptimal model architecture for multilingual text encoding and cross-modal alignment, which employs a shared encoder that overlooks linguistic heterogeneity and relies on English as a pivot for cross-modal alignment, introducing English-centric bias. For this, we contribute MultiPedes, the first large-scale, pedestrian-centric multilingual pretraining dataset. It contains 10 million image-text pairs across four languages, constructed by a multilingual quality-aware data construction pipeline. We also propose ViSeLA (Vision-grounded Semantic Learning and Alignment), a novel framework featuring: (1) a semantic-aware multilingual learning module that extends the mixture-of-experts architecture by routing language-invariant semantics to specialized experts for improved multilingual feature learning; and (2) a vision-grounded cross-modal alignment module that employs a semantic probe to capture fine-grained semantics across modalities, enabling direct cross-modal alignment without relying on a pivot language. Extensive experiments on both multilingual and English-only TBPR datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of ViSeLA. The dataset and code are available at https://github.com/Flame-Chasers/ViSeLA



### When Direction Matters: Diagnosing Relational Instruction Following in Dense Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15417v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15417v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 12:44:22+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 12:44:22+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhanyu Wu, Richong Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dense retrievers increasingly claim the ability to condition retrieval on natural-language relevance criteria beyond a short query. We identify a specific weakness in this capability: the evaluated models perform well when lexical or attribute cues distinguish the target, but become much less reliable when relevance depends on a directional relation over the same entities. We introduce SwitchIR, a controlled diagnostic that contrasts Constraint Shift and Explicit Filtering with Relational Flip, where the query and document pair remain fixed while the requested relation direction reverses. Across representative embedding and late-interaction retrievers, the primary joint metric is high on the two cue-available controls and consistently low on Relational Flip; the same ordering persists in an LLM-assisted, manually reviewed realistic variant. An exact margin decomposition shows that both directions are correct only when the direction-sensitive query--document interaction exceeds the direction-invariant pair preference. Empirically, direction-sensitive changes are common, but their aligned interaction is usually too weak to overcome that preference. Diagnostic fine-tuning shows that models can learn relational behavior that generalizes across relation families, with stronger generalization in the larger model.



### SimRec: Learning to Search, Ask, and Recommend from Simulated Interaction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15426v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15426v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 12:53:52+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 12:53:52+00:00
- **Authors**: Yaxin Zhang, Xingyu Wu, Yuchen Yan, Yuqin Dai, Hong Zhao, Lixing Shen, Zhan Chen, Jian Shao, Yongliang Shen
- **Comment**: 19 pages,5 figures,16 tables. Code: https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/SimRec
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLM-based recommendation agents can search catalogs, inspect products, ask users, and revise rejected recommendations. However, these behaviors are often governed by hand-designed workflows, while existing training methods optimize isolated components such as query rewriting or list ranking rather than the interaction policy as a whole. We present SimRec, a reinforcement learning framework that trains the full interaction policy of a tool-integrated recommendation agent. SimRec introduces two components. A task-anchored user simulator provides feedback on every recommendation: conditioned on the current task need rather than on user profile, and it can be an external LLM or the recommender itself under self-play. A task-aligned reward optimizes the whole trajectory with GRPO, crediting natural retrieval quality and valid tool use alongside the final hit, with training-only target injection to handle sparse hits over large catalogs. On ESCI and Amazon C4, SimRec improves the NDCG@100 of a Qwen3-4B agent from 32.23% to 35.42% and from 22.94% to 24.72%}, outperforming training-free agents on far larger backbones and task-aligned baselines on NDCG. Analysis shows that the trained agent verifies candidates, uses preferences, and recovers from rejected recommendations far more often than its untrained counterpart. Our code is available at https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/SimRec .



### SurvEval: Listwise Re-Ranking Evaluator via Survival-Based Exposure and Multi-Objective Value Modeling
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15434v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15434v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 13:05:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 13:05:16+00:00
- **Authors**: YaChen Yan, Liubo Li
- **Comment**: 5 Pages, 3 figures, ACM conference format
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Re-ranking is a crucial stage in recommender systems, yet existing evaluators oversimplify user browsing assumptions and collapse multi-objective signals into a single scalar, limiting their ability to reflect true listwise utility. We propose \textbf{SurvEval}, a listwise evaluator that jointly models user exposure and item value for re-ranking evaluation. We formalize the sequential browsing process as a discrete-time survival model, deriving monotonically decreasing reaching probabilities that capture the cascading structure of user browsing. We further introduce a multi-objective value estimation module with dedicated per-objective prediction heads and uncertainty-based loss weighting to adaptively balance objectives of varying scales and noise levels. Both components are jointly optimized via a unified transformer backbone, and the listwise utility is obtained by summing the position-wise products of reaching probabilities and conditional item value estimates. Extensive experiments on KuaiRand and Avito demonstrate that SurvEval achieves superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines.



### ReAlign: Reward-Guided Preference Alignment for Generative Re-Ranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15445v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15445v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 13:16:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 13:16:18+00:00
- **Authors**: YaChen Yan, Liubo Li
- **Comment**: 9 pages, 3 figures, ACM conference format
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Re-ranking refines the upstream ranked list to maximize listwise user utility. Existing two-stage generator-evaluator methods decouple list generation from list evaluation, leaving the generator without direct access to the listwise utility signal, while scoring-based one-stage methods score items under a fixed upstream permutation context without explicitly searching the permutation space. We propose \textbf{ReAlign}, a reward-guided preference alignment framework that couples a listwise reward model with a candidate-aware autoregressive generator. The reward model decomposes the listwise reward into position-wise contributions, modeling user exposure through a survival-based exposure module and item value through per-objective heads for heterogeneous engagement signals. The generator is trained in two stages: supervised pre-training on logged exposure lists to establish a relevance prior, followed by reward-guided post-training that aligns the generator with the reward model through complementary distribution and ranking alignment over within-pool standardized rewards. Extensive offline experiments show that ReAlign consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines.



### When Local Definitions Are Missing: Stable Errors in Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15597v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15597v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 15:15:40+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 15:15:40+00:00
- **Authors**: Zengqing Wu, Chuan Xiao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Contracts, technical manuals, and online communities often give familiar words special meanings and define them in one place. A language model that reads a passage without that definition may use the usual meaning and give an answer that sounds right but is wrong. We study this with multiple-choice questions whose correct answers depend on a local definition. The questions come from a synthetic vocabulary, commercial contracts, Reddit community glossaries, and the documentation of a decentralized exchange. We ask three language models each question with and without the definition. Without using the answer key, we then test whether their repeated answers and stated confidence reveal their own errors. Separately, we give a small open model the answer key and test whether it can predict which questions larger models get wrong. We report three findings. (1) Familiar words that a source redefines cause the most errors. Models often repeat the same wrong answer, and neither repeated answers nor stated confidence reliably flag it. (2) The source definition fixes most errors on synthetic questions, but a definition drawn from the model's own prior knowledge does not. In real contracts, the source definition fixes only about half of the errors. (3) Given an answer key, a small model can rank questions by how likely larger models are to get them wrong. These results call for tests that check answers against the full source, including its local definitions.

- **Summary**: The paper examines the choice of text representation for semi-automated systematic literature review (SLR) systems based on active learning. The experimental study was performed on medical SLR case studies, with results reported for random forest classification, cluster initialization and uncertainty sampling. ### HeriKGQA: Grounded Knowledge-Graph Question Answering over Cultural Heritage Sensor Data
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15904v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.DB, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15904v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 19:09:13+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 19:09:13+00:00
- **Authors**: Lingxiao Kong, Vasileios Arampatzakis, Vasileios Sevetlidis, George Pavlidis, Zeyd Boukhers
- **Comment**: Submitted to NAACL 2027 industry track
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Cultural heritage sites are increasingly monitored by sensor networks, but querying their data requires database expertise. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a natural-language interface, yet over raw sensor records they lose track of which sensor, site, and time each value belongs to, and silently miscompute. We present HeriKGQA, a knowledge-graph question-answering method that confines the LLM to interpreting questions and phrasing answers, leaving computation to SPARQL and deterministic code. Questions are parsed into intents constrained by a heritage ontology aligning W3C SOSA/SSN with CIDOC-CRM, compiled into SPARQL, and answered from query results and temporal, spatial, and anomaly facts derived by a grounding layer, with error correction guarding each LLM step. On 500 questions over real data from five heritage sites, HeriKGQA reaches 99.0% accuracy, 18.2 points above the strongest of three baselines using the same LLM and receiving the same anomaly rules and answer conventions, with comparable answer quality while running almost four times faster. Without grounding, accuracy drops by 12.2 points and on anomaly questions from 99% to 66%. We report lessons from development and live deployment.



### Predicting the Needle in a Petabyte Scale Haystack: Open-Vocabulary Event Anticipation in Satellite Imagery

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16000v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 20:58:52+00:00

- **Comment**: 29 pages, 9 figures, to be published in NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Satellite constellations now image the Earth daily, capturing the lifecycle of events from deforestation to urban construction. Anticipating these events before completion enables timely intervention, yet existing systems cannot jointly identify where a change occurs, what it will become, and when it will finish across an open vocabulary. Building such a model requires diverse event data, but events are like needles in a haystack, making manual annotation at global scale infeasible. We address this problem by representing change as the vector difference between vision-language model embeddings at distinct time points. This arithmetic approach allows us to search for semantic transformations directly in the latent space, enabling (1) an automated data engine for global event discovery without manual annotation and (2) the largest global satellite event dataset to our knowledge, comprising 21,000+ locations across 64+ event types with ~2M images from PlanetScope and Sentinel-2 spanning six continents, to train (3) a novel global-scale event anticipation model. Our model detects change with a 93.5% max F1 score, outperforms baselines 3.6x on predicted event retrieval, and forecasts completion dates with a 3-day median error, even nine months before completion. ### Wayfinder: Adaptive Resource Routing from Agent Citations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16053v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16053v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 22:09:11+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 22:09:11+00:00
- **Authors**: Miguel Romero, George Karypis
- **Comment**: 20 pages, 3 figures, 10 tables. Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: To act effectively, agents must gather contextual information across resources such as chats, issue trackers, email and knowledge bases, to answer questions like "what was decided in the last project meeting?" or "where is the FAQ for our internal tooling?" Where this information lives in a user's environment is unknown to the agent, so it re-explores the same resources across consecutive interactions. Common routing approaches represent each resource as a tool and rely on high-level descriptions to generalize to unseen environments. Such descriptions often fail to separate similar or overlapping resources and cannot improve from the agent's subsequent interactions. We introduce Wayfinder, an adaptive router that uses the documents cited by the downstream agent as feedback about where relevant information resides. These citations update Wayfinder's internal resource representations, which subsequent interactions use to prioritize the resources likely to contain relevant information. We also introduce resource augmentation during offline reinforcement learning, which anonymizes and permutes resource identities to discourage training-time overfitting of which resource holds which topic; our ablations suggest it helps most when few training environments are available. Because no openly available professional environments pair realistic queries with resource-access patterns, we construct and release three controlled testbeds derived from existing datasets: NFCorpus, FEVER and Tech Startup. Compared with supervised, tool-retrieval and contextual bandit baselines under a shared closed-loop protocol, Wayfinder gives the best routing when resources contain semantically overlapping content that static descriptions cannot distinguish, while description-based tool retrieval suffices when resources are drawn from distinct corpora.



### REWEAVE: Online Learning for Workload-Adaptive HNSW Indexes
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16060v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DB**, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16060v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 22:18:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-11 22:18:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Minghao Li, Nick Koudas, Raghav Mittal
- **Comment**: 20 pages, 9 figures, 5 tables. Full version with appendix
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vector databases rely on Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) indexes for similarity search, but a graph built from data alone can be poorly matched to a skewed or changing query workload. We present REWEAVE (Reward-Guided Rewiring of Vector Graphs), an online index-maintenance method that learns edge utility from query feedback and periodically rewires the bottom layer within the original per-node degree cap. The method supports sparse exact-neighbor feedback combined with self-labels, and standard HNSW serving without learning overhead once adaptation is disabled.   Under explicit assumptions we prove value-estimation and graph-stabilization guarantees. Under stationary skew, REWEAVE improves held-out recall by up to 23.3 percentage points over HNSW. In comparisons with query-aware baselines on up to 100M vectors, it achieves up to 3.3x NGFix's query throughput at recall@100 of 0.80. On Amazon-ESCI with query drift, it increases stream recall@100 from 0.745 to 0.979 with 34% fewer search distance computations than frozen HNSW. These results illustrate the potential to improve retrieval quality and reduce search effort within an existing graph-degree budget, while incurring additional work during adaptation.
