### SIMQ: A Single Graph-based Index for Multi-Vector Query
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22079v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.DB
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22079v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 02:01:39+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 02:01:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Yifan Lu, Jiabao Jin, Xiaoyao Zhong, Peng Cheng, Xiangyu Wang, Haotian Li, Hua Chen, Junqing Chen, Wei Xi, Heng Tao Shen
- **Comment**: under ICDE 2027 review, 13 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-vector retrieval models such as ColBERT achieve state-of-the-art effectiveness by representing documents as sets of token-level vectors and scoring via MaxSim, but their high computational cost limits scalability to large corpora. Existing approximate methods face fundamental bottlenecks: centroid interaction matrix approaches require computing a full matrix at every query and rely on GPU-accelerated clustering, while data-oblivious compression methods yield unstable recall that requires repeated compensation. We propose SIMQ (Single graph Index for Multi-vector Query), a framework that bridges single-vector graph indices and multi-vector retrieval through data-aware sampling-based clustering and per-token graph navigation, simultaneously bypassing all aforementioned bottlenecks. SIMQ organizes all token vectors into fine-grained clusters via data-aware sampling, builds a single graph index over cluster centroids independently of PLAID's residual representation, and approximates the MaxSim score through per-token graph search without materializing any interaction matrix or relying on GPU acceleration. We provide theoretical analysis showing that, under a manifold assumption and successful cell retrieval, data-aware partitioning has a smaller expected approximation error than a data-oblivious partition. Building on this foundation, SIMQ+ further introduces count-based scoring and a dynamic candidate set to improve robustness across varying query conditions. Experiments on five benchmarks compare recall, throughput, and construction time against several baselines, primarily on CPU; large-scale PLAID k-means centroids are built on a separate GPU machine. SIMQ has been integrated into VSAG, an Ant Group's open-source vector search library.



### Cluster-Guided Decoding with Trust-Region Constraints for Personalized Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22163v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22163v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 03:53:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 03:53:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Gezheng Wen, Chao Shen, Raviteja Anantha, Tan Shen Cliff Chung, Layne C. Price
- **Comment**: 5 pages, 2 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Most recommenders score each item in the catalog separately, and do not explicitly model which semantic region of the catalog a recommendation belongs to. Models expressive enough to offer that control are often too slow to run over a full catalog for online inference. In this paper, we present Cluster-Guided Generative Reconstruction (CG2R), which learns a hierarchy of semantic clusters and uses a cluster-level prior to steer item ranking for personalized recommendations. Unlike existing systems that only use clusters to pre-select candidates, CG2R adds the cluster prior directly to the item scoring rule, so that the learned cluster structure shapes the ranking itself. Because the prior is assembled from cluster centroids rather than per-item statistics, it remains defined even for cold-start users when no history is available. Across the three benchmark datasets evaluated, CG2R outperforms classical, neural, and LLM-based baselines in terms of Recall and nDCG, with its performance gains widening as the catalog grows sparser. In addition, CG2R serves recommendations in milliseconds, suggesting potential as low-latency first-stage retrieval.



### Teaching Language Models to Narrow Boolean Patent Searches from Search Feedback
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22270v1

- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22270v1)

- **Authors**: Seiya Kawano, Koji Marusaki, Hirofumi Nonaka, Koichiro Yoshino
- **Comment**: 13 pages, 3 figures, 3 tables. Accepted at NLPIR 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Patent prior-art searchers issue Boolean queries line by line, broadening to find relevant documents and narrowing until candidates can be screened. We distill 38,397 Japan Patent Office search reports into executable expert strategies, re-execute them, and evaluate generated strategies by citation recall and the smallest set containing each citation. Prompted models write valid, high-recall strategies but leave citations in sets too large to screen. We adapt a generator offline from scored rollouts in two forms: strategy-level learning from a screening-aware strategy score, and line-level learning from citation-preserving narrowing credit, which upweights the query lines keeping a found citation in a smaller set. On shared rollouts, line-level credit outperforms strategy-level scores on screening measures; iterated, it cuts the median citation-containing set from 768 to under 50 and places more citations in sets under 100 documents than experts, at lower recall. Strategy-level preference learning reaches near-complete recall; the two forms are complementary.

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In traditional recommender systems (RSs), textual information typically serves as auxiliary signals for representation learning. In contrast, in LLM-based RSs, textual information becomes the primary medium for recommendation generation through semantic understanding and reasoning. This shift fundamentally changes the attack surface, making promotion-driven manipulations more feasible, effective, and harder to detect. However, effectively detecting such attacks remains largely unexplored. To fill this gap, we present the first systematic study on detecting promotion-driven attacks against LLM-based RSs, aiming to safeguard next-generation recommendation platforms. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that GuardAgent achieves state-of-the-art generalization performance, laying the foundation for robust and trustworthy recommendation in practical settings.



### NONSENSE: Non-Semantic Strings Expose Semanticity Gaps in Dense Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22353v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22353v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 08:04:40+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 08:04:40+00:00
- **Authors**: Ankit Gupta
- **Comment**: Accepted to EACL 2027 (Main Conference). 15 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dense retrieval is central to modern search, question answering, and retrieval-augmented generation, where high similarity is often treated as evidence of semantic relevance. We examine how far this assumption can be stressed. NONSENSE is a black-box sparse-edit probe that constructs visibly non-semantic strings while forbidding query-term copying, optimizing only the dense similarity between a query and the string. Across five BEIR-style datasets and three popular single-vector retrievers, these strings frequently outscore the retriever's own best judged query-relevant passage (QRP), and often rank first in the full corpus. The result indicates a semanticity gap in the evaluated dense retrievers: nearest-neighbor status can be separated from passage-like meaning. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating dense retrievers not only by whether they retrieve relevant documents, but also by how reliably their similarity scores reflect semantic evidence.



### RICoT: Recurrent Interleaved Chain-of-Thought for Page Stream Segmentation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22382v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22382v1)

- **Comment**: 18 pages, 3 figures, 19 tables. Dataset and code to be released
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) have renewed interest in Page Stream Segmentation (PSS), splitting a multi-page file into its constituent documents. Yet published LLM methods still frame the task as boundary classification over one or a few neighboring pages, and commercial document-splitting APIs do not disclose their methods. We reimagine PSS for LLMs as a sequential process over the whole stream and propose RICoT (Recurrent Interleaved Chain-of-Thought), a prompting framework that works through the pages as a reader would: within a sliding window it reasons about each page and commits its boundary decision before moving on, and across windows it carries forward a compact record of what it has read, keeping per-call cost bounded. We also introduce FinancePSS-205, a benchmark of 205 real-world, de-identified financial page streams, to be released publicly. On FinancePSS-205, RICoT matches or outperforms pairwise and windowed prompting with the same backbone for each of three open-weight LLMs; on a TABME test subset, RICoT with GLM-5 reaches document F1 0.847 and boundary F1 0.915, above published zero-shot results. A 2B-parameter student, trained on a larger teacher's decisions with and without the page-level reasoning, performs comparably in both modes and comes within 0.05 document F1 of the strongest prompted model on FinancePSS-205.



### Deal With It: On Better Schedules for Listwise Reranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22392v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22392v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 08:42:11+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 08:42:11+00:00
- **Authors**: Benjamin Clavié, Herumb Shandilya, Hanna Lichtenberg, Aamir Shakir, Makoto P. Kato
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Listwise LLM rerankers cannot reliably rank hundreds of documents in a single call, and rely on schedules to orchestrate their calls. Parallel schedules split candidates into groups and advance a fixed quota from each. When a group holds more of the top passages than its quota, some are cut, and expensive repair calls become required. We show that the way candidates are split matters where such a quota can bind, and that, in expected cuts, the best split there deals them round-robin by retriever rank, like cards. Although not conceptually new, dealing has remained unanalyzed. We prove that, under a simple retriever model, no split with equal quotas cuts fewer of the true top ten on average, and that contiguous chunks cut more as the retriever improves. DealRank, the simplest schedule our analysis allows, is on average within 0.1 nDCG@10 points of the best schedules over six LLMs, in two rounds. It is consistently 1.1 to 1.4 times faster than BlitzRank with concurrent first calls up to a thousand candidates. There, it needs three rounds and is eleven times faster than the sliding window. In existing schedules such as BlitzRank, dealing reduces repair calls from 59% of queries to 14%, improving latency with no significant change in quality. Random partitioning appears equivalent at a quota of four, as predicted, but ties results to the seed and trails by 1.3 nDCG@10 points at a quota of one over a dense retriever.

- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for generative recommendation, where recommended items are generated directly from users' interaction histories. To further improve recommendation quality, reinforcement learning (RL) has been applied to align these models with recommendation objectives. However, existing RL methods rely on scalar rewards to implicitly guide exploration. These rewards do not specify which aspects of a candidate conflict with the user's preferences or how the next candidate should differ. To address this gap, we propose VerRec, a verbalized feedback-guided RL framework for generative recommendation. Further analyses show that verbalized guidance encourages exploration of more diverse candidates and helps the policy discover more rewarding recommendations during training.



### From Relevance to Utility: Rubric Alignment in Yuanbao Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22503v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22503v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 10:00:05+00:00

- **Authors**: Yu Lu, Lixin Zou, Yangqi Chen, Hui Xu, Qiaolin Xia, Hao Wang, Qianwen Ma, Reed Li, Shuang Zeng, Haijin Liang, Haibo Shi
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures, 5 tables, accepted at KDD 2027
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Agentic search demands that a search engine move beyond per document relevance ranking to assemble document sets optimized for downstream LLM reasoning. Prior work addresses this either by improving individual pipeline stages in isolation or by applying RL based selection on top of relevance trained upstream modules, leaving the full stack unaligned to utility for reasoning. We propose rubric aligned search, a framework that unifies the traditional search pipeline under a single supervision surface. For each query, we derive a rubric from coarse to fine, specifying weighted positive criteria that the document set must satisfy and negative criteria that it must avoid. From this rubric we derive two training signals. First, a lightweight labeler distills rubric grounded judgments into per document utility labels, which supervise a contrastive dual encoder for retrieval and a multi task ranker trained with four rubric aligned scoring heads. Second, a rubric conditioned evaluator produces a set level reward that drives a dual branch RL selector, combining GRPO with best rollout distillation to resolve credit assignment in subset optimization. Deployed in Yuanbao Search, the pipeline improves end to end answer quality by 8.52% over the online production baseline.

- **Summary**: Personalized generative retrieval in search should jointly satisfy the user's immediate search intent and long-term preference. These results suggest that enriched query-related input evidence, together with effective integration mechanisms, is beneficial for building intent-aware end-to-end generative search systems.



### Better Rankers, Worse Retrievers: The Hidden Cost of RL Post-Training in Generative Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22838v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22838v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 14:19:22+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 14:19:22+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuheng Chen, Fan Zhang, Yixuan Xu, Chao Yang, Yang Xiang, Can Yang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative recommenders decode the semantic ID of a user's next item with a language model, and post-training them with reinforcement learning (RL) after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has become standard practice. The gains are mostly reported on the first ten results, yet in deployment such models hand hundreds of candidates to a downstream ranker or reward model. We show that RL post-training has a hidden cost: it improves accuracy at the top of the list (hit@K for small K) but, on all five datasets we study, lowers the recall of the candidate list (hit@K for K in the hundreds). The model becomes a better ranker and a worse retriever. Controlled experiments trace the loss to the negative advantages that RL assigns to the candidates it samples, the rollout. The root cause is that only one next item is logged per user, so RL treats every other candidate as wrong. Yet these candidates are the model's most plausible alternatives, and often the next items of other users with similar histories. Penalising them pushes down exactly what similar users choose next, and these items drop out of those users' candidate lists. This diagnosis points directly to a remedy: out-of-rollout anchoring, a single additional loss term that preserves the SFT model's ordering of the items outside the rollout. It keeps the gain at the top and restores recall to the SFT level on all five datasets, and it is effective under other RL configurations as well. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of this recall loss: we measure it, identify its cause, and show that, once the cause is known, a simple remedy suffices.

### When the tail is earned by its companions: measuring label dependence in long-tailed multi-label classification
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22859v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CL, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22859v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-17 14:38:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-17 14:38:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Christoffer Löffler
- **Comment**: 27 pages, 3 figures, 13 tables. Submitted to Information Sciences
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A rare label seldom occurs alone, and a classifier can recover it from the frequent labels it co-occurs with, its companions, without having learned the label itself. Existing diagnostics of this need a trained model: none says before training whether a corpus admits the failure, or what the standard aggregate hides. Macro-F1, like any measure built from per-label confusion counts, is unchanged by how a label's recall divides between occurrences with and without a companion. We introduce a within-label estimand of that division, a reading rule under which a fall in dependence counts only if companion-free recall rises, and a statistic of training labels that bounds, before training, how far reported recall can exceed companion-free recall. Across 23 corpora, 7 training objectives and five families of label-correlation mechanisms, companion-free recall falls short on all six corpora where the estimand is defined. Up to 18.4% of the tail true positives macro-F1 credits would vanish at the companion-free rate, within the training-label bound on every corpus. No long-tail objective reduces the dependence, but architecture moves it. Models whose tail score is computed from other labels' predictions are strongly dependent.
