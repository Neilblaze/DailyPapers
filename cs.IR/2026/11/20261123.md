### Look Before You Leap: Re-Architecting Disk-Resident Vector Search with TriVS
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29948v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DB**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29948v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-23 03:11:10+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-23 03:11:10+00:00
- **Authors**: Yizou Chen, Baotong Lu, Peiqi Yin, Zhisheng Hu, Qi Chen, Ming-Chang Yang
- **Comment**: 16 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Disk-resident approximate nearest-neighbor search (ANNS) scales vector search beyond DRAM, yet state-of-the-art systems remain I/O-bound, even with fast SSDs and sophisticated I/O execution. To understand this bottleneck, we conduct an I/O-centric analysis that breaks each query down into an approach phase that locates a query-near region and a converge phase that resolves the final top-k. This analysis reveals complementary I/O tradeoffs: cluster-based search circumvents approach-phase I/O through in-memory routing but reads coarse-grained, full clusters during convergence, whereas graph-based search employs fine-grained, vector-level I/O throughout, enabling efficient convergence at the cost of inevitable I/O during the approach phase.   To combine the complementary I/O benefits of the two granularities, we present TriVS, a new paradigm that re-architects disk-resident vector search as a trilogy: approach coarsely, distill progressively, and converge exactly. Its key innovations come in two parts. First, it decouples the approach and converge phases from a single search granularity, employing cluster-based search to avoid approach-phase I/O and leveraging vector-level I/O for efficient convergence. Second, to bridge the two granularities, it introduces a new in-memory distill phase between the original two, progressively reducing routed clusters to a vector-level shortlist using compact quantized vectors before disk access. Moreover, coarser clustering shrinks the centroid-routing index, allowing TriVS to reallocate existing memory to distillation rather than expand its memory budget. Our evaluation on five datasets spanning diverse vector types and scales shows that, at Recall@10=0.9, TriVS achieves on average 3.42X PipeANN's throughput, 51.2% lower latency, and 63.7% less disk I/O, while using less memory and storage than PipeANN.



### Structure-Exploiting Hierarchical Retrieval for Long Narrative Documents: A Precision-Oriented Alternative to Learned Abstraction Trees
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29966v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29966v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-23 03:30:50+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-23 03:30:50+00:00
- **Authors**: Rohit Singh, Nathaniel Weems
- **Comment**: 10 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented generation over book-length documents must locate a small, coherent set of passages inside tens of thousands of tokens. Flat dense retrieval ignores document structure; RAPTOR instead builds a hierarchy by recursively clustering and summarizing with a language model. We study a complementary design that exploits the native structure of a document: a two-level retriever that (i) selects chapters with a hybrid BM25+dense match over chapter recaps, (ii) runs a chapter-filtered dense search, and (iii) expands top passages into contiguous, section-aware spans under a token budget, plus content-hash-gated delta ingestion to keep the index cheap to update. We evaluate on five public-domain books (113 chapter-localization queries) and on QuALITY and NovelQA (200K+ token novels). In-house, we find a seed-stable precision/ranking tradeoff: with section expansion off, hierarchical retrieval returns a higher fraction of chunks from the correct chapter (+3.3pp, Wilcoxon p=0.014); with expansion on, ranking improves (MRR, p=0.040). Accuracy differences are not significant. On QuALITY, hierarchical retrieval beats flat top-k (81.0% vs 70.4%, p=0.0019) and matches the full article (p=0.74). On NovelQA, hierarchical and flat retrieval are statistically tied (55.7% vs 56.8%, p=0.24); hierarchy helps aggregation questions while flat wins on localized facts. Against a faithful RAPTOR baseline we find a complementary tradeoff, not superiority. Delta ingestion reuses 94 to 99.7% of embeddings on incremental edits. We make no claim of semantic chapter-boundary inference and scope claims to English narrative prose.

### Beyond Knowledge Transfer: How User Overlap Shapes Diversity in Cross-Domain Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30156v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30156v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-23 07:03:46+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-23 07:03:46+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuhan Zhao, Weixin Chen, Shu Chen, Li Chen
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A common paradigm in cross-domain recommendation (CDR) transfers knowledge through users shared by source and target domains. Although this overlap bridge is effective in improving accuracy, its effect on diversity remains unclear. We identify two bottlenecks: the bridge can be too narrow when overlapping users cover limited target-domain tastes, and too loud when shared signals dominate the sparse personal evidence of non-overlapping users. To address both, we propose DivCDR, a model-agnostic Push--Pull framework. Pull constructs confidence-gated virtual positives for overlapping users and optimizes their exposure-entropy gain, broadening the preferences carried by the bridge. Experiments on three datasets and five representative CDR backbones show that DivCDR improves recommendation diversity while preserving or improving accuracy in most settings.

### Fetch, Read, Reuse: How AI Search Agents Bend the Web's Rules
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30546v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.NI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30546v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-23 11:55:44+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-23 11:55:44+00:00
- **Authors**: Yixi Chen, Zafar A. Qazi, Marco Canini, Suhaib A. Fahmy
- **Comment**: 29 pages, 11 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: AI search agents form a new and fast-growing class of web traffic. Like a browser, they fetch pages on demand for one user; like a transcoding proxy, they rewrite what they fetch; and like a shared cache, they answer other users' questions from the stored copy. The rules publishers rely on, such as robots.txt, Retry-After, and Cache-Control, were written for more familiar clients, so the agent's vendor decides which of them to follow. We present a black-box measurement study of how these agents fetch, read, and reuse pages, covering the search APIs of OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Google and the consumer apps of ten services. We find that OpenAI and xAI do not honor robots.txt, and no service acts on Retry-After. Services silently read less than the origin served: OpenAI drops some formats, no service reads JavaScript-only content, and xAI reads only the first 0.5 MB of a page. OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI share fetched pages across users and largely ignore Cache-Control, so answers can reflect outdated pages for 30 minutes to a day or more. Publishers therefore lack effective control: directives are advisory and followed selectively, describe the served page rather than the rewritten copy, and bound how long a copy is reused but not how many answers it serves. We release our code and data.

### CO-GRAFT: Attributing Reinforcement Learning Gains in Search Agents via Role-Level Policy Grafting
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31185v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31185v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-23 18:38:53+00:00

- **Authors**: Ramraj Chandradevan, Sayontan Ghosh, Vinoth Selvendran
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reinforcement learning (RL) improves large language model search agents by up to 32 exact-match (EM) points over their pre-RL initializations, yet overall accuracy gains do not identify which underlying functional capabilities improve or interact during training. We introduce CO-GRAFT, a reward-agnostic interventional attribution framework in which an RL-trained agent and its pre-RL initialization jointly generate a single trajectory under closed-loop execution with live retrieval. Each policy controls a mutually exclusive subset of four functional roles: intermediate reasoning, search-or-answer decision, search query generation, and final answer extraction. Evaluating all 2^4=16 role assignments without modifying weights or retraining defines a cooperative game whose Shapley values decompose the total RL gain across roles, while M"obius interaction terms distinguish standalone role improvements from multi-role coupling. Applying CO-GRAFT to Search-R1 agents across Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2 models (spanning two sizes, base and instruction-tuned initializations, GRPO and PPO optimizers, and NQ and HotpotQA datasets), we show that query generation never exceeds 15% of the total gain; instead, gains are governed by pre-RL answer validity, search retention, model scale, and fixed task-level search budgets, shifting credit from reasoning toward answer extraction in larger or well-formatted answer response checkpoints while smaller base models rely on reasoning--decision coupling.
