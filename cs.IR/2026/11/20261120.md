### ERA: Embed, Route, Adapt for Cold-Start Cross-Model Latent Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26919v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26919v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 00:06:04+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 00:06:04+00:00
- **Authors**: Venkata Chivukula, Zifeng Wang, Jinsung Yoon
- **Comment**: 20 pages, 2 figures, 11 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Production retrieval systems are tied to the embedding model that indexed their corpus. Moving queries to a different encoder normally requires re-embedding the whole corpus, and at cold start there are no query logs while every document embedded by the new encoder incurs an API or compute cost. We present ERA (Embed, Route, Adapt), a system for cold-start cross-model latent retrieval. ERA spends a small anchor budget on maximally informative documents, selected from the existing target index via Cosine Farthest Point Sampling, trains a lightweight adapter with a Manifold Alignment Loss that couples directional alignment with orthogonal regularization to prevent representation collapse, and pairs the adapter with a deterministic router that escalates the rare, hard queries that define perceived retrieval quality. Across five BEIR datasets, ERA matches or outperforms point-wise L2 and embedding-conversion baselines, with relative gains of up to 35% nDCG@10 on Arguana, and on clustered corpora its anchor selection also improves prior methods under a fixed anchor budget. With 1.2 ms of adapter and router overhead on top of a ~20 ms local encoder, ERA at a 20% nominal escalation budget reduces end-to-end query latency from roughly 160 ms to 38-86 ms while concentrating escalations on the queries where the projection fails most.



### Leader Finding: A New Variation of Expert Finding in Community Question Answering
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26921v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.SI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26921v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 00:15:11+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 00:15:11+00:00
- **Authors**: Sepideh Entezari Maleki, Mahmood Neshati
- **Comment**: Extended version of the ICTIR '21 short paper (doi:10.1145/3471158.3472230). 30 pages, 9 figures, 3 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Expert finding ranks people by their knowledge of a given topic. Many job positions need more than topical expertise, and a team leader is a clear case. A suitable leader knows the technical areas of the project without being overqualified, has strong managerial knowledge, and communicates well with the team. We study the leader finding problem, where the query is a project and a team and the output is a ranked list of candidate leaders. We model the problem in a probabilistic framework that combines technical fit, managerial knowledge, and communication with the team, and we propose one evaluation metric for each of these qualities. On two large collections built from Stack Overflow, the best configuration of our model more than doubles the NDCG@10 of the strongest baseline. These results show that a ranking of leaders must balance several qualities that topical expertise alone does not capture.



### Explicit Style Extraction for Personalized Long-Form Text Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27211v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27211v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 06:52:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 06:52:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Jenitha Reddy Patel, Sameeksha Kini, Amulya Rayasam, Anisa Dye, Abhinav Sharma, Sai Karthik Navuluru, Bo Ni, Li Li, Junda Wu, Tong Yu, Nedim Lipka, Xiaolei Huang, Tyler Derr, Yue Zhao, Zhengzhong Tu, Yu Wang, Hongjie Chen, Thien Huu Nguyen, Nesreen K. Ahmed, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan A. Rossi
- **Comment**: 21 pages, 8 figures, 11 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Personalized long-form generation asks a language model to write new text the way a specific user would. The standard recipe, retrieval-augmented generation, places a few of the user's past texts in the prompt but leaves the model to decide what to take from them. We introduce StylePrism, a taxonomy of 28 stylistic aspects in seven categories, and SP-Refine, an inference-time method that makes style extraction an explicit step: the model first fills StylePrism with the user's patterns, quoted evidence, and habits to avoid, then drafts from the user's samples and revises the draft twice against this profile, guided by its own style scores. On the three long-form tasks of LongLaMP with three LLMs, SP-Refine outperforms LongLaMP's retrieval-augmented baseline in 25 of 27 ROUGE and METEOR comparisons, by 10.2% on average and by up to 35.8%. Annotating users' own texts with the extracted profiles shows that StylePrism captures concrete, checkable habits, from the order of rhetorical moves to a reviewer's sign-off. Ablations show that the profile helps as revision guidance, not as a replacement for the samples: writing from the profile alone is worse in all 27 comparisons, while a single implicit-inference call is a cheaper alternative that comes close. Oracle variants that let the reviser see the reference, together with reference-free controls, show that larger refinement gains require information unavailable at inference time.



### Which Dwarf Is This? Doppeldwarf-306 and Near-Identical Instance Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27490v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27490v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 11:36:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 11:36:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Turhan Can Kargin
- **Comment**: 23 pages, 6 figures in the main text; includes appendix. Project page: https://turhancan97.github.io/doppeldwarf-306/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Most established instance-retrieval benchmarks emphasise landmarks; small sculptures from visually similar series offer a complementary challenge. We introduce Doppeldwarf-306: 1,691 openly licensed photographs of 306 bronze dwarf statues in Wrocław, Poland, many made in themed series that share one style, with the photographer of each image recorded. With no training, nearest-neighbour search over frozen DINOv2 features names the right statue first for 93.1% of leave-one-out queries, and the best of thirteen backbones from seven pretraining paradigms reaches 94.7%. Accuracy falls slowly as candidates are added: for DINOv2, by about 0.8 points each time their number doubles. A natural design for a phone guide compares a photograph only with statues near the user. In simulation this helps, but less than an equal number of randomly chosen statues does, so random subsampling overstates its value; a permutation test ties the penalty to the real layout (p = 0.005). Used as a weight instead of a filter, location raises DINOv2 from 93.4% to 98.7% for a simulated user 25 m from the statue. With the query's photographer withheld, learned local-feature matching raises DINOv2 accuracy from 81.8% to 85.5% and also improves DINOv3. Comparing the two best candidates cuts DINOv2's false acceptance of unknown statues from 38.3% to 19.6% at 90% known acceptance.



### Deep Cross-Division Network for Product-and-Ratio Feature Interactions in Industrial Ranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27514v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27514v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 12:06:35+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 12:06:35+00:00
- **Authors**: Mikhail Trapeznikov, Sofia Gulevskaia, Maksim Utushkin, Aleksandr Poslavsky, Alexander D'yakonov
- **Comment**: Accepted at WSDM 2027. 14 pages, 5 figures, 7 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Learning effective feature interactions is essential for industrial recommender systems. DCN and DCNv2 efficiently automate polynomial feature crossing. However, their cross layers construct interactions using only non-negative integer powers of the input features, with the maximum degree controlled by network depth. Consequently, they cannot directly represent the reciprocal terms required for ratios, rates, and normalized counts unless such features are explicitly provided as inputs. We introduce DCDN, a Deep Cross-Division Network that augments a DCNv2-style cross layer with a division branch. DCDN jointly learns product, ratio, and mixed product-ratio interactions. We characterize the representable interactions as multivariate Laurent polynomials and show that a $K$-layer DCDN generates Laurent monomials of $\ell_1$-order up to $K+1$, with signed degree between $-K$ and $K+1$. We evaluate DCDN against a production-optimized neural-ranking baseline built around DCNv2 and supplied with manually engineered ratio features. Replacing the DCNv2 block with DCDN allowed us to eliminate the entire class of manually engineered ratio features from the model input, reducing the number of input features by $40.1\%$. DCDN is deployed in the production ranking stack of a major short-video service. In a live A/B test on this service, DCDN increased likes by $3.25\%$, shares by $16.26\%$, total view time (TVT) by $0.33\%$, and clicks to open fullscreen by $0.38\%$; all changes were statistically significant. The number of shown videos decreased slightly ($-0.24\%$). These results demonstrate that DCDN can improve user engagement while eliminating reliance on manually engineered ratio features. Our code is publicly released at https://github.com/rw404/DCDN.

### Decision Columns as an Index: Interpretable Embeddings for Explainable Entity Matching and Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27910v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.DB
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27910v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 17:48:55+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 17:48:55+00:00
- **Authors**: Aadharsh Kannan, Robert Bruckner, Philip A. Bernstein
- **Comment**: 25 pages, 7 figures, including appendices. Evaluations on WDC Products and STaRK-Amazon
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Business data systems must determine whether records refer to the same entity and retrieve records satisfying requests whose decisive properties are implicit. As agents act on these records, both tasks require auditable reasons. Dense embeddings perform well but offer opaque coordinates, cannot be filtered by meaning, and require corpus re-encoding to add new properties. We propose interpretable embeddings built from decision columns: each column stores a decision model's calibrated answer to a natural-language question, with outcomes yes, no, or not stated. A record's embedding is its answers across an ordered set of questions. Similarity decomposes exactly into per-question contributions, and new questions add coordinates without altering existing ones.   On public product benchmarks, question-space retrieval matches dense candidate generation statistically in a retrieve-then-verify entity matcher, while providing exact explanations for accepted matches. Questions derived from retrieval failures triple mean reciprocal rank without re-encoding prior answers. For search, inferring the attributes of a suitable result outperforms encoding only the literal request, and combining question-space and dense scores improves retrieval, though not at our prespecified confidence threshold. A single small neural network predicts the full interpretable vector from an existing dense embedding, enabling corpus backfilling from a few thousand labeled rows at roughly 1/200 the cost. We position interpretable embeddings as a complementary, readable retrieval channel for explaining matches, filtering on attributes, and extending indexes as failures expose missing facts.



### DiTraR: Diverse Scorers and Transcript-Guided Re-ranking for Video Corpus Moment Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27931v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27931v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 18:08:03+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 18:08:03+00:00
- **Authors**: Mohammad Alijanpour Shalmani, Alale Rezvani Boroujeni, Ali Amini
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Video corpus moment retrieval asks a system to find, in a large collection of untrimmed videos with subtitles, the video and the time interval that a natural-language query describes. We find that most top-ranked errors of a strong corpus-level scorer come from choosing the wrong video, not from imprecise boundaries, and that re-weighting the evidence the scorer already uses does not fix them. We present DiTraR, which addresses video selection from two complementary directions. A witness-interval scorer decomposes the query into probes and scores every candidate interval of every corpus video exactly; a multi-scorer energy ensemble averages the energies of independently trained scorers before one exhaustive search; and a transcript verifier, a frozen language model that reads the time-stamped dialogue of the highest-ranked videos, re-orders the moments without changing their boundaries. With all settings frozen on development data and a single evaluation, DiTraR reaches 24.36 and 13.55 R@1 at IoU 0.5 and 0.7 on the TVR validation split. The ensemble improves queries about visual content, the verifier improves queries about dialogue, and their gains largely add up.

- **Summary**: Memory benchmarks built on question answering tell an agent which fact to recall. Workplace requests often leave that need implicit: to post an update in the team's channel, an agent must work out which channel from earlier interactions and use it in a tool call. We evaluate Memanto, a memory agent that ranks memories with an information-theoretic relevance score, on DolphinBench, which tests memory through 600 simulated workplace tasks, each validated as requiring memory. Grading uses the benchmark's own judge, but the agent model differs, so these results compare complete systems. The memory system affects both whether an agent completes such tasks and what completion costs.

### GERF: A Token-Efficient Multimodal Retrieval Framework for Schema-Guided Scientific Information Extraction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28295v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28295v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 20:23:14+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 20:23:14+00:00
- **Authors**: Yiyu Qian, Xing Wu
- **Comment**: 9 pages, 3 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Scientific information extraction (IE) is a fundamental component of AI for Science, transforming experimental knowledge embedded in scientific literature into structured and traceable data for evidence synthesis, scientific database construction, and data-driven discovery. However, critical experimental details are often distributed across text, tables, figures, captions, and surrounding discussion, making them difficult to recover using conventional text-only extraction. Existing LLM-based and multimodal RAG pipelines typically rely on full-document context or coarse page-level retrieval, introducing substantial irrelevant evidence and high textual and visual token costs. We propose GERF, a Graph-based Evidence Retrieval Framework for token-efficient, schema-guided multimodal scientific IE. GERF represents each article as a heterogeneous evidence graph that connects schema fields, text chunks, figures, and figure-derived content through semantic and structural relations. Using schema fields as retrieval anchors, GERF combines direct semantic retrieval, Random Walk with Restart, and null-aware evidence expansion to construct compact, field-specific multimodal evidence packages. Visual interpretation is applied only to graph-selected figures, avoiding indiscriminate processing of the entire document. We evaluate GERF from two complementary perspectives: evidence retrieval quality and pipeline-level input-token consumption. On the expert-annotated SOFC-Exp corpus, GERF retrieves gold-supported evidence for 94.55% of the evaluated field instances, providing broad field-level evidence coverage for downstream extraction. In the token comparison, GERF reduces average input-token usage by 73.40% relative to full-context processing and uses fewer tokens than the evaluated page-level pipelines.



### Beyond Relevance: An Empirical Study of Multimodal LLMs for Diversity-Aware Recommendation Reranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28379v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28379v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-20 22:37:29+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-20 22:37:29+00:00
- **Authors**: Chengkai Liu, Yan Fu, Huiyuan Chen, Sheikh Sarwar, Qilin Qi
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) have emerged as promising rerankers for recommendation, yet most existing approaches rely on textual item descriptions and primarily focus on relevance. In visually oriented domains such as video streaming, item images provide complementary signals that may also inform recommendation diversity. This raises two questions: when does visual information improve recommendation quality, and can multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) enhance relevance and diversity simultaneously? We investigate these questions through a training-free, diversity-aware multimodal reranking framework that jointly incorporates item images, textual metadata, and user interaction histories. We conduct an empirical study across three public Amazon Review datasets and an industrial Amazon Prime Video dataset, using both open-source and proprietary MLLMs. Our results reveal substantial domain-dependent effects: adding visual information yields up to 121.9% relative improvement in NDCG@5 on Prime Video, while producing limited or inconsistent gains on product recommendation datasets. Furthermore, MLLM-based reranking improves both relevance and category-diversity-sensitive ranking quality over a sequential recommendation baseline, achieving up to 71.0% improvement in NDCG@5 and 14.1% in $α$-NDCG@5 on Prime Video. These findings highlight the conditional value of visual information and the potential of training-free MLLMs for relevance- and diversity-aware recommendation reranking.
