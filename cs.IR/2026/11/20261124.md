### RELAY: Learning Compatible Representations One Encoder at a Time
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31882v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31882v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 03:42:51+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 03:42:51+00:00
- **Authors**: Yu Wang, Chuan Qin, Qiang Zhang
- **Comment**: 26 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Old and new document embeddings coexist during incremental gallery re-encoding. RELAY trains a new query encoder with a learned projection against the original gallery, then freezes both to train the new document encoder. The same query vector scores both document versions throughout a single re-encoding pass. After backfill, optional query refinement drops the projection and reuses the stored document vectors. Across three text-to-image retrieval upgrades on COCO and Flickr30K, RELAY improves Recall@1 on untouched galleries by 8.66-10.23 percentage points over the original systems. For direct re-encoding, RELAY ranks first in both Qwen-to-Qwen upgrades and second in CLIP-to-Qwen by area under the Recall@1 curve, under both native scoring and matched calibration. With embeddings held fixed, calibrating scores separately for each document version can reduce drops in Recall@1 during backfill and change which method achieves the highest backfill AUC. After query refinement, final Recall@1 is within 0.97 percentage points of conventional joint training in all three upgrades.



### MELODI: Similarity-Identity Decoupling for Lightweight Ranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31940v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31940v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 04:44:33+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 04:44:33+00:00
- **Authors**: Quoc Phong Nguyen, Vuong Le, Volodymyr Vaskovych, Julien Monteil
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: For latency-constrained candidate ranking, we propose a similarity-identity decoupling framework that separates target-history compatibility from target-only information. We study two orders for computing pooled similarity, similarity-then-pool and pool-then-similarity, and identify linearity of the similarity operator in its history argument, for each fixed target, as sufficient for their equivalence, justifying the cheaper pool-then-similarity form. We instantiate the framework as multi-head efficient lightweight operator for decoupling identity and similarity (MELODI), whose similarity branch combines target-conditioned multi-head history pooling with a single residual-free cross layer that is linear in its pooled-history input and whose identity branch is problem-dependent. On public benchmark datasets, MELODI matches or surpasses the evaluated target-aware and target-agnostic baselines. On two large-scale industrial datasets, its area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC), normalised discounted cumulative gain (NDCG), and recall values are equal to or higher than those of the evaluated target-aware baselines. Processor measurements show lower latency than the evaluated candidate-conditioned baselines at large candidate counts, further reduced by request-local history reuse.



### User-Adaptive Fusion of Multiple Foundation Models for Recommender Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31951v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31951v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 04:57:30+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 04:57:30+00:00
- **Authors**: Sunwoo Kim, Hyunjin Hwang, Geon Lee, Kyungho Kim, Kijung Shin
- **Comment**: Published in the WSDM 2027 (Main Track)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Can recommender systems benefit from combining a collection of foundation models within and across modalities? While different foundation models may capture complementary aspects of item metadata, we find that naively combining their representations yields only marginal gains over using a single foundation model. We make a key observation: the users who benefit vary across foundation models. Motivated by this observation, we introduce UF2O, a user-adaptive approach for fusing multiple foundation models for recommendation. In a nutshell, UF2O models the contribution of each foundation model as user-specific by maintaining separate recommendation views and adaptively combining their scores for each user. By performing fusion at the score level, UF2O can also fuse heterogeneous foundation models without directly aligning their representation spaces. Extensive experiments that jointly combine four foundation models on four datasets show that UF2O outperforms existing fusion approaches in both static and sequential recommendation settings, with improvements of up to 7% over the strongest fusion baseline. The code, datasets, and supplementary materials for our work are available at https://github.com/kswoo97/UFFO.



### LARF: Learning-aligned Reasoning Framework for Search Relevance
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32125v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32125v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 07:41:25+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 07:41:25+00:00
- **Authors**: Tian Tang, Guo Chen, Reed Li, Hao Wang, Yu Lu, Haijin Liang, Haibo Shi
- **Comment**: Accepted by WSDM 2027
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Query-document relevance estimation is a core component of AI-native search systems, where a model must assess whether retrieved documents satisfy users' information needs under complex, multi-dimensional criteria. However, directly applying large language models (LLMs) to this task remains challenging because conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) provides limited supervision for domain-specific judgment rationales, while standard trajectory-level reinforcement learning (RL) objectives do not explicitly enforce margin separation among adjacent ordinal relevance labels. Moreover, although such reasoning can be useful during training, chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is impractical for latency-sensitive online ranking. These challenges make it difficult to resolve ambiguous decision boundaries. We propose a three-stage framework LARF (Learning-Aligned Reasoning Framework for Search Relevance): it first performs cold-start SFT on strategically selected samples with multi-agent guided CoT rationales, then applies discriminative GRPO with attribute-wise, list-wise, and rule-consistency rewards together with a margin loss on the terminal relevance token, and finally distills the teacher into a dual-head student whose classification heads are retained for non-autoregressive inference. On a human-annotated web search relevance benchmark with approximately 300K training and 16K test instances, LARF-8B improves six-class classification accuracy by 5.78 percentage points and NDCG by 5.05 points, while the classification-only serving path reduces inference latency by 82.9\%. These results indicate that aligning structured reasoning, terminal decision optimization, and classification-head distillation can provide an effective accuracy--efficiency trade-off for industrial search relevance modeling.



### OmniEVIE: Evidence-Vector-Informed Embedding for Multi-modal Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32203v1

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32203v1)

- **Authors**: Zifei Wang, Wei Wen, Qiang Ji, Qian-Wen Zhang, Ruizhi Qiao, He Liu, Xing Sun, Qiang Lin
- **Comment**: 28 pages, 10 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal retrieval must match dense pages, fine-grained entities, ordered video, and agent tools and memory. Single-vector embeddings lose local evidence, cross-encoders reread every recalled item online, and uncompressed late interaction is too large to serve. We present OmniEVIE, a 4.5B multi-vector embedding model covering text, images, visual documents, video, and tools with one compressed index and a three-stage retriever. Each record stores at most 128 int8 vectors of dimension 64 (9,472 bytes, or 8.82 GiB per million records), providing a mean vector, 1-bit signs, and int8 multi-vectors for coarse, middle, and final search while localizing each hit to a page region or video interval. Training, merging, indexing, and retrieval are co-designed: all tasks share one retrieval format, a three-grade evidence check removes false negatives, dynamic sampling follows the baseline-teacher gap, candidates are compressed with ST-HAC during training to match the index, stage-wise and specialist models are merged by parameter block, and a saliency gate with a noise floor refines query scoring. Trained on public datasets, OmniEVIE scores 60.68 (full d=2048 sequence) and 60.06 (K128 x d64 int8 index) on MMEB-V3 (190 tasks), 77.96 on MMEB-V2 (78 tasks), and 62.96 on ViDoRe V3 (48 tasks). On 1M and 5M records, a single-thread query takes 54.2 ms and 120.2 ms (11.1x and 25.3x faster than exact 4096-d single-vector search; 236x and 532x faster than full int8 MaxSim). Video is encoded directly from frames without a parsing model: on QVHighlights (1,083 queries), the peak falls inside the labeled segment for 78.0% of queries and the returned span (18% of video length) intersects it for 94.3% vs. 44.9% random. Weights and code: https://huggingface.co/tencent/OmniEVIE-4B and https://github.com/Tencent/OmniEVIE.

### See the Answer, Learn to Embed: Learning Universal Multimodal Embeddings from Hindsight
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32439v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32439v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 11:39:03+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 11:39:03+00:00
- **Authors**: Yoonki Cho, Youngju Na, Taegeun Yang, Jinhwan Seo, Sung-Eui Yoon
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) models map heterogeneous multimodal inputs into a shared embedding space, where pairwise similarity supports diverse downstream tasks. Recent reasoning-driven UME approaches use intermediate reasoning to generate additional context for embedding, but often incur extra computation at inference. In this paper, we explore the matched candidate (i.e., the paired target) as an alternative source of context, asking whether a model can "see the answer" during training and "learn to embed" without it at inference. However, direct conditioning on this privileged context can introduce candidate-specific information unavailable from the query alone or cause the candidate-conditioned query representation to collapse toward the candidate embedding. To address this, we introduce Hindsight-Enhanced Multimodal Embeddings (HEME), which learns query-grounded hindsight representations from such candidate context. HEME first trains a hindsight teacher with a hindsight attention mask, mismatched-candidate conditioning, and query-token alignment to limit excessive reliance on the candidate. It then freezes the teacher and distills its hindsight representation and similarity distribution into a standalone student embedder. At inference, the student independently encodes queries and candidates without candidate conditioning or the hindsight teacher. Experiments on MMEB-V2 and the agent and text tasks of MMEB-V3 demonstrate the effectiveness of HEME and show that it enables more efficient inference than explicit reasoning-driven methods.



### LazUnirec: Unifying Retrieval and Ranking in Multi-scenario Industrial Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32590v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32590v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 13:26:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 13:26:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Xiaodong Li, Junfeng Zhang, Shichao Nie, Ziyue Guo, Yiming Liu, Xinxin Xu, Xurong Li, Jinyuan Dang, Zhanming Ou, Jianan Yan, Tao Luo, Xiaoyi Zeng
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Modern industrial recommendation systems typically divide retrieval and ranking into two separate cascaded stages, leading to inconsistent optimization objectives, redundant encoding of user sequences, and a waste of computational resources. Previous efforts have investigated using Transformers to model both retrieval and ranking simultaneously. However, these studies still face two major limitations: (1) Since retrieval and ranking share a single Transformer backbone, the gradient conflict arises when the gradient directions of the two tasks are inconsistent. (2) When gradient conflict occurs, the model cannot determine a reliable direction for gradient updates. To this end, we propose a Unified Retrieval and Ranking approach for Multi-scenario Industrial Recommendation, termed as LazUnirec. Specifically, we construct task-shared and task-private parameters on the Transformer backbone to mitigate the gradient conflict. Wherein, for the attention components, we propose LoRA-modulated attention, enabling retrieval and ranking to share attention parameters while preserving task-private parameters. For the feed-forward networks (FFN), we present task-adaptive sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) to mitigate gradient conflict by constructing task-shared and task-private experts. Moreover, we propose an uncertainty estimation that evaluates the gradient reliability of the retrieval and ranking, thereby guiding the model toward a more reliable gradient update direction when gradient conflict occurs. In addition, knowledge distillation from ranking to retrieval further enables cross-task knowledge transfer. Extensive offline experiments and online A/B testing demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of LazUnirec. LazUnirec has been deployed in online multi-scenario industrial recommendation system and currently delivers significant gains for the platform.

### Speculative Decoding for Semantic-ID-Based Generative Recommendations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32855v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32855v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 15:57:21+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 15:57:21+00:00
- **Authors**: Harshal A. Chaudhari
- **Comment**: 33 pages, 19 figures (23 pages main text, appendices A-E follow). Preprint
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative recommender systems with Semantic IDs (SIDs) represent each item as a short sequence of $L$ discrete tokens and use a sequence model to predict the next item's tokens from a user's history. Industry-scale deployments typically use these models to retrieve multiple candidates per request with constrained beam search. However, this incurs a fixed cost of $L$ sequential model calls per request, and accuracy degrades as the SID generator becomes less sharp, without a guarantee on how close the output is to the exact top-$K$. Production systems therefore cannot detect degraded retrieval for individual requests.   We propose SIDECAR, which uses approximate draft candidates and a tree-attention pass to score the entire draft in a single model call, memory permitting. This pass bounds the probability of every unexplored SID prefix and certifies the exact top-$K$ if the draft contains them. Otherwise, a branch-and-bound fallback expands only the parts of the trie that can still change the output, reaching certification within at most $L$ calls for any draft. Moreover, under a budget of $M$ tokens per call and $Y$ calls, which for $Y = L$ matches the resources of a beam search of width $M$, SIDECAR announces a factor $1 + δ$ within which its list will be exact at every rank. Depending on model sharpness, SIDECAR returns the exact top-$K$ using a fraction of beam search's calls or memory. Even when the budget is too small to recover the exact top-$K$, SIDECAR, unlike beam search, reports the announced factor, allowing the serving system to stop early once a quality target or latency limit is reached.

### Towards Realistic Evaluation of Uncertainty Quantification for LLM-Based Recommender Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33036v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL, cs.LG, stat.ML
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33036v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 17:48:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 17:48:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Hao Wu, Sipeng Chen, Weikai Li, Tianyi Chen, Qiancheng Yang, Hang Zhou, Yizhou Sun, Wei Wang, Xiao Luo
- **Comment**: 27 pages, 5 figures. Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 11/2026. Reviewed on OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=GXDe6REdu3

- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) provide a flexible interface for recommendation systems. However, their issued rankings require reliable instance-level uncertainty monitoring. In this scenario, comparing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods becomes challenging because LLM rankers lack a standard item-classification head. Their scores may depend on option labels, prompts, generation procedures, and the available user and item evidence. Existing methods derive uncertainty from different sources and apply it at different stages of the recommendation process. Some methods evaluate an issued ranking, whereas others use uncertainty to change the ranking itself, making direct comparison difficult. Here we introduce a fixed-ranking benchmark which evaluates eight heterogeneous UQ signals as record-level monitors under a shared output contract. We evaluate these signals across four recommendation datasets and two LLM backbones under a shared fixed-ranking protocol. From the results, we draw three main observations. First, UQ method ordering changes across backbones even on the same reference instances. Second, ranking-aligned Top10Mass provides the strongest scalar Error@10 detection in several evaluation conditions, reflecting effective cutoff-aligned monitoring, while its advantage narrows for popularity-tail targets. Third, scalar UQ signals are weak at identifying which control has the larger clipped NDCG@10 difference, whereas source-specific signals, including Perturbation Rank Instability and simple listwise distances, provide stronger paired-control agreement, reflecting their sensitivity to the two controlled evidence sources. These findings motivate us to evaluate ranking-failure detection, robustness across recommendation conditions, and responses to controlled evidence changes as distinct properties of UQ.



### Delegent: A Multi-Agent Harness That Reduces Cost without Sacrificing Performance
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33098v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33098v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-24 18:34:40+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-24 18:34:40+00:00
- **Authors**: Lingwei Gu, Jimmy Lin
- **Comment**: 11 pages, 2 figures, 5 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Agents built on frontier language models are expensive. At every step, the frontier model re-reads its entire context and executes every subtask regardless of its difficulty. This paper explores the simple thesis that much of this cost can be removed by delegating easy subtasks to a small language model, or SLM, that runs on hardware one already owns. To test it, we build Delegent, a simple and effective agent harness built on Claude Code, in which the frontier model stays in charge of every task but hands focused subtasks, such as investigating code, running tests, and searching a corpus, to SLM subagents. Existing approaches such as cascades and model routers usually choose which model executes the entire task, whereas in Delegent, the frontier model always leads the execution and delegates easy subtasks to the SLM. No router or model is trained, so either model can be swapped freely. With the help of a 27B FP8 SLM, Delegent reduces the frontier cost of long-horizon coding by 67% and of agentic search by 59%, with no loss in task performance, and even a 2B SLM that fits on a laptop reduces the coding cost by 66%. The savings grow with the legwork that the frontier model would otherwise do itself, and a short instruction, which leaves the main coding tasks to the frontier model, accounts for most of the saving and keeps the 2B SLM from losing tasks. ### SAG-Mem-1.0 Technical Report
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33124v1

- **Authors**: Yuchao Wu, XingCheng Liang, Xuanze Wang, Yini Huang, Yue Wu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Long-term conversational memory requires retrieving complementary facts scattered across dialogue sessions, including intermediate evidence with limited similarity to the original question. Existing approaches often rely on derived memory representations or persistent relational structures, introducing construction costs and potentially losing source details. We present SAG-Mem, a conversational memory framework that preserves complete dialogue messages in non-overlapping chunks and discovers complementary evidence through query-time lexical expansion. Memory construction requires no model inference, using a lexical index without LLM-based extraction, summarization, or semantic relation construction. At query time, BM25 retrieves initial candidates, an evidence evaluator identifies useful chunks, and lexical cues from selected evidence guide an additional retrieval round. Retrieved dialogue is further annotated with calendar interpretations of supported relative-time expressions, preserving the original wording and temporal granularity. We also introduce SAG-Mem-Lite, an alternative configuration that replaces evidence scoring with embedding-based candidate scoring, achieving lower operational cost than Full Context and all memory-based baselines across both benchmarks with low query latency. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval, SAG-Mem achieves overall scores of 83.08 and 80.20, respectively, the highest among the evaluated methods on both benchmarks. On LongMemEval, it exceeds the strongest compared baseline by 7.20 points. SAG-Mem-Lite achieves scores of 77.09 and 77.20, respectively. These results support dialogue preservation and evidence-guided retrieval expansion as an effective design for long-term conversational memory.
