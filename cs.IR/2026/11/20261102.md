### EvoMemState: Evolving Memory State for Personal Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03743v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03743v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-02 02:42:04+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-02 02:42:04+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhishang Xiang, Yunbo Tang, Zhongqiao Liu, Junkai Chen, Zerui Chen, Qingguo Hu, Yuxuan Zhang, William Grant, Qinggang Zhang, Jinsong Su
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Long-term memory enables large language model (LLM) agents to provide continuous personalized assistance by retaining user information across interactions. Existing systems support this process through fact extraction, organization, updating, and retrieval, but largely maintain memories as independent facts. This fact-centric paradigm may overlook how new facts update, constrain, or conflict with earlier ones, while leaving their applicability, role, and priority for the current request implicit. In this paper, we introduce EvoMemState, a state-centric framework that augments persistent facts with two complementary states: Relational States that capture how facts remain valid and relate to one another as memory evolves, and a Reasoning State that determines how these facts should be applied to the current request. Specifically, EvoMemState consists of three key components: (i) Ontology-Guided Memory State Induction extracts Persistent Facts and constructs Relational States that maintain their validity, scope, and evolving relations. (ii) RL-Based State Evolution internalizes memory-use experience into a policy through reinforcement learning to generate a request-specific Reasoning State. (iii) State-Aware Reasoning then combines the relevant facts with their Relational States and the generated Reasoning State to guide the personal agent. Extensive experiments on long-term dialogue and memory-use benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over existing memory systems in retrieval completeness and memory-grounded response accuracy. Our code and implementation details are available at https://github.com/XMUDeepLIT/EvoMemState.git.



### Multi-Teacher Reasoning Distillation for LLM Recommendation with Self-Exploration
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02457v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02457v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-02 03:20:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-02 03:20:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Liangbo Ning, Siyuan Liang, Yi Zhou, Yifeng Zhang, Qing Li, Wenqi Fan, Dacheng Tao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reasoning-augmented recommender systems (RecSys) enhance user preference modeling by generating reasoning trajectories over historical interactions before making recommendations. Knowledge distillation provides a practical paradigm for transferring such reasoning capabilities from powerful large language models to lightweight RecSys. However, existing methods typically rely on a single fixed teacher, remain constrained by the teacher knowledge ceiling, and indiscriminately distill teacher-generated trajectories without assessing their actual utility for downstream recommendation. To address these limitations, we propose MUSE (Multi-Teacher Utility-guided Selection with Self-Exploration), an adaptive reasoning distillation framework that jointly improves reasoning generation and reasoning utilization. MUSE first constructs a diverse reasoning pool using multiple teacher LLMs and progressively augments it via student self-exploration, enabling the student to leverage the strengths of different teachers and gradually explore reasoning strategies beyond teacher imitation. We then develop Rank-Gain Utility Assessment (RGUA), which evaluates each candidate according to the normalized full-catalog rank improvement of the ground-truth item and identifies the most and least recommendation-beneficial trajectories. Based on these utility-aware trajectory pairs, we introduce dual-level contrastive learning objectives that guide the student generator to prefer beneficial reasoning while training the recommendation encoder to more effectively exploit recommendation-relevant preference evidence. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework.

- **Summary**: Improving recommendation algorithms for the Web requires coordinating interacting design choices and learning from experiments. - **Summary**: An indirect prompt injection can affect a search-augmented large language model only after its carrier page reaches model context. - **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Relevance modeling is fundamental to e-commerce search, determining how well retrieved products satisfy the needs expressed in a user's query. Accurate judgments require interpreting user intent, comparing product attributes, and applying business-specific matching criteria. Current methods based on large language models (LLMs) leverage contextual understanding and reasoning, focusing on incorporating domain-specific context and enhancing reasoning performance. Despite these advances, difficult query-product pairs remain challenging when evidence from different perspectives supports conflicting conclusions. A single reasoning pass may fail to reconcile these conflicts and ensure that decisive evidence guides the final judgment. To address this challenge, we propose Collaborative Adjudication with Debate and Replay Experience (CADRE), a framework for evidence-based adjudication in e-commerce relevance assessment. CADRE organizes three expert agents sharing a frozen backbone to analyze product attributes, user intent, and business rules in separate contexts, while a trainable Verifier assesses their supporting evidence against the applicable matching criteria to determine relevance. Under expert disagreement, a Verifier-guided debate elicits targeted clarification and revision of disputed interpretations before final adjudication. We optimize the Verifier through multi-turn group relative policy optimization and adapt successful-experience replay so that trajectories yielding correct terminal labels can be reused alongside fresh rollouts in subsequent updates. For efficient online serving, we use CADRE as an offline teacher to distill its relevance judgments into a lightweight student model. Offline evaluations and online A/B tests on an e-commerce search advertising platform demonstrate that CADRE improves relevance classification and key business and relevance satisfaction metrics.

- **Summary**: Large language model (LLM) embeddings have been increasingly adopted in sequential recommendation to encode static item attributes and alleviate the limitations of item ID embeddings under sparse interactions. - **Summary**: Generative recommendation generates the semantic ID (SID) of the next item from a user's interaction history. Existing training objectives mainly supervise target SID tokens, while on-policy distillation (OPD) provides denser supervision through a teacher's probability distribution over all tokens for more effective and efficient training. However, OPD can aggravate popularity bias: teacher feedback favors popular-item tokens, transferring this preference to the student, while student rollout favors popular items, leaving unpopular-item SID paths rarely generated and supervised. These two sources of bias reinforce each other and reduce recommendation diversity. We propose HERO, a diversity-aware OPD framework. Heterogeneous teacher distillation trains two teachers specializing in popular and unpopular items and routes each training sample to the teacher matching the popularity group of its ground-truth item. The student learns from the routed teacher's probability distribution along its own generated SIDs. Diversity-aware item rollout incorporates ground-truth SIDs of unpopular items into training, covering SID paths that the student rarely generates. Only the student is used for inference. Offline experiments on an industrial dataset and three public datasets further demonstrate its effectiveness, diversity, and robustness. Moreover, we deploy HERO in the "Guess What You Like" module on the 1688 homepage, where a 15-day online A/B test shows a 3.412% increase in gross merchandise value (GMV).
