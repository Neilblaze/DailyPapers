### KnowMe Paper Radar: Lightweight Discovery of Promising Papers in the Age of Autoresearch
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23702v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23702v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 01:47:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 01:47:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhuo Chen, Jian Wang, Jinjie Gu, Lijun Wu
- **Comment**: Ongoing work. 67 pages with 34 figures and 37 tables. Project website: https://hackerchenzhuo.github.io/knowme-research-ai/en/ GitHub: https://github.com/hackerchenzhuo/knowme-research-ai
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The growing volume of topically similar papers makes it difficult to identify new work that can offer useful research insights or methodological guidance. We present KnowMe Paper Radar, a lightweight discovery tool that combines multiple sources of evidence into personalized reading priorities. Users specify topics, priorities, exclusions and dates; the system brings together topical relevance, recency, author research histories, collaboration paths and direction-specific institutional context. By exposing each score component and its supporting sources, KnowMe helps readers select promising papers for their current questions and discover relevant work beyond familiar authors. Automatic acquisition, version merging and graph-cache reuse keep this workflow convenient without large language model (LLM) inference during ordinary use. Experiments on 174,640 acquired records show more than threefold faster repeated context preparation, while cached abstracts improve normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank 10 (NDCG@10) from 0.412 to 0.598 on expert-judged retrieval tasks. Complementary analyses examine how the same accumulated records support semantic retrieval, research-history modeling and literature-grounded planning. A browser interface and a structured command-line interface (CLI) make these capabilities available to both researchers and research agents, positioning KnowMe as a practical upstream tool for literature-informed recursive self-improvement (RSI) workflows.



### CONCORD-Rec: Catalog-Conditioned Modality Utility for Semantic-ID Diffusion Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23710v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23710v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 02:09:51+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 02:09:51+00:00
- **Authors**: Haopeng Jin, Bingkang Shi, Tao Yu, Yifei Ma, Hao Liu, Hongzhu Yi, Jinwen Luo
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal generative recommendation with discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs) requires distinguishing historical modalities that merely alter token predictions from those that genuinely reduce catalog-level ambiguity. When audiovisual, visual, or textual streams are missing, degraded, or semantically conflicting, uniform fusion and unconstrained token gates can propagate misleading content across iterative denoising steps. We propose CONCORD-Rec, a reliability-conditioned discrete diffusion recommender supervised by counterfactual catalog-partition utility. Given a fixed tokenizer and a partially masked SID state, CONCORD-Rec evaluates out-of-fold teacher coalitions over the exact feasible catalog support and aggregates item posteriors across the target code partition at each unresolved position. Rather than distilling raw token probabilities directly, the resulting counterfactual loss differences supervise an availability-masked tokenwise gate that allocates bounded modality residuals around a behavioral denoiser while retaining an exact behavioral fallback slot. We establish theoretical fixed-support ranking stability bounds under gate perturbations and evaluate position-level and code-conditional allocation across audiovisual and image-text recommendation benchmarks under controlled missingness, signal degradation, and semantic conflict. Across 20 source-compatible protocol-dataset rows, CONCORD-Rec improves NDCG by 4%; under matched 30% conflict it improves NDCG by 14.63%. The full-vs-uniform-T=16 sweep improves quality by 2.00-3.58% while reducing p95 latency by up to 86.89%.



### ReflectRec: Cycle-Consistency Preference Verification for LLM-based Recommendation

- **DOI**: 10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.1591
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23969v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 07:10:39+00:00

- **Comment**: 15 pages. Published in the Proceedings of EMNLP 2026 (Main Conference)
- **Journal**: Proceedings of the 2026 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2026), pages 36183-36197, Budapest, Hungary
- **Summary**: CoT-style LLM recommenders generate a natural-language preference note as a chain-of-thought prefix to ranking, but the note is trusted without verification, and the ranker never consults behaviorally similar users. We propose ReflectRec, organized around a preference-to-history round-trip verifier whose recall score serves as an operational signal of preference-to-history recoverability, enabling conditional regeneration for low-recoverability preferences and selection among regeneration attempts. Around this verifier, we wire a semantic Stage-1 prompt, conditional regeneration, and Forward-Look retrieval that injects future watches of behaviorally similar users as a collaborative hint. On MovieLens-100K and MovieLens-1M, the full pipeline outperforms CoT4Rec-ICL by +16.25 / +4.46 pp Hit@10, with positive gains across all ValScore buckets and three LLM backbones (Qwen3-30B, Qwen2.5-72B, and Llama-3.3-70B). We also observe a similar verifier-guided regeneration pattern on Amazon Electronics.



### MARS Technical Report
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24006v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24006v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 07:56:58+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 07:56:58+00:00
- **Authors**: Tong Liu, Chaoqun Hou, Longxiang Xiong, Junhong Lou, Jiaqi Zheng, Qixin Li, Zixin Zhao, Yong Duan, Xiannian Hu, Fengze Jiang, Yu Zhu, Cheng Guo, Xinyuan Qi, Zi Zhuang, Ping Liu, Lv Shao, Zongwei Wang
- **Comment**: 46 pages, 5 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Modern industrial recommender systems use multi-stage cascades--retrieval, pre-ranking, ranking, and re-ranking--to narrow millions of candidates to a personalized list. While each stage deploys sophisticated models, pipeline-level decisions on channel activation, quotas, model selection, and score fusion remain engineer-tuned and user-agnostic within each scenario.   This report presents MARS (Multi-stage Agentic Recommendation System), extending personalization from individual-stage predictions to full pipeline orchestration. A near-line agent built on fine-tuned Qwen3-0.6B generates per-user configurations--a seven-dimensional action covering pipeline mode, retrieval quotas, model selection, fusion weights, and reranker settings--from a constrained action space. Configurations are validated, cached, and consumed asynchronously; the cascade never waits for LLM inference. Schema checks, fallbacks, and a kill switch ensure serving safety.   The policy is trained in three stages: Confidence-aware SFT distills schema-valid generation from a teacher model; Format-Anchored Preference Optimization (FAPO) learns action preferences via ActionMask with posterior weighting; Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) refines the policy through Sandbox replay with a frozen reward model, without exposing exploratory actions to live traffic.   Two-week A/B tests across five Taobao scenarios with over ten million daily users show positive gains on all key metrics. The largest reach +5.22% V2P and +7.34% GMV, with positive CTR lifts on both click surfaces. About 20% of requests use a lightweight Minimal mode, and deployment shows 12 ms lower mean serving latency versus production.



### X-split Argus: Learning Reusable History Tokens for Efficient CTR Prediction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24283v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24283v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 11:58:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 11:58:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Alexander Ploshkin, Nikita Stepanov, Alexey Pismenny, Viacheslav Ovchinnikov, Nikolay Savushkin
- **Comment**: Alexander Ploshkin, Nikita Stepanov and Alexey Pismenny contributed equally to this work
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Accurate click-through rate (CTR) prediction depends on user representations that capture both persistent preferences and recent intent, yet processing extensive behavioral histories for every ranking request is costly under production compute and latency constraints. We present X-split ARGUS, a framework for ad ranking whose history compressor and online ranker are trained end to end. The compressor encodes past interactions into a compact set of tokens that are computed offline and reused across candidates, while the online ranker scores ads through cross-attention to these tokens and representations of recent events. Training combines an autoregressive loss for click feedback with distillation of candidate-specific click logits from a larger teacher that scores each candidate using the uncompressed history. The number of compressed history tokens and the length of recent history can be adjusted to fit serving compute and latency budgets. Offline evaluation shows a 0.81% relative reduction in normalized entropy (NE) compared with the production baseline. Deployment in a large-scale real-time bidding system increases online inference cost by 15% relative to the production baseline. An online A/B test against this baseline yields relative gains of 4.65% in a business metric reflecting the monetary value of conversions generated for advertisers and 2.96% in clicks.



### Task-Oriented Network Ranging via Joint Graph and Schedule Design
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24702v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **eess.SP**, cs.IR, cs.IT, cs.NI, eess.SY
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24702v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 16:30:02+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 16:30:02+00:00
- **Authors**: Zijian Zhang, Yuan Shen
- **Comment**: 16 pages, 14 figures, with appendices
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Measuring inter-node distances is the basis for network localization, whereas acquiring node positions does not require a fully connected graph of distances. Existing complete-ranging protocols for network localization require every node pair to exchange signals, which prolongs the ranging cycle. In this paper, we propose sparse signal-multiplexing network ranging (S-SM-NR), a task-oriented framework that adapts the transmit-listen schedule to the ranging task required by network localization. First, we establish the S-SM-NR framework by formulating the joint design of the task graph and the transmit-listen schedule. Then, we analyze the minimum round count required by S-SM-NR and establish a connectivity condition for network localization. Subsequently, we develop a closed-form joint design that constructs the sparse task graph and the balanced transmit-listen schedule. In particular, the joint design can achieve the minimum round count under the complete physical connectivity. Finally, simulations show that the proposed S-SM-NR scheme reduces the occupied-round count and improves the localization accuracy by shortening the ranging cycle.



### Variance-Optimal Counterfactual Policy Design for Sample-Efficient Online A/B Testing
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24757v2
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24757v2)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 17:11:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-19 06:42:24+00:00
- **Authors**: Shashank Gupta, Subhadeep Maji
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Online A/B testing is the standard approach for evaluating changes to user-facing systems, such as a new recommendation or ranking policy, before deployment. While the difference-in-means estimator of a standard A/B test is unbiased, it often suffers from high variance. When the treatment and production policies choose the same actions with similar probabilities, the resulting observations can add noise while providing little information about the difference between the policies. Consequently, A/B tests can require large amounts of traffic and long durations to detect small treatment effects. Existing variance-reduction methods mainly improve the estimator after the data-collection scheme has been fixed, and recent overlap-aware designs only optimize a single traffic split between the two policies; thereby, they cannot direct traffic to the actions on which the policies disagree.   In this paper, we jointly optimize how actions are sampled and how the resulting observations are used to estimate the treatment effect. Our contributions are two-fold. First, building on pairwise off-policy evaluation, we derive the variance-optimal action-level logging policy for estimating the average treatment effect between two policies, which has a closed-form solution for any fixed control variate. Second, we use historical logs to jointly optimize the logging policy and a control variate to further reduce variance. Our experimental results in simulated policy A/B tests show that our designs estimate the average treatment effect more accurately than existing variance reduction methods on every evaluated setting, while generally improving statistical power and reducing how often the worse policy is selected. As a result, our designs enable practitioners to reach reliable deployment decisions with considerably less online traffic.



### Can Programs Replace LLM Judges? Executable Program Judges for Scalable and Efficient Relevance Labeling
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24849v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24849v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 18:09:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 18:09:16+00:00
- **Authors**: Eugene Agichtein, Charles L. A. Clarke, Kaustubh Dhole, William Stern
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language model (LLM) judges have become a common way to create large-scale relevance judgments for retrieval evaluation. However, each judgment requires a potentially expensive call to a frontier model, and the judgments are hard to interpret and may not be reproducible. In this paper, we ask whether an LLM can write a readable program for each topic that replaces its per-document judgments. To address these issues, we propose ExecJudges, a method for generating fast, inspectable and scalable judges for automatic relevance labeling. Specifically, ExecJudges uses a frontier LLM to turn assessment guidelines into executable programs, which rely only on lexical matching, standard NLP libraries like parsers, and pool statistics. An ExecJudges-L variant also uses examples of human labels if available. We evaluate ExecJudges on seven TREC collections by comparing the produced labels with trained NIST assessors, frontier LLM judges, and recently proposed per-topic classifiers. Our results show that ExecJudges agrees with NIST assessors at the level of the Claude Sonnet 5.5 LLM judges on TREC DL 2019-2021. With a small number of human labels per topic, the ExecJudges-L variant agrees with NIST more than per-topic classifiers with the same labels on all seven collections, and either exceeds the agreement of LLM judges provided the same labels or is within about 0.05 of it in Krippendorff's alpha. The labels rank the official TREC DL runs with Kendall's tau of 0.87 to 0.94, and writing the programs incurs a one-time cost of $0.17 to $0.49 per topic, with no additional costs for grading. Our results suggest that ExecJudges offers a reproducible, inspectable, efficient and cost-effective alternative to LLM judges for labeling deep result pools for system evaluation or ranking improvements.

### LSP-RAG: Latent Scoring Perspectives for Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25229v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25229v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 22:01:25+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 22:01:25+00:00
- **Authors**: Haochen Liu, Guanghui Min, Song Wang, Yada Zhu, Chen Chen, Jundong Li
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in open-domain question answering, but often struggle with factual accuracy and multi-hop reasoning due to the incompleteness of the training corpora. A promising solution is Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG), which supplements LLMs with structured knowledge retrieved from external knowledge graphs (KGs). However, existing KG-RAG methods either rely on large-scale language models (e.g., ChatGPT) to guide the retrieval process, which leads to high computational costs, or suffer from limited retrieval quality when using lightweight language models, particularly under multi-hop scenarios. We propose LSP-RAG (Latent Scoring Perspectives for Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Knowledge Graphs), a novel KG-RAG framework that enhances hop-wise KG knowledge retrieval through multi-perspective prompted scoring. Each latent scoring perspective is guided by a combination of two types of soft prompts: a perspective-specific soft prompt induces a distinct prompt-conditioned scoring function over candidate triplets, while a contextual soft prompt evolves with each reasoning hop by encoding the query and previously explored KG triplets, enabling the model to preserve consistency and relevance across multi-hop retrieval. Rather than assuming manually defined semantic roles, these latent perspectives are adaptively aggregated to capture heterogeneous relevance signals during retrieval. This design allows LSP-RAG to perform accurate and robust retrieval using lightweight language models. LSP-RAG achieves superior performance across multiple KG-based Question Answering benchmarks compared to existing retrieval systems, including those that rely on much larger language models, demonstrating its effectiveness under limited computational budgets.



### Retrieving Patristic References and Modelling Topics in Bullinger's Reformation Correspondence
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25247v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL, cs.DL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25247v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 22:31:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 22:31:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Lena Pickartz, Philipp Schaer, Phillip B. Ströbel
- **Comment**: Accepted for the Seventh Conference on Computational Humanities Research 2027. To be published in the Anthology of Computers and the Humanities
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The correspondence of Heinrich Bullinger (1504-1575), Swiss Reformer and Antistes of Zurich, documents the political and religious debates of the Reformation, including extensive appeals to the Church Fathers as authoritative sources. Detecting such references computationally remains largely unexplored. We investigate dense and sparse retrieval for patristic reference detection, and metadata-informed topic modelling as a proxy for the topics and ideas discussed. Prior work on Latin intertextuality has fine-tuned dense retrievers for their respective target domain, and we test how well these models transfer to Bullinger's correspondence. We compare lexical (BM25), dense (bi-encoders), and hybrid methods on explicit and implicit references. A Latin encoder fine-tuned on biblical reuse performs best among the bi-encoders (82%, Recall@20 on explicit and 16% on implicit references), while BM25 outperforms dense approaches on explicit citations (94%, R@20). Because BM25 and dense retrieval recover disjoint sets of implicit references, fusing their rankings raises implicit R@20 to 22%, close to cross-encoder reranking of the same candidates (26%) but without its inference cost. For topic modelling, we extend FASTopic with a seed-guided loss function and editorial abstract embeddings. Measurable coherence gains over the baseline remain minimal, but qualitative and temporal analysis shows that some topics track documented historical events. However, dominant topic weights barely exceed a uniform distribution, making topics unusable to inform retrieval. A pilot application of LLM-based concept induction (LLooM) further points towards a more human-interpretable alternative. Together, these results present a first approach to retrieving patristic references and modelling fine-grained topics in Bullinger's correspondence.



### LQ-Rec: Late Quantization for Sequential Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25256v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25256v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-18 22:41:09+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-18 22:41:09+00:00
- **Authors**: Satoshi Koide, Yueqi Wang, Julian McAuley
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative retrieval (GR) for sequential recommendation compresses each item into a short semantic ID and trains an autoregressive decoder to generate next-item codes from a user history. While appealing for cold-start handling and storage, au- toregressive GR suffers a structural scaling limit: typically, longer semantic IDs reduce quantization error in principle but degrade ranking quality and quadratic memory usage against semantic ID length. We propose Late Quantization (LQ), a non-autoregressive paradigm that confines discretization to the output (item) side of the pipeline. The encoder and scoring rule operate in continuous space, while items are stored and matched as semantic IDs produced by an end-to-end-trained residual quantizer. This architectural shift permits an additive error decomposition and a pairwise ordering theorem that jointly bound the quantization- induced ranking drop, a structural guarantee absent in prior semantic-ID recommenders. Across four public benchmarks, the resulting recommender, LQ-Rec, delivers three capabilities simultaneously: ranking quality improves monotonically with the semantic-ID length and outperforms existing baselines; a single trained model supports anytime compression, tuning ranking quality against inference cost without retraining; and LQ-Rec shows superior generalization on a memorization/generalization partition.
