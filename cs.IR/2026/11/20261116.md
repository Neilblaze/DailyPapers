### Straight to the Product: What Online Retailers Miss When Shoppers Ask AI First
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20378v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CY, cs.HC
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20378v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 01:10:36+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 01:10:36+00:00
- **Authors**: Michael Iannelli, Alan Ai
- **Comment**: 5 pages, 3 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: When people ask an AI assistant what to buy, what happens next at the store? We link the same people's assistant conversations to their later browsing at twenty large online retailers. People go to the product the assistant named: among shoppers who had not visited the store in the past week, visits to the named product within a day rise from 0.02 to 2.07 per 100 answers (+2.0 points [1.7, 2.4]), and on days with a product-page visit at the store, 44% show a product from their own answer, against 1.5% for a matched answer given to someone else. The result replicates in a separate five-month period. Few arrive through the assistant's link: 89% of visits carry no sign of it, and 54% follow a web search, the same share as their usual visits. When a search comes just before the first visit, it names the answer's product 28% of the time (1% in earlier weeks), as if the search box were an address bar. The chat names the product; the store logs a search. Once there, shoppers keep browsing.   We propose product follow-through, the share of answers followed by a visit to the product they named, as a way to evaluate shopping assistants and agents by what people do next: it needs no referral data or provider logs and can be computed for each assistant.



### What Unlinked Aggregates Still Prove: Ranker Comparisons from Exact and Differentially Private Item Counts
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20388v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20388v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 01:32:23+00:00

- **Authors**: Chang Xu, Hao Wang, Gedi Zhou
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 4 figures. Accepted for publication in the Privacy and Security of Big Data special session at IEEE BigData 2026. Code available at https://github.com/AnthonyXu109/psbd26-artifact
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Privacy controls can prevent an evaluator from linking purchases to the recommendations users received, while a merchant can still release complete item-level purchase counts for the evaluation cohort. We ask when these counts identify which recommender performs better. The counts constrain who could have bought each item. We bound the performance difference over all assignments consistent with the counts. A reduced transportation network handles full catalogs and can certify attainable endpoints. If the interval excludes zero, it identifies the better recommender for that cohort. With differentially private counts, one confidence guarantee applies jointly to all comparisons from the same release. In two e-commerce cohorts of about 20,000 and 100,000 users, exact counts narrowed every interval compared with bounds that ignored the counts. Most decisions involved a recommender that favors popular items. For NDCG@10, which rewards ranking purchased items higher, each setting combined one of three personalized pairs with a pattern and rate of missing links. Exact counts settled 14 of 168 settings in the smaller cohort and none of 81 in the larger cohort. In the smaller cohort, exact counts broken down by user group settled two personalized pairs when 90% of users remained linked, at the cost of revealing more about individual purchases. Whole-cohort private releases with one count per item retained much of the narrowing but settled no personalized pair in either cohort. Grouping less popular items into bins let private counts settle one personalized pair in the smaller cohort.

### IntentFlow: An Intent-Driven Real-Time Multimodal Search System with Image-Text Queries at Xiaohongshu
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20448v1
- **DOI**: None

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20448v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 03:17:14+00:00
- **Authors**: Dongjia Zhao, Yuchen Ma, Chunsheng Shi, Ziqiu Gui, Chao Yuan, Jianing Zhu, Zhaoxing Zhang, Yahui Liu, Daoxin Zhang, Yi Wu, Yao Hu, Cheng Chen
- **Comment**: 29 pages, 7 figures. Technical report
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal search with image-text queries helps users find notes in user-generated content (UGC) communities and serves as a key search tool for multimodal search agents. For such queries, query-note relevance depends on the user's retrieval intent and cannot be determined by visual or topical similarity alone. Simply reranking visual search results cannot fully address this challenge, as notes relevant to the user's intent may be missing from the initial candidate set. This motivates explicit intent modeling to align candidate retrieval and reranking under consistent relevance criteria. We present IntentFlow, an intent-driven real-time multimodal search system deployed at Xiaohongshu over an index on the order of ten billion entries. IntentFlow represents retrieval objectives with a hierarchical intent taxonomy and uses intent-specific relevance criteria to guide data construction, representation learning, and reranking. An offline agentic data workflow acquires candidates and verifies their relevance under these criteria to produce training supervision. The embedding model combines intent-aware contrastive learning, graded ranking, and distribution distillation, while a compact reranker assesses query-candidate evidence conditioned on predicted hierarchical intents. Evaluations demonstrate that IntentFlow delivers substantial gains in search engagement while supporting real-time search at industrial scale. In a user-level A/B test on the Xiaohongshu platform, IntentFlow outperforms the existing production system based on visual search followed by reranking, achieving relative gains of 28.17% in Top-4 query click-through rate, 18.96% in query click-through rate, and 12.56% in average time spent on search results pages per user. Production monitoring records an end-to-end P50 latency of 1.07 s.



### MAGE: Meta-path Adaptive Graph Evidence for Transferable Multimodal Graph Learning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20449v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20449v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 03:18:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 03:18:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Haiyang Jiang, Tong Chen, Xinyi Gao, Hao Wang, Hongzhi Yin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal graphs integrate textual and visual attributes with relational structures, providing a rich representation of complex real-world data. To enable the broad applicability and reuse of graph models, it is crucial for models trained on source graphs to generalize to unseen graph domains. However, this is challenging because cross-modal interactions among neighbors and the neighboring information needed for prediction can vary across target nodes and graph domains. This gives rise to two key challenges: adaptive cross-modal neighborhood fusion, which captures and integrates cross-modal interactions among neighbors to accommodate variations across nodes and domains; and target-specific evidence selection, which identifies the neighboring textual and visual information needed for each target node to support LLM-based prediction. In light of these challenges, we propose MAGE (Meta-path Adaptive Graph Evidence Prompting), a framework for transferable multimodal graph learning. MAGE represents textual and visual node views separately in a heterogeneous graph and introduces two complementary components. Meta-path Adaptive Multimodal Propagation (MAMP) adaptively aggregates neighborhood information along heterogeneous meta-paths and compresses it into graph-aware soft tokens. Evidence-Guided Instruction Tuning (EGIT) selects relevant neighboring evidence for each query and incorporates its graph-aware representations into instructions for a frozen multimodal LLM. Together, these components support transfer from multiple source graphs to unseen target graphs without target-domain parameter updates. Experiments with three source graphs and four unseen target graphs demonstrate strong cross-domain transfer performance on node classification and link prediction, without requiring parameter updates on the target domains.



### When the Bill Falls: Reusing the Old Index Defers,Never Avoids, Re-encoding in Complete Embedding-Model Migration
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20702v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20702v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 08:10:30+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 08:10:30+00:00
- **Authors**: Nannan Zhao, Jiameng Zhang, Taoyu Zhong, Hao Wang, Bo Wang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Upgrading the embedding model behind a vector index requires re-encoding the corpus. Recent work reuses the old index through adapters or window re-ranking. Across sixteen model pairs on passage and code corpora, plus eleven on Natural Questions (NQ), we find three results. First, reuse never avoids re-encoding: if migration must complete, every strategy eventually encodes every document; reuse only changes when that work is paid and the quality served meanwhile. Second, adapters should be compared with the old model, not only the new one. Of five adapter variants, only same-family LLM bridges beat the old model, and only by a negligible margin. Third, window re-ranking cost can be priced from the old index alone; pre-registered replays on all 8,841,823 passages confirmed the prediction on all three tested pairs. Workload skew also matters: across 36 further pre-registered Zipf replays, the planned family matched on 32. These findings yield three pre-migration rules: whether reuse is worthwhile, which strategy to use, and how deep a window to take. The depth rule priced every window that reached target quality to within 4 parts in 10^5 of the corpus. On NQ, a pre-committed plan selected the family on 10 of 11 pairs and priced every window exactly. Quality inputs transferred less reliably across corpus scale, so the model gap and window quality loss must be measured on the serving index.



### CARE: Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding with Listwise Reconstruction for Retrieval-Oriented Semantic IDs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20721v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20721v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 08:27:21+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 08:27:21+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuan Xu, Dawei Chen, Xiaoguang Lin, Xin Chen, Yulong Ou, Haijun Wu
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 8 figures, 5 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative retrieval replaces nearest-neighbor search with the generation of a document's semantic ID (SID), so the SID tokenizer bounds what the retriever can recall. Common SID constructions (residual k-means, RQ-VAE, finite scalar quantization, capacity balancing) optimize criteria defined on document embeddings alone and do not control whether a query can still find its relevant documents after quantization. We propose two retrieval-oriented SID tokenizers. CARE-SID (Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding for Semantic IDs) quantizes in a retrieval-aligned space: a zero-initialized residual alignment network is trained with a query-document contrastive loss before quantization, an anchor loss and a reconstruction target fixed to the original embedding prevent drift and collapse, and reference-vector rating residual quantization assigns the codes. CARE-LR (CARE with Listwise Reconstruction) then distills, for each training query, the similarity distribution that the original embeddings induce over a candidate list into the SID reconstructions, through a function-preserving layer-resolved head. Both keep a document-only encoding interface. We judge SIDs without training a generator, by row-budget recall in a bucketing view (the partition) and a reconstruction view (the SID with its reconstruction). In offline evaluation on industrial video search data, both tokenizers achieve higher reconstruction-view recall than residual k-means, and CARE-LR improves further on CARE-SID, mainly through the listwise distillation. A comparison of two document embeddings shows that contrastive alignment helps most when the embedding has not been trained on search data, and that the embedding affects SID recall far more than the quantizer. CARE-SID is deployed in the generative retrieval stage of an industrial video search system.



### UniGraph: A Unified Graph-based Index for Dynamic Hybrid Dense-Sparse Vector Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20758v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.DB
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20758v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 09:06:58+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 09:06:58+00:00
- **Authors**: Keyu Chen, Xiaoyao Zhong, Jiabao Jin, Peng Cheng, Wangze Ni, Jingkuan Song, Heng Tao Shen
- **Comment**: Under ICDE 2027 Review, 14 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Hybrid dense-sparse vector retrieval combines semantic signals from dense representations with lexical signals from sparse representations, typically ranking items by a query-dependent weighted sum of dense and sparse vector distances. Existing systems commonly search separate dense and sparse vector indexes and rerank the union of their candidates, incurring two retrieval passes and potentially discarding items that rank highly only after the semantic and lexical signals are combined. Constructing a graph-based index directly under a hybrid distance can mitigate this premature candidate loss. However, because varying the hybrid weight alters the underlying neighborhood relationships, a single static graph built at a fixed weight cannot accommodate dynamic query-specific weights. Therefore, we propose a Unified Hybrid Graph (UniGraph), which merges neighbor sets computed at multiple hybrid weights into a single bounded-degree graph index, enabling hybrid vector retrieval with different query-specific weights without index reconstruction. We further develop an adaptive weight-guided search framework that chooses between sparse- and dense-guided entry points according to the query weight, together with a dense-first hybrid distance pruning strategy that avoids unnecessary exact sparse distance computations. Extensive experiments show that UniGraph provides a favorable Recall-QPS trade-off across different query weights. In the mixed-weight setting, UniGraph delivers 27.6%~52.3% higher QPS than the fastest baseline on each dataset at Recall@100 greater than or equal to 0.95. UniGraph has been deployed at scale in Ant Group's production environment, where hybrid retrieval with query-dependent weights is in strong demand.

- **Summary**: Enterprise AI can now perform analytics, reporting, and data-driven workflows, yet the data foundations it relies on remain hard to operationalize: business meaning, relationships, metric definitions, and reporting conventions are absent from database schemas and scattered across undocumented systems and tribal knowledge. ### Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal for Schema Retrieval in Text-to-SQL
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20979v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DB**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20979v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-16 12:14:15+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-16 12:14:15+00:00
- **Authors**: Fabio Picariello, Salvatore Cipolla, Susanna Bonura, Vito Morreale
- **Comment**: Presented at the 5th Italian Conference on Big Data and Data Science (ITADATA 2026). To appear in CEUR Workshop Proceedings
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLM-based natural-language interfaces to relational databases are gated by a schema-retrieval step: the generator can only be as good as the slice of schema it is shown. On a database with hundreds of tables, that slice must be selected and the selection is two-sided---omit a needed table and the query becomes unrecoverable, include too many and the irrelevant ones act as noise that degrades the model's output. When an agentic retriever that lets the model inspect the schema can be too costly or too slow, the fallback is an embedder that ranks tables by cosine similarity and keeps a top-k. This fallback has two weaknesses: a fixed budget is suboptimal question by question, and embedding similarity overlooks foreign-key-linked tables that are necessary for joins. We present Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal (AEGT, ElbowHop for short), a property-graph table retriever that addresses both: an adaptive elbow cutoff replaces the fixed budget with a per-question one under a single aggressiveness parameter, and bidirectional foreign-key traversal deterministically recovers the bridge tables embedding similarity misses. We evaluate the retriever on 3 corpora of increasing candidate-pool size (BIRD Mini-Dev queried per database, 3--13 tables; its eleven schemas merged, 74; and a merged Spider-train graph, 546) and isolate the two mechanisms under a per-question budget-matched protocol. Across these settings the adaptive elbow emerges as the dominant mechanism---at matched recall it roughly doubles precision over a fixed budget on the larger pools, the margin widening as the pool grows---while the foreign-key traversal pays off on the join-critical bridge tables dense retrieval cannot reach, in the single-hop tight-budget case. We characterize the method as a low-cost, recall-robust first stage for schema retrieval on which a downstream selection or generation step can build.
