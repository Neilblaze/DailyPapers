### Exploiting Feature-invariant Scaling Laws for Efficient A/B Experimentation in Recommendation Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13539v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13539v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 04:02:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 04:02:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhiliang Chen, Sammy Nammari, Jiahao Su, Xiaoqiang Luo
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Researchers and engineers improve recommendation systems by performing A/B experiments with new model architectures, optimization methods, and data features. However, running full-scale experiments (e.g., full-model training with online data) for every feature change is prohibitively computationally intensive, while the result from a single small-scale experiment is often misleading. To address this challenge, we show that modern recommendation models exhibit feature-invariant scaling: the change of a model's performance follows a predictable scaling-law even when different feature changes are applied to the baseline model. We introduce an efficient method that exploits scaling-laws and uses statistical inference to predict (with confidence intervals) the full-scale effect of an A/B experiment in recommendation systems. This enables practitioners to efficiently filter out neutral or regressive feature changes with only small-scale experimentation. We validate our method on three distinct recommendation model families---Wukong, HSTU, and LLM-based recommendation models---across more than 40 architectural, optimization, and data changes. Our method accurately distinguishes beneficial feature changes from neutral and regressive ones while using only a fraction of the compute required for full-scale evaluation. It also identifies several changes that improve HSTU's state-of-the-art performance on MovieLens-20M. Our results demonstrate that scaling laws can be practically exploited to make compute-efficient decisions for improving industrial-scale recommendation systems.



### IFRS Compliance Verification: An Open Multi-LLM Benchmark and Content-Conditioned Ensemble Classifier
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13595v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13595v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 05:12:13+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 05:12:13+00:00
- **Authors**: Aghoghomena Akasukpe, Li Yang, Xinyao Zhou, Chen Shen, Miguel Vargas Martin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Verifying corporate financial disclosures against the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) is a core auditing task in which experts cross-reference disclosures with hundreds of fine-grained regulatory paragraphs: a laborious, costly, and error-prone process that motivates growing interest in large language models (LLMs) as compliance assistants. Yet single-model accuracy remains insufficient for high-stakes use, and naive ensembling is vulnerable to systematic per-model biases that can drag aggregate accuracy below that of the strongest individual annotator. This gap is addressed with a multi-stage framework, supported by a new benchmark of 19,998 IFRS regulation-disclosure pairs comprising 11,161 unanimous multi-LLM labels and 8,837 human annotations on items where the LLMs disagreed. The framework couples a three-stage domain-adapted sentence embedding pipeline (BERT masked language modeling on SEC EDGAR filings, unsupervised TSDAE and SimCSE adaptation, and supervised contrastive alignment) with a learned ensemble classifier that fuses frozen embeddings and LLM probability features from GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, and Gemini 2.5 Pro via Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) optimized gradient-boosted trees. On the disagreement set, the ensemble achieves 81.7% weighted F1, exceeding the strongest single LLM by 10.4 points and majority voting by 14.6 points, and outperforms eleven unsupervised aggregation baselines; the retrieval pipeline attains Recall@15 of 45.3% on unseen regulations. The dataset, model checkpoints, annotation platform, and classifier implementation will be publicly released.

### From Counterfactual Edits to Human Narratives: LLM-Driven Interpretations for Recommender System Explanations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13914v1
- **DOI**: 10.1145/3799682.3839968
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13914v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 11:05:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 11:05:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Vojtech Cahlik, Amir Reza Mohammadi, Rodrigo Alves, Pavel Kordik, Eva Zangerle
- **Comment**: Published at CIKM 2026 (Rome, Italy). Code: https://github.com/vcahlik/from-counterfactual-edits-to-human-narratives
- **Journal**: In Proceedings of the 35th ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management (CIKM '26), Rome, Italy, pp. 6954-6959, 2026
- **Summary**: Recommender systems increasingly influence what users discover and consume online, yet the decision logic behind their outputs often remains opaque. Counterfactual explanation (CFX) methods provide an effective way to show which changes to a user's interaction history would alter a recommendation. In recommendation settings, however, CFXs often require many edits, producing long, low-level lists that are hard for users to interpret. Moreover, most evaluations emphasize edit counts while largely overlooking whether the resulting CFXs are coherent and actionable for users. We argue that this gap between generating valid CFXs and communicating model behavior stems from the lack of user-centered abstraction and limited qualitative evaluation. To bridge this gap, we investigate large language models as mediators that summarize raw CFX interaction edit lists into concise natural language explanations describing higher-level preference patterns. We optimize these mediators for correctness, informativeness, and linguistic quality through preference optimization, and evaluate the resulting explanations with human-calibrated metrics derived from separate human-labeled datasets. On the MovieLens-1M dataset, across multiple counterfactual generators and language models, the resulting summaries achieve high overall linguistic quality, while the strongest semantic results are obtained with high-success CFX methods such as ACCENT and SPINRec. Direct Preference Optimization improves the semantic metrics in almost all evaluated settings.



### Controlled Knowledge Engineering for Taxonomy Induction with Tool-Grounded LLM Agents and Runtime-Adaptive Policies
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13966v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13966v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 12:04:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 12:04:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Jonas Limniatis, Bennet Schaarmann, Georg Reuber, Robert Stahlbock
- **Comment**: Submitted to Knowledge-Based Systems. Code: https://github.com/bschaarmann/TIGA
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Taxonomies are structured knowledge representations that support semantic search, recommendation, product catalog organization, and retrieval-augmented generation. Their automated construction from a fixed set of candidate entities requires more than plausible text generation: the resulting hierarchy must be complete, structurally valid, and semantically coherent. Large Language Models (LLMs) are promising for this task, but iterative free-form generation remains vulnerable to omitted or hallucinated entities, invalid hierarchical relations, and inconsistent organizational principles. We introduce TIGA (Taxonomy Induction with Tool-Grounded Agents), a knowledge-engineering architecture that formulates taxonomy induction as an iterative decision process over an explicit state and constrained action space. TIGA combines executable taxonomy-editing tools, an memory that records tool calls, rationales, and outcomes, and a runtime-adaptive organizational policy that the agent revises during inference. An optional global reviewer evaluates the constructed taxonomy against the candidate set and the active policy. We evaluate TIGA on four benchmark taxonomies using two LLM backbones and compare it with single-shot and iterative prompting-based baselines. TIGA achieves the strongest structural-quality results in most evaluated configurations, but does not dominate on every precision measure. The gains are accompanied by substantially higher token consumption, API-call volume, and inference time. Ablation results show consistent benefits from the policy and interaction-memory components, whereas the effect of global review depends on the dataset and the available revision budget. These findings characterize tool grounding and runtime policy adaptation as promising but costly mechanisms for improving the controlled construction of structured knowledge representations.

### LUMIR: Decoupled Multimodal Representation Learning from Heterogeneous Data for Next-POI Recommendation

- **Authors**: Zehui Wang, Wolfram Höpken, Dietmar Jannach
- **Comment**: 16 pages. Accepted at the DaQuaMRec Workshop, co-located with ACM RecSys 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: As multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources has become increasingly available, learning effective representations has attracted attention for improving recommendation performance. Existing approaches often tightly couple multimodal fusion with the architecture and optimization objective of a specific downstream model, which limits the generality and reusability of the learned representations. To address these limitations, we propose LUMIR, a decoupled multimodal representation learning framework based on contrastive learning. Multimodal information collected from heterogeneous data sources is first semantically unified into a shared textual representation space using large language models (LLMs). An interaction-aware fusion mechanism is then designed to model cross-modal interactions, while sequential signals are incorporated as a structural constraint to guide representation learning. To evaluate the learned representations, experiments are conducted on a multimodal point-of-interest (POI) dataset under the next-POI recommendation setting across multiple sequential recommendation backbones. The results show that LUMIR consistently outperforms existing multimodal fusion baselines in both accuracy-oriented and beyond-accuracy metrics, demonstrating that decoupling representation learning from downstream optimization provides an effective paradigm for learning reusable multimodal representations. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/wzehui/LUMIR.

### SHARP: Semantic Hierarchical Agentic Reasoning for Next POI Prediction with Dynamic Memory
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14220v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14220v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 15:37:53+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 15:37:53+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuhang Zhan, Silin Zhou, Zhuo Chen, Haoxiang Lei, Yunren Feng, Lisi Chen, Shuo Shang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Next point-of-interest (POI) prediction supports personalized location services by anticipating users' next destinations. Recent methods leverage large language models (LLMs) for semantic reasoning and graphs for collective mobility modeling, improving POI prediction. However, three challenges still remain. Bridging LLMs' semantic reasoning and specific POI prediction remains challenging. User mobility also evolves over time, making it necessary to retain and update historical knowledge within limited LLM contexts. Meanwhile, global transition graphs smooth out time-specific category transition patterns. To address these challenges, we propose Semantic Hierarchical Agentic Reasoning for Next POI Prediction with Dynamic Memory (SHARP), an agentic hierarchical framework in which category-level reasoning guides specific POI prediction. A trajectory memory agent maintains a dynamic trajectory memory bank that preserves earlier mobility patterns and tracks behavioral changes with limited context growth. We construct multi-granularity time-aware category transition graphs with global and time-conditioned views to preserve temporal regularities obscured by global aggregation. A category predict agent combines these graphs with trajectory memory to predict likely next categories. SHARP learns semantic IDs (SIDs) through a residual-quantized variational autoencoder (RQ-VAE) to construct semantic POI representations, which are jointly optimized with a category-guided POI predictor end-to-end. On three real-world datasets, SHARP improves Acc@5 and Acc@10 by an average of 5.29 and 4.52 percentage points, respectively, over the strongest baseline.

- **Comment**: Accepted to ReNeuIR @ SIGIR 2026. 16 pages

- **Summary**: Transformer sentence encoders provide strong retrieval quality, but their inference cost becomes a bottleneck when embeddings must be produced on CPUs or refreshed frequently. ### Point-in-Time Encoders for Earnings Calls
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14325v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR, q-fin.ST
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14325v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-10 16:49:15+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-10 16:49:15+00:00
- **Authors**: Alessio Brini
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 3 figures, 7 tables. Accepted at the 3rd IEEE International Workshop on Large Language Models for Finance, IEEE BigData 2026. Code: https://github.com/Alessiobrini/point-in-time-encoders-for-earnings-calls
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Text encoders turn earnings calls into vectors for search, for language models that answer from retrieved passages, and for forecasting models. A live retriever can use an encoder trained once on all available text, as finance retrievers are, but a backtest needs an encoder that read no text written after the date of each vector. To address this look-ahead bias, from 412,248 calls we train vintages under four pretraining recipes, with yearly cutoffs from 2010 to 2024, and each vintage reads only text dated on or before its cutoff. We then fine-tune each vintage on the question-answer pairs of the calls themselves. The vintages we train on calls alone, with 33.8M parameters, retrieve answers on held-out calls better than the general retrievers we tested in every test quarter. Their vectors also raise by about half the rank correlation between next-day returns and forecasts built from past returns and firm size. When each vintage continues the previous one, a later vintage retrieves better on earlier quarters than the vintage available at the time, and a point-in-time language model that answers from the passages the later vintage retrieves inherits the gain. The 2024 vintage of our families also forecasts earnings surprises better, although the interval of this gain includes zero on calls that no vintage read. It forecasts returns no better, so point-in-time vintages matter most for retrieval and little for forecasts of returns.

- **Summary**: Text-to-SQL systems over large pooled catalogs must first retrieve the tables a question needs.
