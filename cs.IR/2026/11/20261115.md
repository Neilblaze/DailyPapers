### StaleBench: Isolating Stale Retrieval in Agent Memory, and Pricing the Fix
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19752v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19752v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 03:29:02+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 03:29:02+00:00
- **Authors**: S M Mahbub Murshed
- **Comment**: Accepted to Findings of EACL 2027. 39 pages, 3 figures. Code and data: https://github.com/thenexuslab/stalebench-release
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: An agent that stores facts over time eventually stores several versions of the same fact. Asked a value-agnostic question - "which team does X play for now?" - a similarity retriever cannot prefer the current version, because every version matches the entity the query names. StaleBench isolates this stale-retrieval failure on 892 real updating facts, with currency labels from the source (Wikidata validity years via TempLAMA), never from a retrieval score. Our central result is that retrieval metrics can rank memory policies backwards: a retention policy and its retain-everything control return the identical statement at rank 1 for every test query, yet differ by 29.5 and 65.6 points in reader accuracy on two readers when no date is shown. Contamination@5, the metric we add, ranks them correctly where current@1 and current@5 do not. Withdrawing the lexical anchor that statements carry costs deployed keying mechanisms tens of points of freshness; entity resolution on both the write path and the query recovers most of the loss. Three deployed systems (Mem0, Graphiti, LangMem) and a reimplemented ERASE each handle supersession differently, and no single scoring convention describes all four. We release the data, a frozen evaluator, and reference baselines.



### Exact Sparse Assignment with Costly Target Embeddings: Fixed-Realization Certificates and an Empirical Audit
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19770v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19770v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 03:50:53+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 03:50:53+00:00
- **Authors**: Hamidreza Hasani Balyani, Mahtab Movahhedrad, Seyed Pouyan Mousavi Davoudi
- **Comment**: 7 pages, 2 figures. This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Embedding-based one-to-one association is common in vision and data integration: detections are associated with tracks and records are linked across databases. Such pipelines often solve a sparse optional assignment whose edge scores add a cheap term to an inner product of neural embeddings. When target embeddings are costly, one can acquire them selectively and stop once a certificate proves that the current assignment is optimal. We give such a certificate for unit-norm embeddings. It bounds the unknown part of every reassignment by a support function and maximizes the resulting robust gains with a second sparse assignment; a shared owner-centred bound dominates any fixed-centre triangle bound. We make precise what is certified when network features are not batch-invariant: the realized values of the acquired rows and every unit-ball completion of the rest, not a second forward pass in a different batch. Auditing against a separately batched cache tests a different property; on our first pilot it produced a spurious $2.49\times10^{-6}$ objective failure. We audit the corrected protocol on four public text entity-matching benchmarks with two frozen encoders; no image data is used. All 96 certificate simulations and 120 online selective timings were exact on their own realization (largest objective gap $6.82\times10^{-13}$). At group size 8 the shared certificate acquires on average 60% of candidate-active targets, against 74% (mean centre), 69% (MEB) and 60% (zero centre): it beats the mean and MEB centres but not the zero centre, and savings concentrate in one benchmark (7% acquired). The prespecified runtime claim is contradicted: selective execution is +0.510 s slower per run (dataset-cluster 95% interval [+0.248, +0.772]). Uniform-microbatch re-encodings preserved the optimum in only 5 of 8 encoder-dataset cells, and 45 of 120 online runs disagreed with an eager cache at near-ties.



### Reflection Is Not Always Worth It: Budgeted Reflection Allocation for Recommendation Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19847v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19847v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 07:11:33+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 07:11:33+00:00
- **Authors**: Jinfeng Xu, Zheyu Chen, Ziyue Peng, Jianheng Tang, Zheng Lin, Wenhao Yuan, Jian Chen, Shujie Li, Victor Leung
- **Comment**: Accepted by WSDM 2027
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: This work aims to make recommendation agents more efficient by reducing unnecessary LLM reflection while preserving full-catalog ranking and inference reranking. Since BRAVE changes only how training-time reflection is allocated, it does not introduce new user data requirements or additional LLM exposure beyond the base agent protocol. The main potential risk is that a poorly calibrated allocator may assign less reflection to users whose preferences are sparse, niche, or under-represented, which could weaken personalization for those users or indirectly affect item exposure.   This risk can be mitigated through deployment-time auditing and conservative allocation design. In practice, reflection budgets should be evaluated not only by aggregate Recall or NDCG, but also across user-history lengths, item popularity groups, and application-relevant cohorts when such analysis is permitted. The allocator should rely on task-side ranking evidence rather than sensitive attributes, and fallback policies can reserve a small portion of the budget for random or fairness-aware coverage when subgroup robustness is important. Privacy risks can be controlled with the same protections required by the base recommendation agent: minimizing user text sent to the LLM controller, anonymizing user identifiers, retaining only necessary interaction evidence, and applying standard access-control and logging policies. Overall, BRAVE is designed as a cost-aware allocation layer, and its ethical deployment depends on validating that the saved computation does not come at the expense of vulnerable users or responsible exposure.



### NoLBERT-Calls: Reading Earnings Calls Without Hindsight
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19849v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR, cs.LG, q-fin.GN
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19849v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 07:19:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 07:19:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Kevin Foley, Jonathan Hartadi, Shivesh Prakash, Hino Wirachapong Suwanphibun, Swapnil Vatsal
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Language models are now a standard tool for measuring what managers say on earnings calls, but the encoders behind these measures were usually pretrained on text from the same years a backtest covers. Holding out a test period does not remove that hindsight, because it sits in the features. We adapt NoLBERT, an encoder pretrained only on text published between 1976 and 1995, to earnings calls without letting it read anything dated after 31 December 2023. The calls supply the training signal themselves: every analyst question is followed by management's answer, and the model learns to pick that answer out from the other answers of the same call. We test on every US call from January 2025 to October 2026 (20,323 calls, 254,575 questions) and make every modeling choice on calls from 2024. The adapted model, NoLBERT-Calls-2023, ranks the true answer first for 78.0% of questions (95% interval 77.8-78.2; chance 8.0%), against 26.3% before adaptation. It is 1.0 point ahead of a call-adapted ChronoBERT, 3.4 points ahead of a call-adapted FinBERT, and 18.7 and 19.8 points ahead of the best off-the-shelf retriever and BM25. A year-by-year test over 2013-2026 finds no look-ahead premium: relative to a model adapted only through 2012, the released model does no better on the years it was trained on than on years it never saw. The price of the cutoff is compute: under one shared 150-minute recipe NoLBERT adapts least well of four encoders, and its lead comes from a longer, staged adaptation of about 13 GPU-hours.



### Attribute or Abstain: Evaluating Evidence-Grounded Retrieval on the Low-Resource African Business Web
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19900v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19900v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 09:29:27+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 09:29:27+00:00
- **Authors**: Bakary Gibba, Muhammad Abba Mandara, Karim Ismail Abd-Ellah Mohamed
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented systems that research companies are judged by how much they find, yet in markets where public evidence is sparse, a confident answer without support costs more than no answer. We introduce AfriBizClaims, a benchmark of 150 company-research queries across six African markets in English, French and Portuguese, including queries about fictitious companies that a correct system must decline. Each claim a system makes is judged against the page it cites by an AI judge, checked against a second AI judge from another model family and against two human annotators, and abstentions are scored against answerability labels. We compare BM25 over a pooled corpus, standard web retrieval-augmented generation, and a multi-step agent pipeline (Konexium V1), all using the same language model. The two simple baselines are well attributed: 98% of their judged claims are supported by the page they cite, and they decline every field of every fictitious company. Counting claims about a company other than the one asked about as errors, the agent pipeline's per-query precision is 68%, and it never answers a question about a named company correctly: it substitutes other firms, including for 5 of the 24 fictitious names, and it is the least stable between runs and the most expensive. In the pipeline we evaluated, more steps between the query and the evidence did not buy more grounded answers. We will release the queries, system outputs, labels and short evidence excerpts.

### DetouR: Plugging Association Routes into Search Agents for Multi-hop Question Answering
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19967v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19967v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 12:21:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 12:21:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Tingwei Chen, Yunxiao Shi, Zhendong Chu, Zhiying Deng, Min Xu, Qingsong Wen
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Search agents answer multi-hop questions by interleaving reasoning with retrieval, deciding at each step what to search next. Existing agents follow semantic routes: they infer what is missing and phrase it as a query, reaching only what the model can name. Yet the decisive page is often one the question never names, frequently just one hyperlink away from evidence already retrieved. Following such association routes reaches more gold pages than semantic search under the same budget. Exploiting them raises two challenges: obtaining the association structure without costly offline graph construction, and keeping linked evidence in the reader's limited context, where relevance- and fusion-based selection discards it. We propose DetouR, a plug-in that reuses the links arriving with retrieved pages and adds no search or LLM call. Link Rerouting replaces near-repeat queries with routes to linked pages, chosen by a lightweight learned scorer; Chain-aware Context Selection runs personalized PageRank over these links so that evidence chains reach the reader intact. Across four agents, DetouR improves EM by 4.0 points on 2Wiki and 1.4 on HotpotQA on average. We also find that heavier LLM models struggle with this non-semantic evidence, adding little over these simple selectors or even falling behind. In summary, our work reveals association routes as a cheap complement to semantic routes that current search agents overlook: added as a plug-in, they improve every agent we test, in replay and in live runs, at no extra search or LLM cost.



### Take a Step Back: A Dual-Granularity Raw Feature Empowered Intermediate Fusion Graph Convolutional Network for Multimodal Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19974v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19974v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 12:37:46+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 12:37:46+00:00
- **Authors**: Jinfeng Xu, Zheyu Chen, Shuo Yang, Jinze Li, Wenhao Yuan, Ziyue Peng, Puzhen Wu, Shujie Li, Jian Chen, Zheng Lin, Edith C. H. Ngai
- **Comment**: Accepted by WSDM 2027
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Although multimodal recommendations have shown the ability to learn user preferences and item properties by leveraging explicit multimodal information, they remain constrained by two critical limitations: low-quality representation learning and modality fusion. For the former, existing solutions primarily focus on multi-granularity representation learning; however, we argue that training such representations is difficult due to the inherent data sparsity problem in recommendation scenarios. For the latter, existing solutions often rely on contrastive learning to align representations of different modalities to facilitate modality fusion, yet alignment-only objectives may over-compress valuable modality-specific information. To this end, we suggest \underline{``Take a Step Back"} and propose a \textbf{D}ual-granularity raw feature empowered \textbf{I}ntermediate fusion \textbf{G}raph \textbf{C}onvolutional \textbf{N}etwork (\textbf{DIGCN}) for multimodal recommendation. DIGCN leverages the multimodal understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct dual-granularity raw features directly. Furthermore, DIGCN introduces an intermediate fusion graph convolutional network equipped with dynamic cross-modality weight correction, designed to capture cross-modality signals and achieve effective modality fusion. Comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets validate that DIGCN consistently outperforms a variety of strong baselines. Additional analyses confirm its superiority under varying levels of data sparsity and cold-start scenarios. Notably, DIGCN demonstrates high extensibility, capable of further benefiting from additional contrastive learning-based alignment tasks.



### EchoSpec: Training Search Agents to Weigh Evidence Above Echoes
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20034v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20034v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 14:25:51+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 14:25:51+00:00
- **Authors**: Shihao Yang, Can Zeng, Xinzhe Li
- **Comment**: 24 pages, 5 figures, 15 tables. Code: https://github.com/unihsy/EchoSpec
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Search agents trained with reinforcement learning decide what to retrieve, when to search again, and when to answer. In real corpora, one claim is copied and paraphrased across many pages while the primary source settling a question appears once. We show that reinforcement-learned search agents grant echoes the credence of independent sources and lose their ability to abstain. Across five base models from 3B to 32B and seven released RL agents on multi-hop Wikipedia questions with primary evidence intact, eight paraphrased false-claim echoes overturn 58-73% of previously correct answers, while exact-match RL drives deleted-evidence abstention below 15%. Conditioning on retrieval localizes the failure to evidence acquisition: once primary evidence enters context, agents overrule eight echoes as reliably as one, whereas echoes fill top ranks and halt search early, so larger budgets, forced search turns, or passive passage filtering alone fail to recover displaced evidence. We introduce EchoSpec, specifying reliability across five corpus transformations with known target answers and training in two stages. Verification warm-start teaches multi-hop bridge-entity recovery past echoes, after which group RL optimizes an evidence-gated $λ$-reward with an outcome-independent acquisition bonus and scale-preserving advantages retaining the optimal rule $λ/(1+λ)$. Across Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B backbones on nine evaluation suites, EchoSpec cuts eight-echo deference by 30-44 points on Qwen2.5-7B down to 29-31%, nearly quadruples eight-echo reach to 26-27% with zero-shot transfer to unseen BGE and BM25 retrievers, and raises standalone QA precision to 47-55%. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/unihsy/EchoSpec.



### How did data sharing evolve in the decade since the FAIR Principles?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20319v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.DL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20319v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 23:29:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 23:29:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Sheeba Samuel, Daniel Mietchen
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A decade after the publication of the FAIR Guiding Principles, what have we learned about data sharing, and what do data availability statements (DAS) and other materials accrued over that period tell us about how data-sharing practices, attitudes, and infrastructures evolved? Here, we present a large-scale longitudinal analysis of over 6.8 million biomedical articles from the PMC Open Access Subset, spanning 2011 to 2025. Using an automated pipeline, we extracted and classified DAS, repository links, accession numbers, and supplementary materials, assigning each article a data-sharing score and independently FAIR-assessing a subset of cited dataset URLs. We find that explicit DAS adoption rose from ~2% in 2011 to 68% in 2025, while "available on request" statements grew in parallel from near zero to over 19%. The corpus growth amplifies both patterns: articles with a DAS containing an explicit repository mention rose from 132 in 2011 to 94,697 in 2025, while "available on request" articles rose from 77 to 173,474 over this span. Structural biology and genomics consistently achieved the highest data-sharing scores, while clinical research and public health ranked lowest. Gold Open Access journals showed a small advantage over subscription journals, while Diamond Open Access journals outperformed both. Internationally collaborative articles scored higher than domestic ones. A gender gap emerged: female first-authored articles' scores declined more than male first-authored ones. The analysis further covered additional dimensions, from compliance with funder/journal mandates and JATS XML tagging standards to computational reproducibility signals and the co-occurrence of DAS with ethics statements. A decade on, the gap between declaring data availability and actually sharing machine-readable, reusable data persists across fields, infrastructures and demographics.
