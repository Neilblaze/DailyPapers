### Baseline and Incremental: User, Item, and Request Debiasing in Instagram Feed Ranking
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27423v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27423v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 00:20:04+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 00:20:04+00:00
- **Authors**: Hsin-Chiang Chen, Hao Wang, Gedi Zhou, Mingze Gao, Abhishek Kumar, Yiben Yang, Haoyu Wang, Hongjing Xia, Bo Chen, Zhengyu Su, Xian Sun, Chang Xu, Kevin Han, Weiwei Li, Zhongchang Sun, Yao Zhang, Qifei Wang, Peggy Yang, Yihan Wu, Haoran Liu, Siyang Sun, Xingyu Pan, Shannon Phu, Haoming Chen, Kevin Zhao, Chen Liang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A feed ranker scores candidates with a value model, a weighted sum of engagement predictions. Each prediction conflates a baseline, what this viewer, item or request would produce anyway, with the incremental amount by which this candidate exceeds it. The literature mostly removes the baseline. We argue that a multi-signal value model should keep the raw prediction and add the incremental part as a separately weighted term. On Instagram Feed, three debiasing families deployed independently, for viewer habit, item popularity and request context, are one parameterised operator, and a design procedure picks a point in it. Media-type (format) bias goes instead to a debiased label chosen by the same procedure. In a controlled four-arm ablation of deployed Feed terms (neither term, each alone, both), the two-term arm beats both single-term arms at production weights on point estimates in 11 of 13 judged (term, metric) pairs. Of the 26 direct contrasts, 17 are significantly for it and only 1 against it, on impressions. The ablation also suggests that the offline diagnostic should screen signals, not rank them. Such terms have served Instagram Feed production for most of a year. Beyond Feed, Meta, whose apps serve billions of people, has adopted the operator across surfaces, apps and both ranking stages. Its launches there drive sessions and daily active users, the core visitation and retention metrics.



### Factual Access States in Diffusion Language Models: From Mechanism to Knowledge Editing and Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27580v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27580v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 03:45:45+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 03:45:45+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuchen Guo, Zhuoyuan Mao, Yiwen Guo
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Factual recall is a lookup: some representation inside the network serves as the key, and what that key is made of decides which queries can retrieve the fact, what an edit must cover, and what a retriever can read. We call it the factual access state. In autoregressive (AR) models the subject position enriches attributes largely independently of the relation, and relation-specific selection happens later, at the readout position. In masked diffusion language models (DLMs) we find the state is assembled differently, and much earlier: in the first attention layer the wording of the relation is routed into the position of whichever entity is visible and the early feed-forward layers look the fact up on this fused representation. The state therefore varies with the relation's surface form and with the query direction, so the queries about one fact occupy a region of state space rather than a point, and an operation on the fact succeeds only for queries that enter it. We treat the state as an interface to a DLM's factual knowledge, and put it to two uses. Writing to the state gives a knowledge editor: from one declarative statement, Multi-View Edit fits one shared value per direction across that region and installs it in the early-MLP block, preserving source-prompt reliability while substantially improving paraphrase and reverse question answering, where AR editors gain near zero. Reading it suggests a complementary use: the state at each entity position can serve as a relation-specific retrieval vector with no training, returning both pieces of evidence for two-entity queries on which pooled, prompted and lexical retrievers almost never return both.



### GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CV**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27829v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 08:19:14+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 08:19:14+00:00
- **Authors**: Weijian Jian, Siyang Chen, Yuguang Yang, Yajun Zhang, Chunyu Xie, Dawei Leng, Yuhui Yin
- **Comment**: 28 pages, 7 figures, including appendices
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Existing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retrievers often requires costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval framework trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feedback. A shared context-preserving retriever produces page and region multi-vector representations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candidate region crops. An auxiliary vision-language model attends to each crop separately, aggregates crop-specific contexts using the routing weights, and predicts the question followed by the answer under a next-token prediction objective. Gradients through the routing distribution assign query-conditioned credit to the indexed region representations, without requiring query-to-region annotations. At inference, the auxiliary model is removed. GARRET also reuses the contextualized region representations, together with page summary tokens, to construct a compact page representation. We introduce ViDoRe-V3-Region, a corpus-wide benchmark containing 200,682 fixed candidate regions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page.



### Robustness Analysis of Pareto-based Joint Evaluation of Fairness and Relevance in Recommender Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27850v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27850v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 08:31:48+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 08:31:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Theresia Veronika Rampisela, Maria Maistro, Tuukka Ruotsalo, Christina Lioma
- **Comment**: Accepted to ACM Transactions on Recommender Systems (TORS). Extension of arXiv:2502.11921(1)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recommender Systems (RSs) can be evaluated for many aspects, two of them being recommendation relevance and fairness. These two aspects can be evaluated either in isolation, using a measure for each aspect; or jointly with a single`relevance-aware' fairness measure that quantifies fairness with respect to relevance. Yet, neither approach provides a simultaneous evaluation of both relevance and fairness: the first approach may result in two different rankings of models, each corresponding to an evaluation aspect; the second approach often provides an imbalanced evaluation of the two aspects. To address this gap, we propose Distance to Pareto Frontier (DPFR), a new approach to jointly evaluate RS fairness and relevance. DPFR is based on a Pareto frontier of relevance and fairness scores from existing measures. The frontier is generated from user-item interaction data and represents various Pareto-optimal trade-offs between the two aspects. Thus, the distance from a point in the frontier provides a joint score of fairness and relevance. As DPFR can be used with existing measures, it is modular and intuitive. Our experiments with 4 recommenders and 3 fairness-aware re-rankers on 6 real-world datasets reveal inconsistent associations between existing measures and DPFR, showing that they are not reliable proxies for evaluations based on Pareto optimality. We further examine the robustness of DPFR as a joint evaluation approach for fairness and relevance by conducting experiments with artificial recommendations and with various fairness-relevance trade-offs, as well as by analysing DPFR's agreement to simple measures of fairness and relevance. These analyses show that DPFR is more robust than existing joint evaluation approaches. Finally, we provide guidelines for evaluating fairness and relevance jointly with DPFR. Our code: https://github.com/theresiavr/robustness-DPFR-recsys-evaluation.



### SPAR: Stepwise Personalized Autoregressive Recommendation with Prefix-Balanced Semantic IDs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27898v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27898v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 09:17:14+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 09:17:14+00:00
- **Authors**: Chunrui Zhang, Gaoyun Lin, Chang Zhou, Zheng Hu, Qiran Song, Shimin Cai, Tao Zhou
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Semantic ID (SID)-based generative recommendation retrieves items through successive token-level decisions, making personalization at each decoding step essential. However, existing methods insufficiently account for prefix-dependent user evidence and imbalanced historical interaction loads among candidate branches. This can cause irreversible branch-selection errors and favor popular paths. We propose SPAR, a generative recommendation framework that jointly improves stepwise personalization and SID construction. Its PATH Decoder retrieves prefix-relevant evidence from compressed long-term user memories and combines it with recent behavior, user profiles, and event context to guide each token decision. Its RQ-PIB module balances historical interaction loads among sibling branches under each parent prefix while preserving semantic structure. Experiments on large-scale Youku production data show an average relative HitRate improvement of 2.66% over the strongest baseline. SPAR also increases target-prefix retention by 5.79 percentage points and reduces the traffic-weighted largest child-branch share by 32.84% across SID levels. In a 7-day online A/B test, SPAR serves 10% of Youku's production traffic and improves average watch time per user by 3.95% and play conversion rate by 3.38%. These results demonstrate more reliable hierarchical decoding, balanced SID branches, and improved user engagement in industrial recommendation.



### AutoSim: A Self-Improving User Simulator for Conversational Search Evaluation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28054v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28054v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 11:40:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 11:40:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Saber Zerhoudi, Jelena Mitrovic, Michael Granitzer
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 4 Figures, SIGIR-AP 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: User simulators promise to evaluate conversational search systems at a scale human studies cannot reach, but only if their behavior is realistic enough to trust. We study AutoSim, a conversational-search user simulator whose relevance judgments rank a controlled pool of retrieval systems in agreement with NIST across three TREC iKAT years (Kendall's $τ$ of $0.66$, $0.91$, and $0.91$; all $p{\le}0.002$), an agreement that survives removing its persona. AutoSim self-improves against a held-out signal from real users, and we find that fine-tuning its weights makes it behave like real users, which in turn gives a more accurate estimate of how well a system serves them. Reaching that behavior takes more than prompting: when discriminating between real and simulated user sessions, no prompt we tried lowers a held-out real-versus-simulated classifier below AUC $0.85$, whereas fine-tuning the weights drives it to $0.45$, where neither the classifier nor a human reading full dialogues can tell it from a real user. That added realism is what makes the evaluation more accurate: where real users retrieve little, a prompted simulator over-estimates retrieval effectiveness by $+0.136$ nDCG and returns a relevant passage on $57\%$ of the turns where real users find none, enough to change a deployment decision. Only the weight-level simulator escapes this bias. We release the framework and code.



### DeclineCraft: A RAG Abstention Benchmark for Jointly Measuring Combined Retrieval and Parametric Knowledge
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28340v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28340v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 15:07:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 15:07:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Atharva Thakur, Abulhair Saparov, Bruno Ribeiro
- **Comment**: 38 pages, 28 figures, 12 tables. Code: https://github.com/Thakur-Atharva/declinecraft-benchmark
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reliable retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) requires recognizing when neither retrieved documents nor parametric knowledge supports an answer. We introduce DeclineCraft, a dynamic abstention benchmark whose unanswerable questions target Minecraft feature additions after a corpus cut-off, with audits of possible parametric exposure. Later cut-offs support evaluation of future models. Across 27 LLMs, we measure Correct Abstention Rate (CAR; how often models acknowledge insufficient knowledge on unanswerable questions), Answerable Attempt Rate and Answer Accuracy. We find that (1) single-shot retrieval significantly raises CAR in 38 of 40 configurations (model and thinking setting) versus no retrieval. (2) Iterative RAG, in which the model self-assesses its context and can request up to two further retrieval rounds, significantly lowers CAR versus single-shot RAG in 30 of 40 configurations (mean 46.4%), with little change in Answer Accuracy (68.5% to 69.3%). Adding query rewriting and reranking to hybrid (dense & sparse) retrieval lowers CAR for all 12 models tested. (3) Larger models answer more accurately (Spearman $ρ= +0.60$ with total parameters), but neither model size nor release date shows a significant association with CAR. (4) Without retrieval, enabling thinking on the same weights lowers CAR significantly for 8 of 13 models (by 14% points on average), and the responses shift from acknowledged uncertainty to premise denial.



### Multimodal Reranking Is a System One Decision
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28435v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28435v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 16:00:45+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 16:00:45+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhuchenyang Liu, Yao Zhang, Yu Xiao
- **Comment**: Position paper. 19 pages (9 main), 2 figures, 12 tables. Code: https://github.com/Ryenhails/rerank-as-decision
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal rerankers are usually dedicated models: a vision-language model (VLM) is trained on retrieval data to score the candidates of a query. Scoring options is not specific to reranking. System One decision models such as Jev map a state and a typed question to one probability per option without generating text, and general models can make such decisions with little or no training. Reranking is one such decision, with the query and its candidates as the state and the candidates as the options. We therefore argue that a general VLM can serve as a multimodal reranker through System One decisions, and that decision tuning can improve its reranking. We ask a VLM one choice question over the top 16 candidates of a first-stage retriever and rank the candidates by the probabilities of their labels, read from one forward pass. On MMEB-V2 image retrieval, this choice scores 5.4 to 7.3 hit@1 above a yes/no question per candidate, and the yes/no question falls below the first stage. With the choice, zero-shot Qwen3.8-27B beats the supervised Qwen3-VL-Reranker-2B on all three benchmarks and comes within 1.6 points of Reranker-8B on each. It matches Reranker-8B on MMEB-V2 visual documents (84.79 vs. 84.78 nDCG@5), at 1.1 to 1.5 times its latency. JEV-27B-VL, the same backbone tuned on text decisions only, improves page reranking by 0.6 to 1.1 points, with gains on most tasks (sign test, p <= 0.017), and leaves natural images unchanged. The zero-shot model is larger than the supervised rerankers and of a newer generation, so these comparisons are confounded; we discuss this and other alternative views, and the experiments that would test the position.



### Evolving Semantic Indexes for Generative Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28568v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28568v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 17:27:46+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 17:27:46+00:00
- **Authors**: Jinming Nian, Jyun-Yu Jiang, Henry Tsai, Yi Fang, Zhirong Liang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative retrieval (GR) casts search and recommendation as autoregressive generation of discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs). SID design largely determines GR quality, but its discrete, interdependent components (SID construction, collision handling, decoding, and training) cannot be optimized end-to-end with gradients and remain largely hand-designed. We introduce a framework that improves these components through LLM-driven evolutionary program search, to our knowledge the first application of this approach to GR. A locally served, open-weight 31B model mutates executable GR components, so no proprietary LLM API is used, and a 1M-parameter proxy decoder provides fast feedback in place of repeatedly training the 1B target generator. Across 21 SID programs on NQ320K, proxy and target Recall@100 show strong rank correlation (Spearman $ρ= 0.90$). Guided by this proxy, evolution improves the seed GR on NQ320K, MSMARCO300K, and a multilingual e-commerce dataset under a fixed training recipe. On NQ320K, jointly evolving SID construction and collision handling improves Recall@1 from 55.8% to 59.3% and Recall@100 from 85.8% to 90.6%. A component study shows that proxy reliability depends on what is evolved: improvements transfer reliably for the semantic index but not for the generator's training objective or the full pipeline. LLM-driven evolutionary search is thus a practical tool for GR design, provided proxy reliability is established for the chosen search space.



### ECHO: Efficient Cascade with Homogeneous Operators for Distillation-Guided 100K-Sequence Modeling
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28569v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28569v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-20 17:28:16+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-20 17:28:16+00:00
- **Authors**: Wenhao Li, Qinglin Jia, Chenghou Jin, Zihong Xian, Jiezhou Ji, Yanzhi Liu, Zezheng Wang, Ruiming Tang, Chaoyi Ma, Zhaojie Liu, Wenwu Ou
- **Comment**: 12 pages, 8 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Modeling lifetime user behavior sequences is essential for capturing long-range preferences in industrial recommendation, yet 100K-length user sequences make target-aware ranking prohibitively expensive. Search-based methods that retrieve from raw sequences incur candidate-dependent costs that grow with sequence length, whereas latent compression methods reduce computation at the expense of item-level addressability. We propose ECHO (Efficient Cascade with Homogeneous Operators), a framework that balances efficiency and fine-grained retrieval by retaining a subset of original event records per user via a Distilled Selector (DS) and retrieving a smaller set of target-relevant records per candidate via a Target-aware Fine-grained Retriever (TFR). DS is supervised by a candidate-aware full-history teacher via per-target distillation, enabling target-aware user-level selection without incurring full-history computation for every candidate. The retriever and target-attention scorer share the teacher's learned relevance parameters, ensuring consistent relevance estimation across the cascade. At a sequence length of L = 100K, ECHO reduces online sequence-branch FLOPs by approximately 160x compared with a representative end-to-end model. Three deployment optimizations, including common user inputs, compact record encoding, and key-side projection absorption, further reduce memory, storage, and data-movement overhead. Experiments on KuaiRand-1K and an industrial-scale short-video platform demonstrate relative GAUC improvements of 0.113%-1.520% over state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselines across multiple tasks. Furthermore, online A/B testing shows a 0.613% improvement in app usage time.
