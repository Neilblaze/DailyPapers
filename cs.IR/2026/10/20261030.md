### Context as Infrastructure: Knowledge Compilation for Retrieval in Agentic Systems with Harnesses
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41172v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 02:48:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 02:48:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhichang Yu, Baining Cao, Sbaaoui Idriss, Qi Wang, Gangqiang Xu, Ling Qin, Xiaoling Jin, An Liu, Shaohao Cai, Xiaoyang Bao, Shengle Chen, Changchang Ding, Pengfei Zhu, Jingxiang Xu, Yifeng Mao, Lu Liu, Hai Jin, Yingfeng Zhang
- **Comment**: 13 pages, 2 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented generation is becoming agentic. A search harness writes queries, reads results, reformulates, and decides when to stop. This largely solves accuracy: a harness with only BM25/grep and MiniMax M3 answers 85% of BrowseComp-Plus and 91% on multi-hop FRAMES in our runs, without compiled or vector indexes. It exposes a context problem: an undisciplined loop repeats queries, rereads documents, and fills the window with unused passages. The missing layer is knowledge compilation: a configurable ingestion pipeline where LLMs enrich documents with context no chunk contains alone--navigation hierarchies with claim-evidence leaves, entity graphs, timelines, mind maps, ontologies, wikis, and memory. Compilation is not a better retriever: under a competent harness, structural artifacts gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES, and a PageIndex-style outline degraded at scale. It is a supply layer. A navigation tree whose leaves are claim-evidence pairs--the mechanism we measure--cuts query tokens by 91% when evidence suffices and adds 1.9% when it does not, a 38% average reduction; this implies a 2% break-even sufficiency rate and a measured rate near 43%. A decision gate cuts latency from 140 s to 30 s, though substitution's token saving is bought with retrieval rounds and carries its own latency cost. Other artifacts--ontology, graph, wiki, timeline, mind map, memory--pay off in deployment (governance, integration, onboarding, temporal orientation, continuity) where accuracy benchmarks cannot see them. We describe RAGFlow's pipeline and harness (open source at https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow), which turns the loop into five disciplined stations and keeps the context entering the model the smallest sufficient set; together they form an agent-native database: a store that hands the agent compiled, governed context instead of a ranked list of passages.

- **Comment**: 12 pages, 7 figures, 10 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Map applications bridge everyday mobility with local services, creating opportunities to recommend places by uncovering users' implicit intents. However, utility-driven map interactions provide only indirect and incomplete evidence of consumption demands, making it challenging to infer personalized service needs from observed behaviors. We propose MILES-Rec, a cross-domain POI recommendation framework that infers latent service intents from heterogeneous map behaviors and expresses them as prioritized natural language queries. It first learns from progressive reasoning demonstrations that synthesize user profiles and map histories for situation understanding, intent expansion, and query prioritization. Extensive experiments against general-purpose LLMs and specialized recommendation baselines demonstrate superior POI retrieval utility and strong service and semantic alignment, with consistent gains from behavioral policy optimization across four backbone scales.

- **Authors**: Chongzhi Zhang, Yuqi Zuo, Aixin Sun
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In modern information retrieval (IR), documents are increasingly encountered as structured sequences of visually rich, paginated units, rather than as flat text. Example documents encountered in our daily life include Word files, PDFs, and slide decks. Free text-centric retrieval systems either ignore this structure entirely or destroy it through arbitrary passage chunking. In this paper, we formulate page-centric Visual Document Retrieval (VDR) with pages as the fundamental indexing units and document context available for assessing page relevance. The formulation distinguishes two retrieval granularities, page and document, and three retrieval paradigms: independent-item, evidence-set, and iterative retrieval. We then assess 42 benchmarks using eight criteria covering corpus characteristics, query construction, relevance annotation, and ranking support. Our analysis shows two main findings on current VDR research. First, evaluation remains concentrated on page-level independent-item retrieval, with fewer resources supporting document-level, evidence-set, and iterative settings. Second, queries originating from real user information needs independently of target corpus content and graded relevance judgments are uncommon, while the coverage and verification of relevance judgments vary substantially. Both findings reveal gaps between the capabilities applications require and those existing evaluation protocols can establish. We provide benchmark selection guidance and identify priorities for future benchmark design, emphasizing alignment between task requirements, relevance semantics, and evaluation protocols.



### BrowseFlow: Sparse Event Flow Matching for Generative User Simulation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41374v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41374v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 07:41:35+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 07:41:35+00:00
- **Authors**: Yitian Pang, Jiayi Zhang, Zhenjia Ma, Xiaowei Yue, Dagui Chen, Jian Xu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: User simulation aims to reproduce how users interact with content and products across online platforms. Recent generative approaches have enriched user simulation with behavioral history and contextual information. However, realistic browsing simulation still faces two challenges. (i) Stochastic Responses: Even with the same recommendation feed, the same user may produce different browsing responses, yielding distinct behavioral trajectories. A logged trajectory records only one such realization. (ii) Sparse Key Events: Clicks and likes provide important feedback but occur sparsely within browsing trajectories dominated by progression actions such as scrolling. Many stepwise simulators predict these actions in a dense sequence, where key events contribute relatively few training targets. To address these challenges, we propose BrowseFlow, a conditional discrete flow matching method for generative user simulation. For Sparse Key Events, BrowseFlow represents each response by its browsing extent and an ordered sequence of key events, directly modeling their counts and locations under exposure constraints. BrowseFlow learns the conditional distribution of complete browsing responses through probabilistic extent prediction and discrete flow matching over key events. Random event retention defines probability paths over partial event sequences, while transition rate matching learns stochastic insertion dynamics. For Stochastic Responses, we use the energy score, a proper scoring rule, to evaluate response fidelity and generated variability, complemented by the Wasserstein distance between key event count distributions. We further introduce TaobaoBrowse, a benchmark pairing real recommendation contexts with fine-grained browsing trajectories. Experiments across three datasets demonstrate that BrowseFlow consistently achieves the best distributional performance.

### Fidelity Synopses: Pre-Execution Plan Selection for Approximate Vector Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41407v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DB**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41407v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 08:19:23+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 08:19:23+00:00
- **Authors**: Adithya Ramanatha, Sanskar S. Singh, Maya Ramanath
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Vector search has become a database operator with many physical implementations: different index families, build-time and search-time parameters, and compressed or disk-resident variants. Each trades cost against fidelity, the fraction of a query's exact top-k nearest neighbors that a configuration returns. Deployments nevertheless choose one configuration offline and use it for every query, so they are sized for their hardest queries and overpay for the rest. On a 5.8M-item LAION benchmark with four embedding models, running each query on the cheapest configuration that suffices for it would roughly halve the cost in a typical setting. Exploiting this requires predicting each configuration's fidelity before running it, which is hard because fidelity is defined by the very exact search that approximate indexes avoid. Relational optimizers solve the analogous problem for cardinality with offline synopses; we propose one for vector search. A fidelity synopsis divides the embedding space into cells around a set of landmarks and records, for every cell and configuration, how often that configuration met a fidelity target on a sample of queries. At query time, one nearest-landmark lookup yields a calibrated estimate for every configuration, and an optimizer chooses configurations so that the workload misses the target no more often than its requirement allows. We test the two premises the synopsis relies on, that fidelity varies smoothly across the embedding space and that its loss is specific to each configuration. Held to the same requirement a static deployment is built to meet, synopsis-guided selection lowers cost in all 16 combinations of model and query direction, to a median of 0.72x the static deployment, with a batched lookup of 21-32 microseconds per query. The saving persists against the best static choice among the same indexes, and within a single index at no extra memory.

### Search When Needed, Proceed When Reliable: Metacognitive Reliability Supervision for Efficient Agentic Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41738v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41738v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 13:44:30+00:00
- **Authors**: Minhao Sun, Zhijing Wu, Xian-Ling Mao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Efficient agentic search requires large language models (LLMs) to determine whether they can reliably proceed with their current knowledge or need additional external evidence. Supervision based on final-answer correctness or trajectory-level feedback evaluates realized trajectory performance. However, observed trajectory performance is jointly shaped by stochastic generation, retrieval quality, and downstream reasoning. Such signals therefore provide only indirect evidence of how reliable the current decision state was and how retrieved evidence changed its reliability. An effective reward should encourage search when external information is needed and favor searches that contribute to the answer. To this end, we propose a metacognitive framework for training LLMs to make search decisions based on the reliability of their intermediate reasoning states. Our framework estimates the reliability of local conclusions to distinguish whether the current information supports proceeding without search from how much retrieved evidence improves that reliability. The current-state reliability score assesses whether the model can proceed without search. The search-action score measures how retrieved evidence changes estimated reliability. Together, these scores define a reliability-aware process reward for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), providing finer-grained supervision for search and no-search behavior. Experiments across seven question-answering benchmarks demonstrate that our method achieves a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than strong agentic-search baselines. Our code and model checkpoints will be open-sourced.

- **Summary**: Location-based recommender systems shape where people go in cities and learn from the visits they steer, forming a human-AI feedback loop. Yet platforms judge these systems by their offline accuracy on past visits, a test in which the suggestions never change where people go and which therefore cannot capture long-term effects. Studying these effects in cities is challenging because, unlike online resources, travel constraints and limited venue capacity shape where people can go, while each recommended visit also changes whom other visitors encounter. It lowers both venue disappearance and segregation across algorithms, largely without directing people to venues where they may feel less comfortable. Our results suggest that impact assessments of recommender systems should account for the feedback loop and report segregation and venue disappearance beyond accuracy.
