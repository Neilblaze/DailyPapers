### WeSearch: A Unified Model-Centric Retrieval Framework for WeChat Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00530v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00530v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-31 01:35:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-31 01:35:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Kepu Zhang, Yuzhe Cai, Yue Shu, Dawei Chen, Jinhai Zhu, Yuan Xu, Zehua Dai, Tongke Ni, Ziyao Chen, Chen Fu, Jie Shao, Chao Wang, Xiangmin Wang, Ruifeng Zhao, Rongchun Yao, Jie Yin, Weixin Qiu, Nan Song, Junyang Li, Fan Li, Quanchao Hui, Yulong Ou, Qiang Chen, Hao Cao, Sen Yang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We present WeSearch, a unified, model-centric retrieval framework for large-scale, continuously evolving content search. Prevailing methods assign documents fixed hierarchical Semantic IDs, implicitly assuming each document follows a single coherent semantic path-an assumption that often breaks down for multi-topic documents. WeSearch instead retrieves query-relevant clusters and then ranks atomic documents within the resulting query-specific candidate space, decoupling candidate pruning from fine-grained relevance modeling. Combined with relevance-aware training, compact document representations, and incremental index updates, this design enables weekly-or daily when needed-model refreshes. Deployed as the primary semantic retrieval path in WeChat Search alongside a lexical syntax tree, WeSearch shows consistent one-week A/B gains on corpora from hundreds of millions to billions of documents, and a one-month full-traffic A/B test confirms stable improvements of +0.68% CTR and +2.27% per-user article clicks, together with a 41% reduction in retrieval resource cost and a 19 ms reduction in graphic-text retrieval latency. WeSearch is now fully deployed in WeChat Search, serving 100% of production traffic.

- **Summary**: Persistent Web information assistants must combine evolving document facts with user requirements established and revised across sessions. Yet systematic evaluation of how changes in both sources affect response requirements and evidence attribution remains limited. To address this gap, we introduce Dual-Source Evolution Benchmark (DuSE-Bench), a benchmark comprising 1,000 tasks across five scenarios, including 700 whose required premises originate in both dialogue and documents. - **Summary**: Generating an educational explanation for a learner's query is not a single retrieval step but a pipeline: a system must first retrieve a compact set of complementary resources--passages, figures, examples, activities--that jointly support an explanation, then structure that set into an ordered, timed, role-assigned plan before rendering it as a lesson or video. Existing retrieval benchmarks evaluate neither stage at the level these applications require: they score individual documents rather than explanation sets, and provide no ground truth for how a set should be organised into an explanation. - **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00884v1)

- **Authors**: Shuyang Zhang
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 3 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Web agents and answer engines fetch pages at question time, and liveness checks of cited sources treat an HTTP 200 response as success. We measure how often that assumption fails and what it does to measurements built on fetched pages. We fetched 10,307 URLs in three days with a polite static client and a rendered browser client: pages from 1,507 sites in five strata, random non-existent paths on every site, and the 4,795 URLs cited by expert-verified claims in ExpertQA. We define a taxonomy of what a 200 response contains, label the static 200 responses automatically, and check the labels against 300 items reviewed by an LLM-based assistant; a human check of 60 of them retained every reviewed usability label after discussion. By the automatic labels, 20.9% of the pages that a static client receives with status 200 do not contain the page's main content: app shells, bot challenges, block pages, error and placeholder pages, and partial content. On the held-out test split, the reviewed sample lowers this estimate from 25.5% to 20.0%. 13.1% of the sites whose random-path probe was answered returned 200. Cheap heuristics catch most of it. Text length, probe comparison and redirect-to-root, combined and tuned on held-out sites, recover 86% of unusable reviewed responses at a false-alarm rate near 5%. Content-reading detectors perform comparably: the best, the text classifier, gains five to seven points of recall at more than twice the false-alarm rate. We argue that agents and their evaluations should report fetch outcomes explicitly instead of inferring them from the status line and will release the labelled data and code.



### PACT: Defending Recommendation against Promotional News Rewriting via Attack-Diverse Score Consistency

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00972v1)

- **Authors**: Chongxian Chen, Fan Mo, Xin Fan, Hayato Yamana
- **Comment**: 11 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Personalized news recommenders are increasingly vulnerable to LLM-powered promotional attacks, where content providers rephrase headlines to manipulate rankings without altering underlying facts. Defending against such manipulation requires covering a wide spectrum of LLM generators and optimization strategies--ranging from stylistic prompting to reinforcement feedback--without incurring prohibitive training or inference costs. In this work, we present PACT (Pooled Attack Consistency Training), a model-agnostic defense that decouples relevance estimation from superficial rhetorical variations. PACT integrates three synergistic operations: it pools offline, fact-checked rewrites from multi-generator and multi-strategy attack sources; samples a constant number of variants at each training visit to yield an unbiased estimator of the pool-wide consistency objective under a fixed compute budget; and aligns original and rewritten ranking scores within the same user context jointly with click supervision. Requiring no architectural modifications or test-time filtering, PACT deploys the trained recommender with zero extra latency. Across nine experimental settings spanning three recommendation backbones and three unseen-generator attacks, PACT consistently achieves the lowest promotion rate and target exposure, outperforming the strongest defense baseline by up to 3.0 percentage points in promotion reduction under LANCE. Further evaluations verify that PACT's superiority holds robustly under unseen attack-strategy holdouts and adaptive white-box retraining, while keeping clean recommendation utility virtually intact.
