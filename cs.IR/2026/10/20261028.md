### Predicting What Answer Engines Cite: Open Decision Models, Rerankers and a Distilled 4B Student on Ten Engines under Controlled Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38090v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38090v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 00:11:13+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 00:11:13+00:00
- **Authors**: Elisha Bajemon, Andre-Louis Rochet
- **Comment**: 12 pages, 1 figure, 4 tables. Model: https://huggingface.co/citead/geo-citation-ranker-4b ; benchmark and code: https://huggingface.co/datasets/citead/geo-citation-benchmark
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative engine optimization needs a scorer that ranks candidate pages by the share of an answer they will receive as citations. We measure pretrained rerankers, a hosted typed decision model (Jev), eight open decision models and distilled students on GEO-Bench queries answered by ten engines that receive the same candidate sources in their prompt. Where several engines answer the same query, they agree with one another at a within-group Spearman correlation of 0.704. Jev and two open 27B decision models, used without training, are the strongest scorers at 0.575 to 0.578. A Qwen3-Reranker-4B fine-tuned with low-rank adapters on teacher labels for other GEO-Bench queries, released as GEO Citation Ranker 4B, reaches 0.542 to 0.549 across three seeds, and its differences with the 27B models and with Jev are not distinguishable from zero on 229 queries. Most of the remaining distance between the student and Jev disappears when Jev scores one passage at a time, as the student does. Students trained on teacher labels and on observed engine citations reach the same level, and labels from Jev alone match the two-teacher target at 0.6B. We release the model, the benchmark, the predictions of every reported scorer and the code.



### Manifest-Assisted Retrieval in Artificial Intelligence Search: Evidence Gaps and a Security Evaluation

- **DOI**: None

- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 06:58:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Gangzhen Qian, Kainan Zhou, Chuhong Xu, Hang Xiao, Zhi Jing, Rong Bao, Lu Yi, Zhaoyi Li
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 3 figures, 5 table. Accepted at IEEE FLLM 2026. Author accepted manuscript. Reproducibility artifact: https://fllm-conference.org/2026/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The external sources retained in our non-systematic audit did not isolate the incremental security or utility effect of consuming /llms.txt while ordinary retrieval remained fixed. The initial audit covered 39 sources through August 16, 2026, with later targeted updates through October 14. A purposive 15-tool study observed no automatic requests in 45 trials at one endpoint; a non-probability log study found that 97% of domains with detected files received no recorded requests in May 2026. We separate publication, fetch, model use, and action; define five operational threat paths and paired security-utility estimands; and exercise deterministic pre-model controls with 30 author-defined parser fixtures. We also execute a reduced pilot: 24 tasks across eight sites, five conditions, and 120 real model responses, with ordinary evidence held fixed. Frozen automatic scoring identifies attacker-target adoption in $21/24$ raw adversarial responses, with one further response unresolved; the adversarial URLs-only condition has no known adoptions and one unresolved response. The pilot measures output changes conditional on supplied evidence, without live retrieval or external actions. Together, the framework and pilot distinguish manifest-content effects from publication, discovery, and downstream execution, while leaving full retrieval and agent evaluation open.



### Towards Reliable, Scalable, and Value-Aligned Generative Recommendation for Large-Scale Advertising
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38378v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38378v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 07:17:24+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 07:17:24+00:00
- **Authors**: Yatong Xiao, Yibo Sheng, Lixiang Wang, Mingjie Sun, Yunhao Sha, Pengfei Zhang, Shengyu Wang, Peng Wang, Wenjin Wu, Peng Jiang
- **Comment**: 9 pages, 3 figures, under review
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative recommendation (GR) reformulates item retrieval as autoregressive generation of discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs), offering a unified paradigm for personalized recommendation. GR4AD, as an industrial generative recommendation framework, has greatly improved advertising revenue while enabling efficient real-time serving in Kuaishou.However, continued deployment has exposed three limitations: deterministic SID assignment remains sensitive to semantic boundaries; conventional backbone scaling provides diminishing revenue gains under strict serving constraints; and sequence-level value optimization provides limited credit assignment to intermediate SID decisions, causing likelihood-based beam search to prematurely prune potentially high-value candidates. We present GR4AD-X, which addresses these limitations through reliable SID construction, serving-efficient capacity scaling, and unified value alignment. Specifically, GR4AD-X improves SID reliability through Multi-Path assignment. It expands model capacity along multiple complementary dimensions rather than relying solely on wider or deeper backbones, achieving a better effectiveness-efficiency trade-off under strict serving constraints. Finally, Unified Value-Guided Generation and Discrimination (UniVGD) aligns generation with commercial value through prefix-level reward optimization, value-guided beam pruning, and discriminative reranking within a unified model. Large-scale online A/B tests on the Kuaishou advertising platform demonstrate that GR4AD-X achieves a cumulative revenue improvement of 3.07% over the deployed GR4AD baseline while satisfying strict latency and throughput constraints.



### CoWalk: Cooperative Walks for Agentic Batch Queries on Disk-Resident Graphs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38404v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DB**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38404v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 07:49:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 07:49:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Qiao Liang, Zhiguang Chen, Yue Yu, Nong Xiao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Workloads such as query rewriting, deep research and group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) issue batches of semantically related vector queries. Existing disk-resident approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) systems process these queries independently. On three workloads, 31 to 67 percent of the I/Os of a batch fetch a page that another query of the batch has already fetched. A page cache shared by the batch removes these I/Os, yet it makes a single-thread search only $1.1$ to $1.2{\times}$ faster, because the search time goes to distance computation and the cache does not change the walk of any query. We present CoWalk, which runs the queries of a batch as cooperating walks on one thread. Every query keeps its own candidate list, a page is read once per batch, and the full-precision vector on every fetched page is ranked by every query of the batch. A query therefore reaches the target recall with a shorter candidate list, which computes fewer distances and issues fewer I/Os. At recall@10 of 0.95, CoWalk answers a batch on one thread $1.6$ to $1.9{\times}$ faster than the fastest per-query baseline and issues $2.8$ to $6.9{\times}$ fewer I/Os. On a server it serves $3.0$ to $6.9{\times}$ the throughput of that baseline at a similar batch latency.



### Quantum-Inspired Pairwise Ranking Aggregation via Active LLM Feedback
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38683v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38683v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 18:55:57+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 18:55:57+00:00
- **Authors**: Quan Mai, Susan Gauch, Gibson Nkhata
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Ranking aggregation from multiple retrievers is a powerful yet under-explored technique in modern retrieval and RAG systems. We introduce LLM-IRA, the first extension of the quantum-inspired interactive ranking aggregation framework to document reranking. By leveraging large language models (LLMs) solely for pairwise judgments on high-disagreement document pairs, our method learns fusion weights in a principled, interactive way. The final ranking is a simple, ultra-fast pointwise linear fusion of scores, no full pairwise matrix or expensive listwise prompting is required. Experiments on the BEIR benchmark and TREC Deep Learning tracks with an open-weight instruction-tuned judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) show that LLM-IRA matches or numerically exceeds strong static fusion baselines on 5 of 6 evaluated benchmarks. We report two weight-update variants: momentum blending and a full accumulated-history refit, which together reach statistical significance (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p<0.05) over the best unsupervised aggregator on 4 of 6 benchmarks (DL19 under momentum; DL20, DBpedia, and NFCorpus under accumulated refit, which is the stronger of the two configurations overall). On the benchmarks we profiled (DL19, DL20), it already clears the best unsupervised baseline within as few as 5 LLM calls per query, well before exhausting its typical ~25--30 call budget. LLM-IRA also achieves cost-effectiveness comparable to state-of-the-art zero-shot LLM rerankers at an order of magnitude fewer LLM calls, and requires no training data or model fine-tuning.



### Leaving, Staying, or Living There? Duration Commonsense Reasoning with Human-LLM Co-annotations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38755v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38755v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 19:35:59+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 19:35:59+00:00
- **Authors**: Chenyang Yu, Zhaomin Xiao, Yan Huang, Eduardo Blanco
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Gardening in the backyard implies living there, while checking into an Airbnb more likely means staying for a few days. Such duration judgments are important for understanding human mobility, travel behavior, and social context. However, duration is rarely stated explicitly in social media posts and must instead be inferred from noisy textual, visual, and contextual cues. In this paper, we investigate whether vision-language models possess such duration commonsense. We create a new corpus of geotagged tweets co-annotated by humans and LLMs to serve as a testbed for this task. We find that state-of-the-art models struggle with duration inference, even when guided by multi-turn Chain-of-Thought prompting. Error analyses further reveal that models often fail to integrate critical contextual information, or they overly rely on prominent visual details while overlooking subtle hashtags and semantic nuances. These findings underscore the significant challenges remaining in context-aware multimodal understanding.



### Contextual Embeddings Beyond the Gold Chunk
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38854v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CL, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38854v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-28 20:34:39+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28 20:34:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Sedigheh Eslami, Markus Krimmel, William Barber, Antoine Chaffin, Maximilian Schall, Jonas Golde, Louis Milliken, Ekaterina Glazkova, Denis Bykov, Bo Wang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Contextual embeddings represent chunks of a document with respect to the document's global context. These models are typically trained and evaluated under the assumption that a relevant document contains only a single relevant chunk. In this work, we argue that in real-world use cases a retriever should not only find answers spread over several chunks but also surface the evidence needed to verify them. To address this, we introduce pplx-embed-v2-context, a family of contextual embedding models trained by distilling graded chunk-level relevance from a query-aware context compression model. This removes the need for gold-chunk labels and makes the training targets independent of the chunking. We utilize a vision-language backbone for training and release pplx-embed-v2-context in 9B and 0.6B sizes with compatible embedding spaces. On the evaluation side, we introduce two benchmarks that label both the chunks that answer a query and the evidence needed to verify the answer. Context-bench is a controlled diagnostic, and Q2D-Chunk is built from real-world search queries. To encourage further research, we also publish Q2D-Chunk as a public leaderboard. Our 9B model sets a new state of the art on both benchmarks and ConTEB.   We find that the visual retrieval capabilities of our 9B model transfer from its vision-language base model and propose to extend chunking to image inputs. We demonstrate that our models achieve state-of-the-art performance among dense retrievers on visual ViDoRe-V3 and that performance improves with granular image chunking.
