### Decision-Native Recommendation with Large Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34124v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34124v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 07:46:40+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 07:46:40+00:00
- **Authors**: Daoxuan Liu, Jiayi Liao, Jiancan Wu, An Zhang, Xiang Wang, Xiangnan He
- **Comment**: 12 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) bring world knowledge to user preference modeling in sequential recommendation, but generation-based recommenders express next-item predictions indirectly as text. Generated outputs may fail to identify a unique candidate item, depend on candidate presentation order in the prompt, and lack explicit confidence in the recommendation. Recent studies explore Jev, a model for fast option selection with explicit confidence. Inspired by Jev, we introduce decision-native recommendation, a new paradigm that jointly models item choice and recommendation confidence using LLM representations. It requires valid candidate selection, consistency under candidate reordering, and confidence that reflects recommendation accuracy. We propose Direct Item Choice Estimation (DICE), which learns a personalized next-item distribution over supplied candidates from user interaction history and recommends the highest-scoring item with calibrated confidence. Tree-structured attention shares history computation across isolated candidate branches for order-consistent candidate scoring in a single forward pass. On three recommendation datasets, DICE achieves higher top-one accuracy than all evaluated baselines. Separate evaluations show unchanged item choices under tested candidate reorderings and end-to-end speedups of up to approximately $6\times$ over the evaluated generation-based methods. Its confidence estimates support selective recommendation by identifying more reliable predictions and guide additional computation for low-confidence examples in offline routing.



### LoopGR: Parameter-Efficient Scaling for Generative Recommendation via Recurrent Depth
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34157v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34157v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 08:40:32+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 08:40:32+00:00
- **Authors**: Huizhe Zhang, Yudong Li, Liang Chen, Chengxiang Zhuo, Zang Li
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative recommendation formulates item recommendation as autoregressive generation rather than a discriminative scoring problem. Although empirical studies exhibit favorable scaling behavior in recommendation, unleashing the benefits of scaling remains challenging under the strict model size and inference cost constraints of real-world systems. A central challenge is to combine expressive modeling of user-item interaction histories with flexible computational depth without proportionally increasing model size. We introduce LoopGR, a parameter-efficient scaling framework for generative recommendation via recurrent depth. LoopGR employs a tailored hierarchical recurrent depth mechanism that alternates between a shared generative decoder and a lightweight context updater. For efficient inference, we further develop recurrent trajectory self-distillation that distills a low-budget student from a high-budget teacher by aligning both predictive distributions and evolution directions. Extensive experiments show that LoopGR consistently outperforms strong generative recommendation baselines. Compared with a representative encoder-decoder baseline, LoopGR achieves superior performance while reducing model size by up to 52%. These results establish recurrent depth as a new scaling dimension, offering a flexible and resource-efficient scaling path for generative recommendation.



### MIDA: Multi-Interest Decoding with Adaptive Aggregation for Session-based Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34192v1
- **DOI**: 10.1109/TKDE.2026.3740693
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34192v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 10:05:39+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 10:05:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Linjiang Guo, Nitin Bisht, Shiqing Wu, Guandong Xu
- **Comment**: Published in IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Session-based recommendation (SBR) infers user interest from limited interaction sequences, yet current models show a consistent performance drop when longer histories are available. This degradation is associated with decoder designs that overemphasize recent items while underutilizing distant, yet semantically relevant, interactions, leading to poor modeling of long-range dependencies and multi-interest behaviors. To address this challenge, we introduce MIDA (Multi-Interest Decoding with Adaptive Aggregation for Session-based Recommendation), a lightweight and encoder-agnostic framework that provides a reformulation of the decoding stage. The central design principle is a two-space decoupling: clustering operates on raw item embeddings so that semantic groupings are not distorted by encoder-learned relational biases; encoder outputs are reserved for aggregation, where sequential context remains intact. Concretely, MIDA (i) employs differentiable multi-interest clustering on raw embeddings, preserving semantic structure that encoder representations would otherwise obscure, and (ii) fuses stability and plasticity branches through a gate conditioned on average cluster size, so that the inferred clustering structure directly governs aggregation behavior through a structural coupling across the two stages. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that MIDA consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, with meaningful accuracy improvements while remaining computationally practical.



### $Δτ$-Bench: Pushing Agents Toward a Moving Capability
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.IR, cs.LG, cs.SE
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34238v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 12:10:52+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 12:10:52+00:00
- **Authors**: Shuofei Qiao, Xuanguang Pan, Yijun Chen, Wendi Feng, Bin Wu, Runnan Fang, Yuqi Zhu, Shumin Deng, Emine Yilmaz
- **Comment**: Work in progress
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In this paper, we introduce $Δτ$-Bench, an executable agent benchmark that models the structural evolution of large tool ecosystems. In $Δτ$-Bench, independently evolving services continually reshape how tasks can be accomplished: familiar tools may disappear or be replaced, while more efficient alternatives emerge amid overlapping functionality and noisy documentation. Against this moving capability frontier, we evaluate whether agents can proactively discover efficient tool-use paths and promptly revise their accumulated procedural knowledge, bringing together demands that existing benchmarks fail to examine. $Δτ$-Bench comprises 1,588 validated multi-turn tasks across 42 domains and six successive ecosystem states generated through capability-preserving transformations. Our evaluation shows that even the strongest evaluated model or harness achieves only 42.3% task success, while initial gains from existing skill-learning methods can not consistently translate into better adaptation as the ecosystem evolves. These findings motivate Steward, an ecosystem manager that jointly manages tools and procedural knowledge through incremental skill maintenance and dynamic provisioning during task execution. Experiments show that Steward sustains higher task success across ecosystem changes with lower skill-maintenance overhead, while improving both recovery from disrupted workflows and adoption of more efficient alternatives.



### Recovering Business Rules as Cascade Lookups for Enterprise Field Prediction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34258v1
- **DOI**: 10.1007/978-3-032-37682-4_6
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.DB, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34258v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 12:33:59+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 12:33:59+00:00
- **Authors**: Changxu Duan
- **Comment**: Accepted at ECML PKDD 2026 Applied Data Science Track
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Enterprise sales-order systems rely on manually maintained configuration rules to determine fields such as payment terms, shipping points, and sales ownership. When these rules are incomplete or stale, operators must enter values by hand, and downstream errors spread into billing, logistics, and reporting. This paper studies whether missing or outdated rules can be recovered from historical transactions instead of being relearned as opaque statistical patterns. We introduce a knowledge-graph-guided workflow in which an LLM agent proposes candidate business keys, meaning column combinations such as customer and document type, validates them through SQL profiling, and compiles the resulting evidence into deterministic cascade lookups. The LLM is used only during offline rule discovery. At inference time, prediction is a transparent key lookup with no model-serving requirement. Experiments on enterprise sales-order prediction and public relational benchmarks show that cascades are most effective when fields follow stable business rules, while learned graph models remain preferable when prediction depends on temporal or neighborhood patterns. Drift analysis further turns residual errors into diagnostics for configuration maintenance. Code and data: https://github.com/Fireblossom/CascadER.



### Factual Yet One-Sided: Stance Poisoning and Evidence Balancing in Retrieval-Augmented Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34294v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.CR, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34294v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 13:50:10+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 13:50:10+00:00
- **Authors**: Puyuan Yang, Shaoxuan Dong, Liujie Wu, Xiaochun Cheng, Bo Li
- **Comment**: 29 pages, 4 figures, 20 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The stance expressed in responses from retrieval-augmented generation systems is significantly influenced by the stance distribution of the retrieved materials. Existing research on knowledge poisoning has primarily targeted factual errors. In contrast, this study focuses on injection attacks in which the facts are correct but the stance on a controversial issue is one-sided. At a mean topic-level poisoning share of 18.9% within the post-injection ProCon documents, all four generation models from different vendors exhibited stance shifts aligned with the poisoning direction; across four concentration levels, the shifts increased monotonically.   This paper proposes a Stance-Balancing Middleware positioned between the retriever and the generator. It consists of stance detection, fixed balancing (2 supporting + 2 opposing + 1 neutral), and a debiasing prompt, without introducing additional large language model calls. Across all eight concentration-direction settings, the complete method reduced the magnitude of stance deviation by an estimated 0.106 to 0.350; the six settings at poisoning shares of 11.9%-18.9% remained significant after Holm adjustment (adjusted p <= 0.009). In the cross-model experiment, all eight model-direction combinations moved along the stance axis in the corrective direction, and seven of eight showed a clearly detected reduction in absolute distance from 0. In the concentration sweep, the post-defense intervals included 0 in all eight settings, while the cross-model pro-poisoning results retained small positive residual point estimates of +0.045 to +0.063. For 20 candidate documents, stance detection took approximately 1.5% of the median generation time on a single GPU and approximately 16% on CPU.



### Cross-Lingual Information Retrieval in Tetun for Ad-Hoc Search

- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34362v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 15:14:17+00:00

- **Authors**: Altedio Araújo, Gabriel de Jesus, Sérgio Nunes
- **Comment**: The EPIA 2025 international conference on Artificial Intelligence
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Developing information retrieval (IR) systems that enable access across multiple languages is crucial in multilingual contexts. In Timor-Leste, where Tetun, Portuguese, English, and Indonesian are official and working languages, no cross-lingual information retrieval (CLIR) solutions currently exist to support information access across these languages. This study addresses that gap by investigating CLIR approaches tailored to the linguistic landscape of Timor-Leste. Leveraging an existing monolingual Tetun document collection and ad-hoc text retrieval baselines, we explore the feasibility of CLIR for Tetun. Queries were manually translated into Portuguese, English, and Indonesian to create a multilingual query set. These were then automatically translated back into Tetun using Google Translate and several large language models, and used to retrieve documents in Tetun. Results show that Google Translate is the most reliable tool for Tetun CLIR overall, and the Hiemstra LM consistently outperforms BM25 and DFR BM25 in cross-lingual retrieval performance. However, overall effectiveness remains up to 26.95 percentage points lower than that of the monolingual baseline, underscoring the limitations of current translation tools and the challenges of developing an effective CLIR for Tetun. Despite these challenges, this work establishes the first CLIR baseline for Tetun ad-hoc text retrieval, providing a foundation for future research in this under-resourced setting.



### A Domain-Aware Pipeline for Structured Extraction and Retrieval-Augmented Question Answering over arXiv Superconductivity Literature

- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34391v1)

- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 15:48:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Ivan Iorsh, Aleksandr Kapitonov, Ivan Filianin, Matvey Lesonen, Nikita Chizhov
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Scientific literature in materials physics is growing faster than researchers can manually inspect it, yet most retrieval tools rely on keyword matching or generic question answering that ignores domain-specific structure in PDF files. This paper presents an end-to-end pipeline that transforms arXiv superconductivity publications into a searchable corpus with structured metadata and retrieval-augmented question answering. The system combines thematic corpus construction, domain-aware extraction of materials and superconducting transition temperatures, and hybrid retrieval that merges dense chunk embeddings with structured publication fields. We test whether normalized domain attributes improve evidence retrieval and answer groundedness relative to lexical-only and dense text-only methods. Extraction is evaluated on a manually verified gold set of 59 superconducting materials, 46 of which have confirmed experimental critical temperatures. On the 33-material primary positive subset, domain-aware pair accuracy reaches 45.5% versus 9.1% for a regex baseline. Retrieval and generation are compared under a fixed language model (qwen2.5:7b-instruct) on a 100-question benchmark, varying only the retrieval mechanism: BM25, dense, hybrid, and hybrid with domain metadata. BM25 gives the highest source recall (Recall@5 of 47%), and adding extracted metadata to hybrid retrieval does not improve it (39% versus 41%), which we attribute to noise in the extracted fields; the main bottleneck is locating the supporting span inside a correctly retrieved paper. As a materials-informatics application, literature-derived temperatures are matched to an independent theoretical reference set: 200 materials appear in both sources, and 20 have a median experimental critical temperature above the theoretical value.



### Theoretically Sound Item Similarity Measures for Recommender Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34402v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34402v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 16:04:41+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 16:04:41+00:00
- **Authors**: Noah Daniëls, Joey De Pauw, Bart Goethals
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Quantifying the similarity between items is fundamental to neighborhood-based collaborative filtering, yet many commonly used similarity measures are heuristics that lack theoretical justification. The use of heuristics forces practitioners to rely on empirical trial-and-error, offering little insight into why certain measures outperform others or how they might be improved. In this paper, we present a principled approach for designing item similarity measures grounded in probabilistic modeling and parameter estimation.   By adopting this formal perspective, we derive two previously overlooked similarity measures based on the Bernoulli and multinomial distributions. Through an extensive ablation study we highlight the critical role of bias, probabilistic smoothing, treating the unknowns correctly, and choosing appropriate probability distributions. Finally, we show that the commonly used cosine similarity can be interpreted as the solution to a specific optimization problem, thereby providing a novel perspective on this decades-old heuristic.



### Multimodal Requirements Traceability Link Recovery in Industrial Settings
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34503v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.SE**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34503v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 19:00:49+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 19:00:49+00:00
- **Authors**: Haoming Huang, Shinpei Hayashi, Kazuya Kohata, Myeongki Kim, Yuki Kurihara
- **Comment**: Accepted to APSEC 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Requirements traceability links support activities such as change-impact analysis, coverage checking, and validation. Industrial requirements can include traceable images, which text-only approaches cannot process directly. For a fixed classifier configuration, binary decisions alone offer no direct way to adjust the balance between missed and incorrect links. We extend a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach for inter-requirements traceability link recovery (TLR). Our extension retrieves text and image candidates separately, classifies each retrieved pair with a vision-language model, and filters the predicted links by verbalized confidence. We evaluate our approach on three non-overlapping industrial requirement sets and six public text-only datasets. On the industrial test sets, our approach achieves an F1 score of 0.310, compared with 0.114 for our replication of the baseline approach by Hey et al. Our approach recovers links in all four modality combinations while improving text-only F1 on the industrial data and maintaining similar performance on the public datasets, but recovery remains limited by failures to retrieve related text requirements for image queries. Confidence filtering produces link sets with different precision and recall, removing false positives and some correctly recovered links from the unfiltered link set. This study provides industrial evidence on the potential and limitations of a current RAG-based TLR approach for multimodal requirements and identifies cross-modal retrieval as the component to prioritize in further optimization.



### The Price of Scale in Proactive Memory Recall
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34507v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR, stat.ML
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34507v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 19:07:36+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 19:07:36+00:00
- **Authors**: Yichuan Chen, Xin Wang
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 4 figures, 2 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/RiverChenStillCompiling/price-of-scale
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A long-lived assistant that brings up memories without being asked must decide at every turn whether anything in its store is worth an interruption. We show that this decision is a multiple-testing problem whose difficulty grows with the store size $N$. On streaming replays of LongMemEval and LoCoMo, a similarity threshold tuned to 5% false alarms at $N=8$ reaches 87-100% at $N=2048$, across embedding models and for explicit and implicit cues; LLM judges show the same growth from a lower base. We prove that a gate with false-alarm rate $α$ operates at a per-item false-positive rate of about $-\ln(1-α)/N$, which pins its hit rate to the far left of the single-comparison ROC curve and, under regular variation, makes it decay as a power of $N$ with an explicit constant. The resulting prediction matches the hit rate of a per-$N$ oracle threshold to within one percentage point. The analysis explains why lexical and semantic channels need thresholds growing like $\ln N$ and $\sqrt{\ln N}$, and why per-cue normalisation roughly doubles the hit rate at matched false alarms. A cue-swap conformal gate, which compares the current cue with the user's other cues on the same store, needs no labels and has a finite-sample false-alarm guarantee under exchangeability; on LongMemEval it holds false alarms near 5% at the hit rate of a label-tuned threshold. Three annotators (Fleiss' $κ=0.63$) find that about nine in ten false alarms are genuine interruptions. Code and data: https://github.com/RiverChenStillCompiling/price-of-scale

- **Summary**: The largest open German text datasets are built from broad web crawls, which provide scale but limited coverage of specific domains. We introduce RefinedGAW, a processed and annotated resource derived from 26 semi-annual crawls of the German Academic Web, totaling 122 million documents and 314 billion tokens, with full crawl and capture history of every text. We release a queryable metadata index for fine-grained data selection and a rehydration tool for retrieving and processing source documents.



### HARP-LoRA: Hierarchy-Conditioned Rank Projection for Low-Rank Adaptation in Scientific Table Understanding
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34589v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34589v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-25 21:40:59+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25 21:40:59+00:00
- **Authors**: Hossein Sholehrasa, Mohammadhossein Amini, Doina Caragea, Remya Ampadi Ramachandran, Dan Wu, Zihang Shi, Zelin Li, Lindsey Hulbert, Eder Martinez, Xuan Xu, Majid Jaberi-Douraki
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Tabular data is widely used in scientific applications, where complex layouts, including multi-level headers, merged cells, captions, and footnotes, make information extraction difficult. This is particularly important in pharmacokinetics (PK), where values must be correctly associated with the drug, species, dose, administration route, unit, and experimental condition. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reduce adaptation cost but do not explicitly incorporate relationships between table values and surrounding headers. We introduce HARP-LoRA, a hierarchy-conditioned rank projection method for low-rank adaptation in scientific table understanding. HARP-LoRA extends projected LoRA by constructing token-specific intrinsic representations from the row- and column-header hierarchies of each data token. These representations condition the low-rank down-projection, incorporating hierarchical context while keeping the pretrained model and random projection tensors frozen. We evaluate HARP-LoRA on 950 manually annotated PK tables from 587 scientific articles and on WikiTableQuestions (WikiTQ) using Llama~3.2 3B, Llama~3.1 8B, and Gemma~3 27B. With Llama~3.1 8B, HARP-LoRA achieves a PK F1-score of 57.64\% and WikiTQ accuracy of 53.15\%, improving over LoRA by 1.42 and 6.56 points, respectively. With Gemma~3 27B, it achieves the best PK F1-score of 70.63\% and WikiTQ accuracy of 56.87\%. These results use only 172K, 197K, and 547K trainable parameters, far fewer than LoRA and TableLoRA. Overall, conditioning lightweight adapters on table-header hierarchies improves extraction from scientific tables and reasoning over flatter general-domain tables while maintaining parameter efficiency. Code is available at: https://github.com/hosseinsholehrasa/HARP-LoRA
