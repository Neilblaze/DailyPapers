### PinGER: Token-Efficient Generative Retrieval with Semantic IDs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33435v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33435v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 04:24:45+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 04:24:45+00:00
- **Authors**: Xinyi Li, Sujan Reddy Ale, Yi-Ping Hsu, Zelun Wang, Yilin Chen, Hanyu Li, Hanlin Lu, Arkin Dharawat, Lulu Yao, Yaxin Li, Bella Huang, Xiangyi Chen, Jaewon Yang, Jiajing Xu, Charles Rosenberg
- **Comment**: 11 pages, 5 figures, 8 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative retrieval replaces nearest-neighbor search with token-by-token decoding of an item's semantic ID (SID). But existing SID-based methods primarily use L levels of tokens sequentially to represent each item in the input history. As a result, a fixed context window holds L times fewer items than it would with one embedding per item. We present PinGER, a generative retriever deployed at Pinterest that retains SID outputs while making input histories compact, built on three ideas. (1) An asymmetric attention mask over interleaved SID and dense tokens lets later items attend only to the dense ones, so the model is supervised at every SID position in training yet serves one token per item while computing the same function. (2) Generation is controllable through goal-conditioned outputs, reinforcement learning, and a prefix-diversity penalty, which together steer retrieval toward surface-specific goals and diverse candidates. (3) A serving stack built for efficient wide-beam generation is paired with decoding constrained to valid identifiers and candidate resolution from a host-local index. Deployed in Related Pins, PinGER lifts saves of fresh Pins by 3.60% and overall saves by 0.62%, while reducing the surface's end-to-end latency by 0.7% and eliminating the cost of the nearest-neighbor search it replaces.



### DyHist: Query-Aware Historical Dynamics Modeling for Temporal Knowledge Graph Extrapolation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33473v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33473v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 05:51:02+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 05:51:02+00:00
- **Authors**: Peiyuan Jiang, Yao Liu, Yubo Wen, Yanglei Gan, Lu Liu, Ruojie Zhang, Jiaye Yang, Yuewei Xue, Jiaya Deng, Xiaogu Guo, Qiao Liu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Temporal Knowledge Graph (TKG) extrapolation aims to predict future facts from observed historical events. Existing methods usually achieve this goal by jointly modeling local historical evolution and long-range historical contexts. First, local evolution modeling often summarizes local history into an overall evolving representation, where query-relevant local deviations may not be sufficiently exploited as useful predictive features. Second, existing long-range history modeling often treats historical recurrence as a stable cue, while overlooking that its reliability may vary across relations and recent contexts. To address these issues, we propose DyHist, a query-aware historical dynamics modeling framework for TKG extrapolation. DyHist captures local dynamics by separating recent evidence from medium-range contexts and explicitly modeling their query-relevant deviations as predictive features. It further uses a relation-aware historical prior to calibrate candidate scores with time-decayed support, recent trends, and relation-specific strength, enabling recurrence signals to contribute differently across relations and recent contexts. Experiments on four benchmarks show that DyHist achieves the best MRR against recent baselines. Code is available at https://github.com/PANPANKK/DyHist.



### Novel or Not? PatEx-Gym: Agentic Prior-Art Search for Novelty Assessment in Patent Examination
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33503v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33503v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 07:21:41+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 07:21:41+00:00
- **Authors**: Qiyao Wang, Xinyi Chen, Hongbo Wang, Jiayuan Lu, Jianhang Li, Minghuan Tan, Yuan Lin, Min Yang
- **Comment**: 19 pages, 8 figures, 3 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Patent examination is a challenging real-world task that requires agents to discover sparse prior art, iteratively refine queries, inspect technical evidence, and produce novelty assessments documented in Office Actions (OAs) across heterogeneous scientific and technical domains. However, existing benchmarks largely focus on static and limited candidate sets, leaving the full search--reasoning--assessment process underexplored. We present PatEx-Gym, the first framework for developing and evaluating agents for patent examination, which integrates three components. First, PatEx-Bench provides 1K realistic examination tasks, two large-scale retrieval corpora containing over 1M patents and 52K scientific papers, and a harness runtime supporting iterative search, document visits, reasoning, and OA generation. Second, we introduce PASS, an annotation-free Prior-Art Agentic Search Trajectory Synthesis framework. Specifically, it exploits document visits as implicit relevance signals and evidence-acquisition anchors to model intra-turn query behavior, inter-turn search progression, and trajectory-level evidence and outcome quality, yielding PatEx-SynData. Third, we train PatEx-Agent at the 4B scale via supervised fine-tuning on the curated trajectories, establishing a lightweight baseline. Extensive experiments show that final decision accuracy alone does not fully reflect examination quality, while prior-art discovery remains difficult even for strong frontier models. Our PatEx-Agent achieves competitive performance against substantially larger general-purpose agents. These results show that training on PatEx-SynData enables a compact 4B model to serve as a competitive baseline for prior-art search and patent examination. We view patent examination as a demanding testbed for agentic search and deep research agents.



### SkillQR: Agentic Skill Discovery for Reason-Intensive Query Rewriting
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33768v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33768v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 16:19:31+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 16:19:31+00:00
- **Authors**: Kenan Jiang, Meixiu Long, Zihao Zhang, Ran Xu, Eugene Agichtein, Fei Liu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dense retrievers provide grounded evidence that mitigates hallucinations in outputs from Large Language Models (LLMs). Real-world queries are often verbose, ambiguously framed, or require multi-step reasoning, introducing irrelevant details that weaken semantic matching for dense retrievers. Query rewriting is a widely adopted pre-retrieval technique that leverages LLMs to reformulate queries and bridge the semantic gap between such queries and the retrieval target. Existing rewriting approaches either rely on expensive proprietary models, substantial training data, or iterative retrieval and rewriting, while largely applying a uniform rewriting strategy regardless of the retrieval corpus. We observe that effective query rewriting instructions are not universal: the optimal strategy depends on the retrieval target, rather than the model alone. We introduce SkillQR, a query rewriting framework that automatically discovers reasoning strategy sets from the retrieval target and dynamically routes a query to the appropriate rewriting strategy at inference time. Without relying on training data, SkillQR achieves competitive performance compared to fine-tuned rewriting models on the BRIGHT and R2MED benchmarks, while also providing lower inference latency than iterative rewriting approaches.



### TriageRAG: Failure-Aware Self-Training for Adapting Retrieval-Augmented Language Models to Specialized Domains
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33776v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33776v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 16:26:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 16:26:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Samarth Varshney, Ritu Yadav
- **Comment**: 19 pages, 4 figures, 8 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets large language models (LLMs) answer knowledge-intensive questions from retrieved evidence, but adapting a general-purpose RAG system to a specialized domain such as medicine is difficult when only an unlabeled corpus is available. Self-training approaches such as SimRAG address this by letting the LLM write its own question-answer pairs from domain passages and fine-tuning on the pairs that survive a round-trip consistency filter. We observe that these approaches treat every retained example identically: they never ask whether the student already knows the answer, already extracts it correctly from the retrieved context, or fails - and if so, whether the failure lies with the reader or with the retriever. To tackle this, we propose TriageRAG, a failure-aware self-training approach that probes the student on each self-generated question with and without retrieved context, triages it into one of four categories (known, solved, context-failure, retrieval-failure), and routes each category to the training signal that addresses it, including a parametric route that teaches the reader to answer when retrieval misses. Probing is cheap and repeated every round, so the training mixture follows the student, and the triage histogram doubles as a label-free diagnostic of where the domain gap lies. Experiments on 11 datasets spanning three domains with a Qwen3-4B backbone, and on CS-Bench with Qwen3-8B, demonstrate that TriageRAG outperforms a budget-matched SimRAG by 1.2-6.4 points and is the only method that recovers the closed-book level in every case where retrieval hurts the base model. A single ablation identifies the mechanism: round-trip filtering removes every training context that lacks the evidence, and a reader that never sees such contexts learns to over-trust noisy retrieval.



### Bridging Search Intent and Tabular Data in Dataset Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33936v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.DB
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33936v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-24 22:40:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-24 22:40:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Wenjing Lin, Madelon Hulsebos
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dataset search remains a bottleneck in data analysis workflows, as existing systems largely rely on keyword matching or shallow retrieval that fail to capture user intent. To facilitate intent-driven search, we present two retrieval components to equip multi-faceted dataset search interfaces through natural language. First, we propose Hypothetical Schema Embeddings (HySE) that uses a language model to generate task-aligned hypothetical table schemas which are used for embedding-based retrieval, bridging the gap between intent and table content. We introduce KaggleDS, a corpus comprising 6,503 tables and diverse task-queries derived from Kaggle, and show that HySE consistently improves recall over strong keyword and embedding-based baselines, as well as over a dense table retriever fine-tuned on KaggleDS. We also show that HySE generalizes to end-to-end question answering on, for example, the NQ-Tables dataset, a corpus of 169,898 Wikipedia tables with human-written queries, where HySE improves Recall@10 by +6.0 pp over dense retrieval baselines. Second, we introduce FlexPrune, which translates dataset constraints expressed in natural-language into SQL predicates over a customizable metadata index, enabling filtering across arbitrary metadata attributes. Combined with HySE, FlexPrune increases Recall@10 by +6.0 pp over dense retrieval followed by tag-based filtering. Our results show that natural-language-based retrieval supporting task and metadata queries offers a viable alternative to schema-level keyword search in dataset search.
