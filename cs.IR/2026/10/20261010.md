### Co-adaptive Rule Routing and Constraining for Structured Knowledge Retrieval
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14465v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14465v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 01:45:48+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 01:45:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhisheng Qi, Haoyu Han, Jiaxi Li, Bing He, Huiyuan Chen, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan A. Rossi, Mahantesh M. Halappanavar, Jin Lu, Yu Zhang, Tianming Liu, Yu Wang
- **Comment**: Under review at ICLR
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Structured knowledge retrieval acquires task-relevant evidence from structured knowledge sources for downstream tasks. Existing methods rely on unconstrained retrieval or predefined query-rule supervision, such as author-paper rules for scientific discovery and anatomy-drug rules for precision medicine. Such supervision is rarely available in practice. We propose Co-Adaptive Rule Routing and Constraining Retrieval (CoRE), which jointly learns which reasoning rule to assign to each query and how to retrieve evidence under that rule, without predefined query-rule pairs. A routing agent selects a rule, and its corresponding retriever traverses the knowledge base and ranks evidence that satisfies the rule's constraints. Because routing and retrieval are interdependent, CoRE alternates between reinforcement learning for the routing policy and contrastive learning for the rule-constrained retriever. The selected rules provide supervision for retriever training, while retrieval performance rewards the routing policy. This closed-loop process enables both components to co-adapt without explicit query-rule supervision. Experiments on STaRK-QA datasets across scientific discovery, biomedical reasoning, and e-commerce knowledge management demonstrate CoRE's effectiveness. Ablations show the benefit of co-adapting the router and retriever. Generation-based routing transfers across datasets and benefits from multi-domain pretraining followed by target-specific fine-tuning. Analysis of rule usage across queries also informs automatic rule refinement and discovery.



### RES-MR: Risk-Aware Reasoning for Explainable and Safe Medication Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14474v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14474v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 02:06:48+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 02:06:48+00:00
- **Authors**: Cong Wang, Jin Li, Shoujin Wang, Yishuo Li, Huilin Gu, Wenpeng Lu
- **Comment**: 12 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Medication recommendations seek to deliver personalized drug combinations based on patients' clinical information, where ensuring transparent decision-making and personalized safety are of paramount importance. However, existing approaches predominantly operate as ``black boxes'', offering limited interpretability and hindering clinician trust. Furthermore, they typically rely on fixed, patient-agnostic safety constraints, neglecting the heterogeneity of risk tolerance across individuals, which poses significant risks to vulnerable populations. To address these gaps, we propose \textbf{RES-MR}, a novel Risk-aware Reasoning framework for Explainable and Safe Medication Recommendation using LLMs. RES-MR follows a two-stage training paradigm: (1) an explainable clinical reasoning distillation stage to elicit diagnostic capabilities by fine-tuning on synthesized reasoning trajectories, grounded in patient-specific knowledge from graph retrieval; and (2) a risk-aware policy optimization stage to dynamically balance therapeutic efficacy with personalized safety. This stage employs disentangled drug factors to construct patient-specific reference prototypes that capture desired therapeutic drug features and risk features to avoid. These prototypes enable risk boundary calibration and guide the model toward safe recommendations via reward shaping and clipping. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RES-MR significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of accuracy, safety, and explainability. Our code and data are available at: https://github.com/wangcong2001/RES-MR.



### EVISTA: Unified Multimodal Video Search with Evidence-Aware Temporal Path Optimization
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14510v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14510v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 03:18:13+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 03:18:13+00:00
- **Authors**: Minh-Huy Tran, Van-Thinh Vo, Minh-Khoi Nguyen, Duy-Tan Nguyen, Anh-Quan Nguyen-Tran, Loi Nguyen Khanh
- **Comment**: 15 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: In the era of big data, precise event retrieval from large video collections has become increasingly important and challenging. In particular, locating multiple events from the same video in their temporal order is difficult when different events are described by different types of evidence, such as visual content, on-screen text, and spoken words. We develop EVISTA, a unified multimodal video retrieval system for the Ho Chi Minh City AI Challenge 2026, which integrates visual-semantic, OCR, ASR, object, and metadata search over a shared keyframe index, and propose TEMPO-P, an evidence-aware temporal path optimization method for ordered multi-event retrieval. Building EVISTA for this task reveals two limitations of independent event retrieval: a relevant video may be missed when one event is weakly retrieved, and independently retrieved event candidates may be temporally inconsistent or unable to represent events defined primarily by text or speech. TEMPO-P addresses these limitations by aggregating candidate videos across events and modalities, jointly scoring keyframes using visual, OCR, and ASR evidence, and selecting a compact temporally ordered path within each video through dynamic programming. On five organizer queries and 36 additional multi-event queries, TEMPO-P matches visual-only temporal alignment on visually described events (R@1 0.42 for both) and retrieves queries containing text-defined events, which visual-only alignment cannot represent (R@1 0.38). TEMPO-P is integrated into EVISTA as its ordered-event retrieval mode.



### A Stateful Research Information Infrastructure for Scientific Discovery
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14538v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.DL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14538v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 03:50:03+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 03:50:03+00:00
- **Authors**: Lingyuan Kong, Zhengran Wu
- **Comment**: Project Website: https://gewu-insight.com
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Artificial intelligence (AI) is being applied to an expanding range of scientific activities, including literature analysis, hypothesis generation, software development and automated experimentation. Yet these capabilities rely on a more fundamental condition: scientific information must remain identifiable and traceable across systems and over time. In practice, different versions of a work and their associated records and files are often scattered across services and local directories. This fragmentation forces researchers to identify and contextualize the same work repeatedly, disrupting continuity from discovery to interpretation and reuse. Here we present Gewu Insight, a research information infrastructure that addresses this discontinuity by organizing scientific works as persistent, stateful research objects. It unifies venue-, topic- and author-based discovery records through a shared paper identity. Within a local library, metadata, versioned resources and research context can then accumulate around the same work. These capabilities are integrated into an iterative workflow for scientist-led, AI-assisted research, allowing earlier interpretations to be revisited and refined. A bounded evaluation showed high candidate precision across the three discovery modes, with paper identity, resource distinctions and research state preserved in controlled workflows. By making continuity an explicit property of research infrastructure, Gewu Insight can serve as an inspectable scientific memory layer shared by researchers and AI assistants, supporting human judgment and providing a foundation for future, more epistemically grounded scientific AI.



### LLM-based User-Product Relevance Evaluation for Dynamic Product Advertising
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14550v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14550v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 04:15:08+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 04:15:08+00:00
- **Authors**: Wen Zhang, Xiao Bai, Hsiang-wei Chao, Mengfan Zhu, Tong Zhao, Mingxuan Ju, Xiaodong Liu, Congfei Zhang, Yunzhi Zhou, Yajun Wang, Jinchao Li, Yu Zhang
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 2 figures, CIKM 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Dynamic Product Advertising (DPA) retrieval systems are a core component of modern e-commerce advertising; however, their evaluation remains challenging. Conventional offline engagement metrics are limited by sparse, biased feedback, while online evaluation is costly and time-consuming. In this work, we propose a user--product relevance metric for offline evaluation of DPA retrieval systems. The metric first compresses the engagement history into a compact set of intent representatives via Semantic-ID-grouped, event-stratified segmentation, then assesses the relevance of a recommended product to each individual intent and aggregates them as a user-level relevance score, yielding a stable and interpretable measure of retrieval quality. To compute this metric at scale, we leverage large language models (LLMs) as semantic relevance judges, capturing nuanced functional and semantic relationships that heuristic methods often miss. We propose an agentic, human-aligned prompting framework that generates multi-category relevance guidelines and distills teacher-model judgments into an efficient student model for scalable evaluation. The resulting metric strongly correlates with offline and online business metrics, enabling faster and more reliable DPA retrieval iteration before A/B testing.



### Fine-Tuning Strategy, Not Just Data: A Statistically Rigorous Audit of Gender and Stereotypical Bias in Embedding Models for HR Recommendation Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14579v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL, cs.CY, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14579v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 04:57:38+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 04:57:38+00:00
- **Authors**: Ekta Yadav
- **Comment**: 8 pages, 4 figures, 5 tables. Accepted at RecSys in HR '26 (The 6th Workshop on Recommender Systems for Human Resources)
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Embedding models underpin modern job-candidate matching: resumes, job descriptions, and queries are encoded as dense vectors, and recommendations come from nearest-neighbor retrieval over that space. Any social bias in the embeddings therefore feeds directly into HR recommendations. We audit six multilingual sentence-embedding models (LaBSE- and E5-family, both public and custom fine-tuned) using Bias-in-Bios (occupational gender bias) and StereoSet (stereotypes across profession, race, gender, and religion). We treat statistical validity as a first-class concern: we identify a non-independence error in a naive significance test, where pooling non-independent pairwise similarities inflated a trivial 0.0046 mean difference to $p \approx 0$, and replace it with a profession-level paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, individual-resampling bootstrap confidence intervals, and permutation tests for StereoSet. Under the corrected methodology, knowledge-distillation (KD) fine-tuning of E5-Base significantly increases measured bias on Bias-in-Bios ($Δ= -0.177$, $p < 10^{-8}$; more negative means more bias), although this measure is sensitive to global embedding geometry. The KD result is corroborated by pro-stereotype patterns in the StereoSet profession category (uncorrected $p < 0.05$). Binary-classification fine-tuning of the same base model shows no statistically distinguishable change ($p = 0.218$). Fine-tuning strategy is thus an actionable design lever for practitioners building HR recommenders. We also report which benchmark categories are underpowered, and note that two domain-adapted models could not be included in significance testing due to a software-access constraint, which limits our conclusions.



### Decoupling Knowledge Forgetting and Refusal Learning in MLLM Unlearning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14605v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CL, cs.IR, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14605v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 05:46:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 05:46:42+00:00
- **Authors**: Haodong Chen, Shengtao Wen, Dong Liang, Lingbing Guo, Xiang Chen
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Current evaluations of multimodal unlearning typically focus on whether a model stops outputting target information. This surface-level criterion, however, masks two critical failure modes. First, knowledge suppression techniques often induce confabulation, leading the model to hallucinate facts about the unlearned subject. Second, refusal training merely alters observable behavior without erasing the underlying knowledge; such models might decline open queries but still identify correct answers under multiple-choice probing. To overcome these limitations, we propose Forget-Aware Self-Distillation (FASD). This method is designed to achieve honest ignorance, a state where the model genuinely loses the target knowledge and learns to acknowledge its absence. FASD separates these goals into two complementary channels. The behavior channel distills appropriate refusals from an ignorance-prompted copy of the model itself. Crucially, target tokens are masked from the teacher distribution to avoid accidentally reteaching the forgotten concepts. Simultaneously, the knowledge channel directly suppresses ground-truth answer spans independently of the model's generated responses. Experiments demonstrate that FASD successfully unlearns target knowledge, prevents information leakage in open-ended tasks, and preserves performance on retained data. The resulting model not only forgets the specified information but accurately recognizes the boundaries of its own knowledge. Code and data will be released upon publication.

- **Summary**: Adapting embedding models to new tasks requires training data that reflect task-specific information needs and relevance criteria. ### What Drives AI Citations? Content, Retrieval and Model Knowledge in Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15033v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15033v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 16:18:41+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 16:18:41+00:00
- **Authors**: Mahe Chen, Nick Koudas
- **Comment**: 11 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative search engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity now decide which Web pages users see as sources, and a growing industry of generative engine optimization advises publishers on how to earn these citations. We study what shapes citation in two 90-day collections of answers to commercial questions in contrasting markets: 158,099 answers with 468,037 citations. Comparing pages of the same website, content matters in one way: pages whose title and paragraphs match the questions people ask are cited more, whereas FAQ blocks, structured data, summary boxes and page speed appear effective only across websites. Page properties nevertheless explain little of the variation between pages of a website. Replaying the questions through the engines' APIs shows why. Every page ChatGPT cites is one its own search retrieved, and which pages that search retrieves, for sub-queries the model rewrites on every answer and that rarely coincide with Google's or Bing's results, explains far more of the variation than page properties (32% against 2.8%). Content acts mainly through this step, by matching the engine's queries in meaning. The model's own knowledge shapes it too: ChatGPT writes brands it knows into its searches, prefers websites it knows, and names brands without any supporting page, and controlled experiments show that brand names reorder its rankings of products with identical facts. Pages are thus cited when searched; brands are also named when remembered. The findings replicate in a market of small B2B vendors. We release data and code.



### Faithful Query Resolution for Conversational Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15034v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15034v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 16:19:12+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 16:19:12+00:00
- **Authors**: Saber Zerhoudi, Ivica Kostric, Jelena Mitrovic
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Conversational query rewriting makes context-dependent requests explicit for retrieval, but additional context can introduce an information need the user did not express. This raises a question about the interaction between query fidelity and retrieval breadth. This paper examines that interaction in behavior-adaptive conversational search, using iKAT SCAI 2026 as the evaluation setting. We compare five query-generation configurations within a behavior-adaptive assistant using BM25 and SPLADE retrieval. A minimal-faithful resolver preserves the user's wording and resolves only context-dependent references; weighted reciprocal rank fusion combines its rankings with those from a learned rewriter and the raw utterance. Offline evaluation shows that adding these formulations recovers recall over the faithful query alone, with a slight reduction in nDCG@10. Across our five iKAT SCAI 2026 submissions, the weighted configuration obtains the highest mean retrieval scores, reaching nDCG@5 of 0.534, a 5.3\% relative improvement over the strongest alternative. However, this retrieval advantage does not coincide with the highest conversational satisfaction scores. These findings motivate separating faithful query interpretation from retrieval expansion, while assessing their effects on conversational utility independently.



### MoSAIC: Learning DAG Scheduling Heuristics from Optimal Schedules through Motif-Aware Recovery and LLM-Guided Reasoning

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15205v1)

- **Authors**: Shiva Ahir, Hashmath Shaik, Alex Doboli, Nadeem Yaseen, Simona Doboli
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Efficient task scheduling in heterogeneous computing systems remains a fundamental challenge in embedded and high-level synthesis flows. This paper presents MoSAIC (Motif-aware Scheduling with AI-driven Cognition), a learning-based framework that bridges the gap between exact and heuristic scheduling approaches for DAG-based applications. The system uses the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to learn the relationships between DAG structures and scheduling priority functions. The framework first leverages the OR-Tools CP-SAT solver [4] to generate optimal schedules, which are then used to supervise the learning of compact priority-based scheduling functions through Bayesian Optimization. Experimental results across synthetic graph families and real-world benchmarks, including PolyBench and MachSuite, show that the learned heuristics consistently improve over classical approaches such as HEFT [2], while maintaining low runtime overhead. The results indicate that combining optimal supervision with learning-based generalization provides a practical approach for scalable and adaptive task scheduling.



### PANINI-DR: Controlled Deep Research through Generative Semantic Workspaces
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15208v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15208v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-10 22:42:08+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-10 22:42:08+00:00
- **Authors**: M. Yigit Turali, Shreyas Rajesh, M. Efe Lorasdagi, Chenda Duan, Kartik Sharma, Vwani Roychowdhury
- **Comment**: Under review at ICLR 2027. 37 pages, 1 figure, 22 tables. M. Yigit Turali and Shreyas Rajesh contributed equally
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Deep research requires connecting multiple steps of search, reasoning, and computation, with intermediate findings guiding what to investigate next. As investigations grow, agents must maintain research state across model calls and check findings before errors propagate into subsequent work. We introduce PANINI-DR, a multi-agent harness that separates coordination of the overall investigation from the research and verification of individual subquestions. A shared workspace, built on the Generative Semantic Workspace representation, records subquestions, their dependencies, findings, and supporting evidence. Researchers investigate individual subquestions, while a separate verification agent checks proposed answers against those subquestions and their evidence. Verification feedback guides further investigation, and recorded findings inform subsequent assignments. We evaluate PANINI-DR on BrowseComp-Plus, FRAMES, and MoNaCo against seven state-of-the-art deep-research harnesses, all using a shared language-model backbone and frozen corpora. PANINI-DR achieves the strongest average performance across the three benchmarks. On BrowseComp-Plus, it achieves 73.3% accuracy, outperforming the next-best harness by 16.0 points. Its advantage is strongest on hard questions, where it answers nearly twice as many as the next-best harness. Ablations further show that structured planning and persistent state improve performance, as does checking each finding against its cited evidence before it is reused. Because a model can answer correctly from memory alone, we also analyze the evidence retained by each harness. PANINI-DR achieves the highest answer recoverability from saved evidence on all three benchmarks, while complementary claim-support audits show stronger evidence-backed answers. Accounting for this evidence changes how harnesses compare and further strengthens PANINI-DR's advantage.
