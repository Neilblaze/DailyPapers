### Retrieving Complementary Evidence for Multi-Hop RAG with Semantic Gain Graphs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15285v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15285v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 02:30:20+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 02:30:20+00:00
- **Authors**: Zeliang Li, Xiaofen Xing, Kailing Guo, Xiangmin Xu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-hop retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) requires finding documents whose evidence is useful in combination. Query relevance and graph connectivity help locate related content, but neither directly measures the additional information one document contributes given another. We introduce SG$^2$RAG, a framework that represents this complementarity through semantic gain. Offline, a large language model assesses the relevant and nonredundant information contributed by one chunk given another within neighborhoods proposed by semantic similarity. These ordered assessments are aggregated into symmetric edge weights to construct a reusable Semantic Gain Graph. Online, controlled Personalized PageRank starts from query-relevant seeds and ranks complementary evidence beyond the initial retrieval set. Experiments on MuSiQue, 2Wiki, and HotpotQA with two language model backbones show that SG$^2$RAG achieves the best average EM, F1, and Recall@10 among the evaluated single-retrieval methods. Under identical candidate support and propagation, semantic gain improves average F1 and Recall@10 over similarity weighting by 4.08 and 4.31 points. Analyses of supporting documents and improvements in three iterative retrieval frameworks further support its effectiveness as a reusable retrieval component.



### Understanding ChatGPT Citations: Evidence from Production Web Search Across Model Generations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15382v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15382v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 06:20:46+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 06:20:46+00:00
- **Authors**: Yunxuan Jiang, Shiqi Zhang, Anqi Xing, Xiangyu Chang, Xiaokui Xiao
- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative engines such as ChatGPT Web retrieve webpages to answer user queries, but only a subset are shown as citations. Understanding this retrieval-to-citation process is important for generative engine optimization (GEO), which aims to improve content visibility in generative-engine responses. However, prior studies mainly analyze final responses, leaving the internal execution process and its variation across model settings less understood. To this end, we conduct an observational study of four ChatGPT Web settings from ChatGPT 5.2 to 5.5, covering Instant and Thinking modes at the URL, hostname, and entity levels. We examine how ChatGPT rewrites queries, retrieves candidate webpages, and selects references. The Instant settings remain mostly single-round, while GPT-5.4 Thinking frequently performs multiple retrieval rounds. Across repeated runs, candidates retrieved more often are also more likely to be cited. We then evaluate Google Search and the OpenAI Responses API as external proxies. Responses API candidates provide broader coverage, while its citations more closely match sources cited by ChatGPT Web. Both proxies match ChatGPT Web better at the hostname and entity levels than at the exact-URL level. Even when combined, they recover only about one quarter of cited URLs. We further test whether these external signals remain predictive when combined. Within the external candidate set, a supervised model combining them reaches AUCs above 0.74 for GPT-5.3 and GPT-5.5 at all three levels. These findings suggest that GEO should distinguish retrieval visibility from citation visibility, account for rewritten queries and run-to-run variation, and consider hostname- and entity-level presence in addition to exact URLs. Differences across settings also suggest that GEO strategies should be re-evaluated when the deployed setting or serving environment changes.



### Toward Explainability in Multimodal Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15484v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15484v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 09:35:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 09:35:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Chen Liang, Jinghui Li, Xiuze Zhou, Zongyue Wang, Yuanguo Lin, Han Yu
- **Comment**: 19 pages, 2 figures, 4 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal recommender systems effectively alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by integrating heterogeneous information, yet complex cross-modal representations and model decision-making processes also introduce new interpretability challenges. Despite the rapid growth of research on the interpretability of multimodal recommendation in recent years, relevant methods and evaluation criteria remain relatively fragmented, lacking a unified conceptual framework. To address this gap, this paper categorizes existing methods into modality explanation and post-hoc explanation based on the timing and manner of explanation generation, and further constructs a progressive system for each category. Building on this foundation, this paper synthesizes four categories of evaluation metrics from both objective quantitative and subjective perceptual perspectives, and reviews relevant datasets along the dimensions of application domain and data source. Through a comprehensive analysis of existing research, we further propose future research directions. This paper aims to establish a unified analytical perspective across multimodal representation, recommendation decision-making, and user preferences, providing a research foundation for building transparent and trustworthy multimodal recommender systems.



### Federated Semantic Residuals for Multimodal Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15505v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15505v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 10:13:22+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 10:13:22+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhiwei Li, Guodong Long, Chengqi Zhang, Jing Jiang
- **Comment**: 23 pages, 14 figures. Includes appendices
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Federated Recommendation Systems (FedRSs) learn collaborative scores from interaction histories on device and rank items by them, so a higher score means a higher rank. Item text and images add semantic information that can reorder the list. Existing Federated Multimodal Recommenders (FedMRs) fuse this content into their models, which can obscure how content reorders the list. We therefore propose the SEmantic Residual Adapter (SERA), which keeps content out of an existing FedRS and adds semantic residual scores to its collaborative scores. Content can then lift an item above another only when its semantic residual score exceeds the other's by more than their collaborative score gap. This score grows with the item's content similarity to the user's interaction history and is capped at a residual bound. We prove that for any content, an item can enter the list only if its collaborative score is within twice this bound of the lowest one in the list. A larger bound lets content lift more items, including irrelevant ones, so the bound trades off how far content reaches against accuracy. With a validated bound, SERA improves all three FedRSs on all six datasets without adding personalized parameters. It outperforms FedMRs in most comparisons and lifts some FedRSs above centralized baselines.



### Pathwise Alignment for Collaborative Filtering: Bilateral Conditional Flow Regularization
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15506v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15506v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 10:13:25+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 10:13:25+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhiwei Li, Guodong Long, Chengqi Zhang, Jing Jiang
- **Comment**: 25 pages, 13 figures. Includes appendices
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Collaborative filtering predicts user-item interactions from implicit feedback. We focus on matrix factorization (MF) trained with the Bayesian Personalized Ranking loss. This loss updates each user embedding only through that user's observed items and sampled negatives, so sparse feedback gives MF few constraints. To constrain each user through other users, we propose Pathwise Alignment for Collaborative Filtering (PACF), which trains MF with one network shared by all training pairs. At points between user and item embeddings, a flow matching term trains the network to predict the user-to-item step, reversed and scaled for sampled negatives. A trajectory consistency term pulls each user's predicted path to end at the observed item. Both terms update the embeddings, so the network is discarded after training, thereby maintaining the efficiency of MF at inference. We prove that a network with bounded sensitivity to its input point accurately fits users who share an item only if their embeddings are close, so these users constrain one another. Against eleven discriminative and generative baselines, PACF achieves the highest performance on all seven datasets. Its relative gains over MF are largest where feedback is sparsest. The shared network thus supplies the missing constraints through other users.



### Rethinking Evidence Modeling: Context-Coupled Graph Learning for Multimodal Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15523v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15523v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 10:42:40+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 10:42:40+00:00
- **Authors**: Mingqiao Zhang, Qiyao Peng, Yumeng Wang, Wenzhe Dong, Hongtao Liu, Jian Yang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multimodal graph recommendation integrates observed interactions with item images and text to learn user preferences. These inputs constitute Semantic-Behavioral Evidence (SBE). Many existing graph methods incorporate collaborative and multimodal semantic relations, yet their aggregation and state updates remain context-separated, limiting the continuous composition of preference evidence across layers and the joint use of multiple sources. To address this limitation, we propose CC-MGRec, a context-coupled graph learning method that organizes SBE within a common graph learning context for cross-relation modeling and compositional propagation. Its context-coupled multimodal graph integrates user-item collaborative relations and item-item semantic relations, coordinating their message scales through joint normalization. Recommendation-grounded semantic calibration uses training behavior to adjust semantic neighbor weights while preserving normalized collaborative propagation coefficients. Collaborative-semantic compositional propagation updates shared node states layer by layer to continuously compose collaborative and multimodal semantic evidence, implicitly forming Preference Evidence Chains (PECs), including collaborative-semantic and visual-textual PECs. Joint execution of relation messages on the same shared node states within each layer preserves propagation outputs while reducing intermediate output processing and fusion overhead. We provide theoretical analysis of the proposed mechanisms. Experiments on three Amazon datasets demonstrate improved recommendation quality and faster inference during graph propagation.



### Local Citation Recommendation Without Graph Signals

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15832v1)

- **Authors**: Benjamin Basseri, Pavlos Protopapas, Kemeng Zhang, Alberto Accomazzi, Stephanie Jarmak, Anna Kelbert
- **Comment**: Accepted at WASP 2026, co-located with AACL-IJCNLP 2026. 16 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables. Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/adsabs/LCRC Code: https://github.com/benjaminb/citeline
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Resolving correct citations can be a time-consuming task for research writers. We address local citation recommendation (LCR), where the goal is to retrieve appropriate references for a given sentence. Web tools generally depend on the existing citation graph, which may bias results toward citation count or more recent publications. LCR research using full paper texts has been limited by the lack of publicly available data. We present a new dataset, the Local Citation Recommendation Corpus (LCRC), containing 14,735 scientific statements derived from astrophysics review papers, each with one or more citation targets whose full text is accessible via API. Using this dataset we systematically compare retrieval methods and develop a citation recommendation system that avoids citation graph bias while achieving 8.0% improvement in Recall@25 and 6.9% improvement in NDCG@25 over dense retrieval baselines without model fine-tuning. To support reproducibility, we make our code and dataset publicly available.



### KhyontekAI at CheckThat! 2026: Multilingual Dense Retrieval and Cross-Encoder Reranking for Scientific Web Claims
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15837v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15837v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 18:39:49+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 18:39:49+00:00
- **Authors**: Bhargab Jyoti Bhuyan, Pritam Deka
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 1 figure, 9 tables. Author version of the paper published in CEUR Workshop Proceedings, Vol. 4283 (CLEF 2026 Working Notes, Jena, Germany)
- **Journal**: CEUR Workshop Proceedings, Vol. 4283, paper 59, 2026
- **Summary**: Scientific claims shared through social media platforms frequently reference research findings without explicitly linking to the corresponding scientific publications. Automatically retrieving the correct scientific source for such claims is a challenging multilingual retrieval problem involving semantic matching, paraphrasing, noisy social media text, and cross-domain language variation. In this work, we present a multilingual two-stage retrieval framework for CLEF 2026 CheckThat! Task 1 on Source Retrieval for Scientific Web Claims. Our system combines fine-tuned dense retrieval models with cross-encoder reranking for improving multilingual scientific document retrieval. We evaluate multiple transformer-based dense retrieval architectures including multilingual-E5, BGE, and Contriever under different metadata configurations. We further investigate multilingual joint training, retrieval depth analysis, metadata enrichment, and language balancing strategies for improving retrieval effectiveness. Experimental results demonstrate that metadata enrichment consistently improves retrieval effectiveness, while fine-tuned cross-encoder reranking provides additional gains in retrieval precision. Our final multilingual system achieves MRR@5 scores of 0.6222 for English, 0.5112 for German, and 0.6254 for French, with an overall average MRR@5 score of 0.5862.



### Personalized LLM Recommendation with Semantic IDs: Autoregressive Beam Search vs. Multi-Token Prediction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15864v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15864v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 19:33:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 19:33:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Sejoon Oh, Fernando Amat Gil, Evan Smothers, Dan Iter, Mark Thornburg, Dawit Mureja Argaw, Sourabh Medapati, Thea Wang, Ria Verma, Qiuling Xu, Harshit Syal, Ehsan Gholami, Moumita Bhattacharya, Ashish Rastogi
- **Comment**: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 PALM Workshop
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large-scale personalization is shifting from embedding-table retrieval to LLM-based recommenders that generate the Semantic ID (SID) of the item to recommend, where each SID is a short sequence of discrete hierarchical content codes. A single instruction-tuned model can then unify retrieval, content understanding, and language, and recommend brand-new titles from their content alone using SID; then, how should the model generate the SID? Traditionally, models rely on a single language modeling head that generates tokens autoregressively, typically using constrained beam search. In contrast, an emerging body of work explores multi-token prediction (MTP), where dedicated heads predict all tokens in parallel, substantially reducing the decoding latency associated with beam search. We report an industry-scale, apples-to-apples comparison of these two generation paradigms: single-head autoregressive decoding vs. MTP. We introduce a new auxiliary ranking loss, defined over the joint SID score, to train the MTP objective. We evaluate the SID training objectives on both quality and training/inference cost, inside a multi-task LLM that jointly does personalized retrieval, content question-answering (QA), and instruction following over a large streaming-media catalog. Our experiments show single-head beam search consistently beats MTP on most product-critical metrics: ranking and content QA, and trains at roughly 2.6x lower dollar cost on Qwen3-8B scale; MTP wins only on raw inference throughput. Around this finding, we detail an efficient training and inference stack and report end-to-end gains over the previous non-SID baseline equipped with an auxiliary ranking head: +77% item cold-start ranking, +22% content-QA accuracy, +2.8x faster training, with language metric regression traced to SID vocabulary expansion. We address the language regression by reinforcement learning post training.
