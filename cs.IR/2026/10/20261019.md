### Knowledge Manifold Optimization for Scientific Discovery
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25662v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25662v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 02:08:41+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19 02:08:41+00:00
- **Authors**: Shigeru Obayashi, Yoshiaki Kawagoe, Kazuhiko Komatsu, Tomonaga Okabe
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: The rapid expansion and specialization of scientific literature make it increasingly difficult to identify not only what is known, but where new and still supportable knowledge may emerge. We introduce Knowledge Manifold Optimization (KMO), a discovery-oriented extension of the Knowledge Manifold framework that treats scientific discovery as a multiobjective search over a continuous, literature-derived knowledge space. Candidate locations are evaluated using four complementary objectives: persistent knowledge-regime boundary strength, band-pass white-space novelty, semanticgradient magnitude, and evidential support. Rather than collapsing these criteria into a single weighted score, KMO identifies Pareto-optimal Supported Knowledge Gaps, defined as corpus-relative unoccupied regions that remain sufficiently supported by surrounding literature. The method is demonstrated on a 99-paper corpus in aerodynamics and aerodynamic design. On a 21 x 21 grid, exact Pareto analysis produced 19 non-dominated points, of which 16 formed the practical front after excluding points with zero white-space score. These solutions exhibited distinct discovery modes, including methodology bridges, aero-structural topology/FSI integration, functional aerodynamic-device integration, and rear-wake/vehicle flow-control integration. A distance-maximization baseline preferentially selected extrapolative regions with substantially lower support and reliability, demonstrating why emptiness alone is insufficient. Representative Pareto locations were converted into auditable evidence packages and then verbalized by a constrained large language model into candidate research hypotheses. The results show that scientific discovery can be formulated as structured multi-objective search in an explicit knowledge space, while keeping novelty corpus-relative and separating numerical discovery from downstream language generation.

- **Comment**: 35 pages, 11 figures, 19 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Many scientific advances come from cross-pollination, carrying an idea from one field over to a problem in another. Yet retrieving papers across fields remains underexplored, with existing benchmarks confined to a narrow range of fields. We study cross-pollination retrieval, the problem of identifying transferable ideas across research fields, and introduce CPR-Dataset, a large-scale dataset constructed by mining cross-topic citations. Its evaluation split CPR-eval has 1,300 queries in 26 fields, and its training split CPR-train has 10,573 pairs. On CPR-eval, the best of eleven existing retrievers reaches a Recall@10 of only 0.26, whereas an LLM reranker reaches 0.81. Standard contrastive fine-tuning on CPR-train raises Recall@10 to as high as 0.70. The gain carries over to domains held out from training and to analogy and reasoning-intensive retrieval. On AnaloBench, it raises nDCG@10 by up to 33% over the untrained retriever, whereas training on three existing retrieval datasets lowers it. On BRIGHT, it raises nDCG@10 by up to 70%, more than twice the gain from those datasets. CPR-Dataset is available at https://huggingface.co/CPR-Dataset, and the code at https://github.com/SeokwonJung-Jay/Cross-Pollination-Retrieval.

- **Summary**: With the rapid growth of online video consumption, video advertising has become a dominant component of digital advertising. ### Learning Complementary Agent Groups via Task Hypergraphs for Next-POI Recommendation with Missing Modalities
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25801v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25801v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 05:12:53+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19 05:12:53+00:00
- **Authors**: Jinze Wang, Yuze Liu, Liang Yuan, Gaoyuan Du, Xiaohang Xu, Yong Xiang, Jiong Jin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Next point-of-interest (POI) recommendation uses visit histories and POI content, but text and images are often missing. Large language model agents have been applied to recommender systems for preference modeling and evidence acquisition. The challenge is to select agents that can jointly analyze the available evidence for each request. We propose \textsc{CATH} (\emph{Complementary Agents via Task Hypergraphs}), which learns to select agent groups for tasks such as comparing candidates or analyzing a POI with missing content. Agents with distinct processing roles are the task hypergraph nodes; each hyperedge connects the selected group and records its analysis goal and target POIs. The policy selects members conditioned on the request, task, and agents already selected. During execution, members share candidate-linked results that guide their subsequent operations. Completed tasks update candidate states and agent memories within the request. A shared ranking head converts candidate states into corrections to a fixed base ranking. Supervised ranking trains the executor, followed by policy optimization that accounts for computation cost. Experiments under natural missingness on Gowalla-CA, Foursquare-NYC, and Foursquare-TKY show 8.4--9.1\% relative NDCG@20 gains over HyperAgent4POI, the strongest prior baseline evaluated. Additional analyses compare group-selection and execution designs, test ranking under additional masking, and characterize quality--cost tradeoffs.



### OneChord: Distill What You Rank, Recommend in One Pass
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25805v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25805v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 05:16:36+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19 05:16:36+00:00
- **Authors**: Zesheng Li, Chengchang Pan, Honggang Qi
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Accurate product discovery on the Web requires recommendation models that rank catalog candidates within tight inference budgets. In semantic-ID recommendation, deployment can fuse code probabilities with item similarities, making the final ranking depend on their normalization and relative weights. This motivates a direct training principle: \emph{distill what you rank}. We present OneChord, which uses one learned, catalog-normalized fusion for next-item supervision, refinement distillation, and inference. A shared parallel decoder produces joint SID evidence and item-matching scores. During training, a frozen denoiser supplies legal partial SIDs; the student evaluates these states to construct refined fused targets. Matching direct and refined predictions on shared hard candidates transfers refinement into one-pass recommendation. We characterize the fusion weights induced by candidate conditioning and establish sufficient conditions for preserving the refined predictor's full-catalog Top-$K$ selection. Across six Amazon datasets, OneChord achieves the highest Recall@5 and NDCG@5 among the compared methods on every dataset, with average relative gains of 6.30\% and 7.12\% over the strongest baseline. It also achieves a 1.04--1.18$\times$ full-test generation speedup over the fastest baseline. OneChord makes the served ranking distribution the common object of recommendation learning and distillation.



### Multimodal Decoupling for Diversified Recommendation through Semantic and Structural Shaping
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26024v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26024v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 08:46:34+00:00

- **Authors**: Changran He, Jiaming Wei, Jianghong Ma, Tianjun Wei, Haijun Zhang, Xiaofeng Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recommender systems often suffer from over-concentrated results when optimized for accuracy, limiting recommendation diversity. While diversity has become an important objective, existing methods still face fundamental limitations from both semantic and structural perspectives. On the one hand, multimodal recommender systems provide rich semantic signals, yet are often treated as auxiliary to ID embeddings, leading to ineffective semantic utilization for diversity. On the other hand, the accuracy--diversity trade-off persists during training, where accuracy gains often come at the cost of diversity. We show that this phenomenon is fundamentally governed by both the structural properties of learned representations and the dynamics of optimization. To address these challenges, we propose MOSS, a unified framework for multimodal decoupling through semantic and structural shaping. Specifically, we first introduce an MLLM-based Decoupled Feature Extraction module to disentangle multimodal content into informative representations. To enhance semantic utilization, we design a Category-aware Embedding Selection module that extracts compact, category-aware semantic features. To address structural limitations, we propose two complementary modules: Alternating Dual-Graph Propagation, which alleviates representation collapse by integrating collaborative and multimodal propagation, and Entropy-based Diversity Regularization, which mitigates optimization bias by promoting balanced category exposure. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that MOSS consistently achieves a superior accuracy--diversity trade-off over strong baselines.

### Don't Rewrite the SID Map: Topology Preservation for Generative Retrieval in Dynamic E-Commerce Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26105v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26105v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 10:01:25+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19 10:01:25+00:00
- **Authors**: Jianbo Zhu, Xing Fang, Xiaopeng Chu, Mingmin Jin, Jing Wang, Bokang Wang, Jiaqi Ye, Xiang Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Generative retrieval assigns each item a discrete semantic identifier (SID) and retrieves items by autoregressively generating SID sequences. In dynamic environments such as e-commerce, new items arrive continuously, requiring periodic updates to the SID-to-Item mapping and model to maintain retrieval timeliness. Each update, however, can invalidate search paths already learned by the retriever, and the quantitative relationship between mapping change and retrieval quality has not been systematically characterized. We study this problem with extensive controlled experiments over 67 days of large-scale e-commerce search logs and obtain three core findings. (1) SID semantic drift causes severe retrieval degradation that may be masked by large beam sizes: mapping updates reduce HitRate by 41.78-62.05% at beam size 1 but only 8.33-10.85% at beam size 1000, as redundant candidate generation conceals the true severity of drift. (2) The fraction of changed SIDs is strictly monotonically related to retrieval quality: as the remapped fraction increases from 0% to 72.75%, HitRate decreases monotonically, dropping up to 42.66% at beam size 1. Full remapping remains substantially worse than the preservation policy under warm-start training, indicating that short-budget continued training alone cannot repair topology disruption. (3) The SID topology-preserving principle makes even the simplest warm-start strategy highly effective: fixing old-item SIDs, inserting new-items, and warm-starting from the previous model improves old-item HitRate by 10% and new-item HitRate by 109%, validated across two non-overlapping update cycles. These findings distill into a concise deployment principle: insert new-items, freeze old-items; warm-start when possible, otherwise freeze; never change SIDs the model has already learned. A three-week online A/B test on TmallAPP shows UCTCVR +0.29% and GMV +1.16%.



### FLMNet: Self-Ensemble Distillation from Masks to Seeds for Single-Tower CTR Prediction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26143v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26143v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-19 10:27:04+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19 10:27:04+00:00
- **Authors**: Yifan Wang, Yifei Du
- **Comment**: 44 pages, 10 figures, 15 tables. Code: https://github.com/ivanna10240823-rgb/flmnet
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Click-through rate (CTR) prediction underpins recommender systems and online advertising, where high-dimensional categorical features interact in combinatorially many ways. Several recent CTR models, such as FinalNet and QNN, report higher accuracy when a network also learns from its own averaged predictions, for example by running the same network under different dropout patterns and training each run toward the average. Why this self-distillation helps, and whether its random sampling is needed, remains unclear. This work shows that as the number of dropout patterns grows without bound, the objective reduces exactly to the label loss of the logit-averaged prediction plus a penalty on how much the dropout copies of the network disagree. When dropout does not affect how a network gates its features, this penalty can be approximated in closed form, and FLMNet is designed on this principle and trains without sampling. Networks trained from different seeds disagree in the same form, and distilling several of them into one network recovers part of their ensemble gain. On five benchmarks against seventeen baselines, the resulting models take the top two places in accuracy with a single network at inference, and the gains of the penalty and of distillation, where measurable, largely add up.
