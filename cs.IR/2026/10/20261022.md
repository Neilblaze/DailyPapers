### Better Reasoning, Not Just Bigger Models: A Structure-Grounded Framework for Efficient and Reliable Inference
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30611v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30611v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 02:20:21+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 02:20:21+00:00
- **Authors**: Amirhossein Aghaei, Liangyan Li, Shiyi Yang, Shaohua Wang, Yan Wang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Scaling large language models has substantially improved linguistic fluency, yet reliable autonomous agents require more than fluent generation. In multi-turn inference, current agentic systems often produce semantically plausible but structurally ungrounded reasoning, especially under incomplete observations. We propose Structure-Grounded Uncertainty Reduction (SGUR), a training-free framework that complements token-level generation with active uncertainty reduction over structured hypothesis spaces. SGUR represents incomplete observations as a query graph and aligns them with candidate hypothesis graphs through a prior-weighted partial graph matching mechanism, where database-derived empirical priors assign greater importance to decision-relevant relations rather than treating all graph edges uniformly. The resulting inference state is iteratively refined by an active information-gain strategy that selects the next most informative node to query, systematically pruning the candidate space. We instantiate SGUR on a challenging PrimeKG-derived sequential medical diagnosis benchmark, demonstrating that structural grounding can partially substitute for model scale: starting from only two observed symptoms, SGUR achieves 92.5% Hit@3, outperforming the strongest Graph-RAG baseline by 9.4 percentage points while using approximately 5x fewer tokens. Notably, SGUR enables smaller backbone models to match or exceed the performance of models up to 10x larger, suggesting that structural grounding provides a more robust and efficient paradigm for scaling agent performance than relying exclusively on increasing model size.



### The Fusion Tax: A Diagnostic Analysis of When Hybrid Retrieval Hurts
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612v1
- **DOI**: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3743502
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30612v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 02:24:08+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 02:24:08+00:00
- **Authors**: Aaditya Chauhan, Arun Agarwal
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: IEEE Access, vol. 14, pp. 159620-159637, 2026
- **Summary**: Hybrid retrieval, which fuses a sparse lexical retriever with a dense neural one, is widely recommended in the software documentation surveyed in September 2026, usually with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), on the strength of aggregate gains. We ask what fusion costs on individual queries, treating "do not fuse" as a first-class outcome, in the setting in which hybrid retrieval is deployed: a standard Anserini BM25, five dense encoders spanning 22M-596M parameters and three model generations, a learned-sparse (SPLADE) alternative, and a cross-encoder second stage, on seven BEIR datasets and three fusion functions. Even the best method, dense-weighted score interpolation, ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13-48% of queries and below the deployable dense-only retriever on 8-21% (14-48% and 11-25% at a weight selected under leave-one-corpus-out validation), while beating dense-only in aggregate on every corpus with the primary encoder; the downside is significant under loss-averse risk-sensitive evaluation on six of the seven corpora. We call this loss the fusion tax. It is mostly a ranking effect that preserves recall in aggregate, it is most likely on the queries that only one retriever covers, and it persists across the encoder ladder and the SPLADE substitution. RRF carries the higher tax wherever the dense retriever is clearly stronger and only beats dense-only where the two components are balanced. Fusion failure is weakly predictable within a corpus but transfers unreliably across corpora, because the features that predict it change sign with the sparse-dense balance. A cross-encoder recovers part of the tax only where it is itself reliable. We distill these findings into guidance that keys the fusion decision to the measured sparse-dense balance of the target corpus.



### Compiling Autoregressive LLM Control into Policy-Exact Parallel Inference for Hierarchical RAG
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30664v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30664v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 03:25:21+00:00

- **Authors**: Zehao Lin, Qianfeng Shen, Xixuan Hao, Shaobo Cui, Chunyu Li, Kai Chen, Bo Tang, Zhenjun Wang, Jin Yan, Zhiyu Li, Feiyu Xiong
- **Comment**: 5 pages, 2 figures, 1 table
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models increasingly act as controllers in structured decision pipelines, yet their decisions are still executed by autoregressive text decoding. Hierarchical RAG is one such setting: at each frontier of a document tree, an LLM commits to $b$ of $k$ sections under an input-dependent action grammar, executed as either failure-prone free-form JSON or reliable but strictly serial grammar-constrained generation. We show that this serialization is unnecessary for bounded-horizon control: the reachable future forms a shallow action-prefix tree, which we compile --- one root prefill plus one tree-causal probe evaluate every prefix-conditioned action distribution with serial decoding's tokens, positions, and causal ancestry. A prefix-state lemma makes compiled control policy-exact in exact arithmetic: the same policy with a two-forward critical path and zero output tokens. One further probe pass certifies the exact order-marginalized MAP set. Across four document-disjoint splits and two scales on the primary MetaX device, compiled control matches the serial policy on $84$--$89\%$ of questions --- every instrumented divergence a floating-point near-tie --- and cuts control latency by $35$--$47\%$ versus token-wise serial decoding and $6$--$7\%$ versus an idealized chunked engine; an H800 repetition preserves the ordering.



### OneLatent: Latent Reasoning for Efficient Foundation Recommendation Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30675v1

- **Authors**: Hao Jiang, Peiru Du, Pengfei Yao, Mengting Li, Siyuan Lou, Kuo Cai, Sheng Yu, Qiang Luo, Jian Liang, Ruiming Tang, Fei Pan, Peng Jiang, Wenwu Ou
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong reasoning capabilities, motivating their use as the backbone of foundation recommendation models (FRMs). Existing methods enhance recommendations through explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning under a Think-then-Answer paradigm. However, explicit CoT incurs substantial inference overhead by generating lengthy reasoning traces and relies on manually designed templates that struggle to capture diverse, dynamic user interests. We propose OneLatent, an efficient latent reasoning framework that compresses explicit reasoning traces into several learnable latent tokens, enabling Latent-Reason-then-Answer inference without generating verbose traces. OneLatent first introduces Multi-View Adaptive CoT (MV-ACoT), which creates diverse, high-quality teacher-generated supervision by exploring user interests from multiple perspectives and automatically adapting reasoning complexity to each instance. Building on pretrained FRMs, it then uses a three-stage latent-token alignment paradigm to progressively internalize CoT traces into learnable latent tokens. Finally, a multistage curriculum-based post-training strategy activates latent-token reasoning for downstream recommendation tasks. Experiments on an industrial-scale Kuaishou dataset and the public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark show that OneLatent consistently outperforms explicit CoT-based methods and traditional baselines. Compared with the Think and No-Think variants of FRMs, OneLatent improves SID@64 by 17.44% and 9.33%, respectively, while achieving over 17x higher online inference throughput. We further develop a production serving system for scalable, real-time FRM inference. An online A/B test in Kuaishou's local-services advertising scenario shows that deploying OneLatent with this system yields an estimated 9.6% revenue lift over strong online baselines, including OneRec and OneReason.



### Lychee: Certified Screening of Inverted Lists for High-Recall Vector Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30697v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30697v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-22 04:10:13+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-22 04:10:13+00:00
- **Authors**: Dongfang Zhao
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 12 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Nearest-neighbor search over embedding vectors matches faces against watchlists, transactions against known fraud patterns, and medical images against confirmed cases, where a missed neighbor is a missed threat, fraud, or diagnosis. Because an approximate index cannot report which queries lost a true neighbor, these applications require recall of 0.999 to 0.9999, at a cost far below that of exact search over billions of vectors. Inverted-file indexes reach high recall by probing more lists, but they read and score every probed vector at full precision, so that throughput falls in inverse proportion to the number of probed lists. Graph indexes saturate below these targets, whereas quantized lists must rerank their candidates with the full vectors. Our insight is to decide for almost every probed vector that it cannot enter the top k of the probed lists without reading the vector, through the certified lower bound of exact search by certified exclusion. This paper turns this insight into Lychee, a GPU inverted-file search that screens the probed lists on tensor cores under a threshold from anchors of the same lists and computes exact distances only for the few survivors. We prove that Lychee returns exactly the k nearest vectors of the probed lists, the answer of an inverted-file search at full precision, while it reads a few hundred full vectors per query regardless of the number of probed lists. On eleven datasets from one million to one billion vectors and against ten GPU and CPU baselines, Lychee is 9.7 to 117 times faster than IVF-Flat at equal recall with 200 probed lists on datasets of up to 100 million vectors. It also reaches recall levels that CAGRA and HNSW do not reach and outruns exact search by certified exclusion by 5 to 18 times above ten million vectors. On four GPUs, Lychee searches one billion vectors at recall 0.9999, 44.5 times faster than IVF-Flat on BIGANN-1B.

### Bezier-Guided Adaptive Flow for Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30825v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30825v1)

- **Authors**: Ziang Lu, Yu Zhang, Xinzhe Jiang, Lei Sang, Yiwen Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Cross-domain sequential recommendation aims to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by transferring useful preference knowledge across domains. However, users exhibit varying degrees of preference consistency across domains, while existing methods often rely on global alignment objectives or coarse-grained transfer strategies. Such approaches may introduce irrelevant information and provide limited flexibility in adapting semantic representations to individual user preferences. To address these challenges, we propose BGAFlow, a personalized framework that combines state-adaptive semantic evolution with user-specific transfer control. BGAFlow first establishes a shared semantic space through unified dual-domain pretraining with user profiles inferred by a large language model (LLM). Based on latent interest overlap, it then estimates a personalized horizon to determine the appropriate transfer extent for each user. To enable semantic adaptation, BGAFlow constructs a conditional Bezier curve as a geometric reference and learns a residual flow that progressively refines the trajectory based on intermediate preference states. The learned flow incorporates dynamic preference feedback to adjust transfer directions, while recommendation supervision and trajectory regularization encourage effective and smooth adaptation. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that BGAFlow consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, validating the benefits of personalized transfer control and state-adaptive semantic evolution.

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: General-purpose embeddings trained on profile similarity often misrank candidates in professional networks, where relevance is inherently complementary rather than symmetric. For example, job seekers usually engage with recruiters rather than peers with similar credentials. Current user recommendation systems are often built on profile similarity or social signals such as shared connections, which are effective in friend-oriented social networks where users tend to connect with similar people or within existing social circles.
