### Sequent-Prover: Training Agents for Formal, Checkable SMT-based Reasoning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15258v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.LO
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15258v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 01:10:19+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 01:10:19+00:00
- **Authors**: Debargha Ganguly, Weicong Chen, Mohsen Hariri, Vikash Singh, Sreehari Sankar, Shouren Wang, Wang Yang, Biyao Zhang, Chaoda Song, Alexander Nemecek, Kai Ye, Osama Zafar, Erman Ayday, Joe Stubbs, Srinivasan Iyengar, Vipin Chaudhary
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We introduce Sequent-Prover, a family of verifier-using language-model agents, and the post-training framework that produces them. Agents reason in an executable SMT-LIB scratchpad: they write complete SMT-LIB programs, query Z3 across turns, inspect solver feedback, repair formalizations, and terminate with an explicit answer. Our recipe, verifier-conditioned trajectory distillation, bootstraps from a noisy teacher proposal policy by sampling multi-turn trajectories, retaining the successful tail that is correct, non-trivial, grounded, and executable, then applying supervised fine-tuning and trajectory-level preference optimization. Because solver success alone does not guarantee semantic faithfulness, we evaluate task accuracy, k-sample reliability, and SMT program quality. Across 37 benchmarks and 7,254 problems per model family, post-training raises the 4B model's per-attempt accuracy from 85.6% to 97.0% and four-sample all-correct reliability (pass^4}) from 64.1% to 91.7%. It also reduces average SMT calls from 4.10 to 2.52 and tokens per attempt from 18.97k to 12.72k. Sequent-Prover agents give answers backed by executable formal artifacts.



### Do correct answers have stable latent knowledge support?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15277v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15277v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 02:16:29+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 02:16:29+00:00
- **Authors**: Chuancheng Shi, Shangze Li, Wenhua Wu, Kun Hu, Fei Shen, Zhiyong Wang, Tat-Seng Chua
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models are typically evaluated in terms of the correctness of their output. However, output correctness alone does not confirm whether the latent knowledge supporting an answer has stable internal representations. We therefore propose latent state stability (LSS) and knowledge topology stability (KTS) to investigate the stability of individual knowledge states and their relational structure, respectively. Specifically, we first separately average the model's internal representations of the same knowledge under each elicitation condition. Using the mean representation under the baseline condition as a reference, we remove shared shifts to obtain corrected representations. We then formulate LSS as the distance between each corrected representation and its reference representation, normalized against the typical distance between knowledge items of the same type. We formulate KTS as the cross-condition consistency of the ordered pairwise representation distances among knowledge items of the same type. Next, we construct EpiStableBench, which comprises 4,411 items spanning factual knowledge and mathematical reasoning, with 76,766 controlled queries. Across 16 LLMs from four families, representation stability varies substantially even among items answered correctly under the base condition. Within models, higher stability generally corresponds to higher accuracy under held-out elicitation conditions; across models, stability does not consistently increase with model size or overall accuracy. Meanwhile, we present LatentDPO to learn a knowledge-editing policy that accounts for the stability of latent knowledge. Experimental results show that integrating LatentDPO with multiple existing editing methods consistently improves editing success and generalization across different expressions.

- **Comment**: Accepted to AACL-IJCNLP 2026 (Main Conference). 19 pages, 1 figure, 11 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have made substantial progress in multilingual question answering, yet their ability to understand low-resource sociolinguistic contexts remains under-explored. We study North Korea as a unique testbed where decades of division have produced distinct differences from South Korea in vocabulary, orthography, norms, and ideological discourse. We introduce LENS (Language and sociocultural Evaluation for North Korean Society), a human-validated benchmark of 1,765 questions evaluating LLMs across three dimensions: linguistic robustness, sociocultural perspective alignment, and domain-specific knowledge. Our results demonstrate that linguistic similarity alone does not guarantee sociocultural or domain-specific understanding, highlighting the critical need for grounded evaluation resources for underrepresented cultural and political contexts.

### From Candidate Tokens to Final Outcomes: Decoding Relative Long-Horizon Candidate Value from Reasoning Model Representations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15412v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15412v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 07:28:04+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 07:28:04+00:00
- **Authors**: Yixin Yang, Yue Wang, Fandong Meng, Zhifang Sui
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large reasoning models generate reasoning trajectories token by token. At a given prefix, multiple next-token candidates may be plausible, yet shape subsequent reasoning and differ in their chances of leading to the correct answer. Whether these differences are reflected in internal representations remains unclear. To study this question, we define relative long-horizon candidate value to quantify within-prefix differences and construct the Reasoning Candidate-Token Value (RCTV) dataset, pairing mathematical reasoning prefixes with plausible next-token candidates and their empirical long-horizon candidate values. Using RCTV, we examine hidden states, multilayer perceptron outputs, and attention outputs at candidate positions with sparse linear probes. Experimental results provide evidence that relative long-horizon candidate value is linearly decodable from these representations at multiple layers. Candidate-value probes can also outperform probability- and embedding-based baselines. Furthermore, a controlled online intervention experiment shows that using the decoded signals to guide token selection during generation can improve the chances of eventual success. Our findings provide evidence that internal representations encode information about relative long-horizon candidate value, offering insights into how local candidate-level representations relate to long-horizon reasoning outcomes.



### Sensitivity or Criterion? Measuring and Moving When Language Models Act
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15415v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15415v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 07:36:38+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 07:36:38+00:00
- **Authors**: Sixun Dong, Yebowen Hu, Ming Yin, Yiran Chen, Fei Liu, Chen Chen
- **Comment**: Project page: https://sixundong.com/projects/flipact
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Before an agent acts, it must decide whether action is warranted. A single accuracy cannot distinguish failure to recognize a warranted action from reluctance to act. Signal-detection theory separates sensitivity from criterion, but criteria estimated from authored binary labels depend on each benchmark's example mix. We introduce FlipAct, a diagnostic of tool use in physical states. A rule over annotated tool properties computes each answer and a graded evidence score, fixing the Yes/No boundary at zero across models. Questions form 2-by-2 quads that hold required knowledge fixed while the state flips. The slope of the fitted Yes-rate curve measures sensitivity; its crossing at one half measures criterion. Across 44 open models, sensitivity rises with size within a family, and its ranking agrees with three of four external when-to-act benchmarks. Models respond to evidence sign rather than magnitude, accepting actions that leave the state no better about as often as helpful ones. When Yes means act, most models are conservative across rephrasings; in some, this partly reflects a preference for No. Criterion is not determined by size. Activation steering shifts criterion with weights fixed; distillation from a model's own steered copy puts the shift into its weights without a significant sensitivity change. The distilled shift transfers to six external benchmarks, including two without Yes/No answers, while criterion-free AUC is preserved on the five benchmarks where it is available. Accuracy improves where models start conservative and declines or remains within error where they start permissive. Project page: https://sixundong.com/projects/flipact



### Hidden in Plain Sight: Advancing Harmful Content Understanding via Perception-Aware Adversarial Self-Play
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15449v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15449v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-11 08:50:07+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-11 08:50:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Yisong Xiao, Benlei Cui, Shiji Zhao, Zonghao Ying, Yaoyuan Zhang, Longtao Huang, Aishan Liu, Hui Xue, Ting Ma, Haiwen Hong, Xianglong Liu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Despite significant advances in language understanding, Large Language Models (LLMs) can still miss meaning hidden in plain sight, failing to identify harmful intent in expressions readily understood by humans. Malicious users exploit this gap by strategically disguising harmful content to evade automated moderation, posing a serious threat to online platform safety. The vulnerability reflects a fundamental cognitive asymmetry between human readers, who integrate contextual, phonetic, and visual cues to decipher the intent behind deliberate obfuscation, and text-based LLMs, which rely primarily on token sequences and have limited exposure to evolving evasions. To bridge this asymmetry, we propose PASP (Perception-Aware Adversarial Self-Play), which combines multi-perceptual views with adversarial self-play to improve understanding of harmful intent as evasions evolve. Specifically, PASP constructs textual, phonetic, and visual views of each input, preserving its original context while exposing pronunciation and glyph cues obscured in token space. During adversarial self-play, an attacker uses defender feedback to generate intent-preserving evasions, while the defender is trained using an intent-invariance reward to understand harmful intent across changing surface forms. Extensive experiments show that PASP reduces adaptive attack success rates by up to 73.71% and 74.49% on ChineseHarm-Bench and EvoHarmBench, respectively, preserves model capabilities with minimal degradation, and effectively interprets real-world evasive expressions in e-commerce.

- **Summary**: Concepts are the basic units through which we navigate the world and communicate meaning. But do you and I really mean the same thing when we think or speak about a concept? ### SARATHI: Decoupled Data-Free Probing and Adaptive Reconstruction for Structured LLM Pruning

- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15514v1)

- **Authors**: Sankar Behera, Dhruv Singh, Satyadev Ahlawat, Yamuna Prasad
- **Comment**: Accepted to EMNLP 2026 Main Conference
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Structured pruning reduces the inference cost of large language models (LLMs) by permanently removing neurons and weight rows, yielding dense, hardware-agnostic speedup. Existing post-training methods couple neuron selection and weight reconstruction through a shared calibration dataset, introducing domain bias and out-of-memory failures at high sparsity. We propose SARATHI, a framework that decouples these two stages. A data-free \emph{NMF Residual Subspace Probe} scores neurons using intrinsic weight geometry, without accessing any calibration data, and carries a formal connection to Robust PCA. \emph{Adaptive OBS} then reconstructs the pruned weights with a localized calibration batch, operating in $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ peak memory per layer and eliminating the global memory bottleneck of coupled methods. ### A Step in the Right Place: Understanding and Optimizing Timestep Placement in Continuous Diffusion Language Models

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Continuous diffusion language models represent an emerging paradigm for language modeling. They generate text in parallel by iteratively refining continuous representations and mapping them back to tokens through a decoder. However, realizing the efficiency advantage of parallel generation requires maintaining generation quality with only a few denoising steps. Even when the number of steps is fixed, their placement along the denoising trajectory remains flexible. In this paper, we demonstrate that changing timestep placement alone can substantially alter the final latent representations and thereby affect the decoded token distributions. Building on these findings, we introduce StepTune, an algorithm that searches for timestep placements that improve generation quality. The search is conducted on a small calibration set and guided by a language-aware objective, with the model and sampling budget fixed. Once calibrated, the timestep placement is fixed and reused across inputs. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements in unconditional generation perplexity and conditional task metrics across all evaluated sampling budgets. In particular, StepTune enables 16-step sampling with a frozen model to outperform its native 64-step baseline on both XSum summarization and WMT14 translation and reduces end-to-end latency by approximately $3.8\times$. These results establish timestep placement as a practical means of improving the quality-efficiency trade-off in continuous diffusion language models.

We report every shortfall and derive design rules tied to measured effects.
