- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLMs are increasingly used to design heuristics for computer systems. However, most synthesis frameworks evaluate candidates by their average performance, which can mask severe regressions on specific workloads. Even adding adversarial training inputs doesn't eliminate these regressions, and percentile objectives (e.g., optimizing for the 95th percentile) overlook how severe regressions can be beyond the selected threshold.   We present TAILOR, a framework that guides LLM synthesis using the Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) of the cost difference between each candidate and an existing baseline on the same workload. ### Agentic Context Management for Multi-Turn Human-AI Conversations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00518v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00518v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-31 01:13:55+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-31 01:13:55+00:00
- **Authors**: Matthew Ho, Chenglong Wang, Baolin Peng, Nebojsa Jojic, Philippe Laban, Michel Galley, Jianfeng Gao, Lianhui Qin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLMs degrade in multi-turn conversation partly because of context pollution: the model's own earlier outputs stay in context and anchor later turns, so an early misreading of the user's intent can persist after the user corrects it. Assistant omission (AO), dropping every assistant message, removes this pollution but fails when interaction is referential: later turns refer back to drafts, decisions, and tool-call results that exist only in assistant turns. We propose Agentic Conversation Context Curation (AC3), a training-free method in which a separate analyzer (1) consolidates the task specification from the user's messages alone, (2) audits the assistant's prior outputs against it, and (3) edits the context, either appending the analysis or discarding the assistant side and rebuilding the context from the specification. A second finding: pollution is contagious. An analyzer that sees the assistant's reasoning while extracting the specification can do worse than no intervention, and instructing it to ignore them did not close the gap, so we exclude assistant turns structurally. On Lost in Conversation (replaying difficulty-selected instances), where AO is an oracle by construction, AC3 closes 55-84% of the gap between full context and AO on three of four tasks and exceeds AO on the fourth, and an LLM that faithfully summarizes the history does not reproduce the gain even with more compute. On real WildChat conversations, a GPT-5-mini judge prefers AC3's next turn over both full context and AO in every configuration of our main table, though this preference depends on the judge; on CollabLLM results are mixed. In stateful tool use (tau2-bench), AO collapses to 0% on every model; AC3 does not collapse, though it falls slightly behind full context. Detector studies show that Reset removes injected pollution but is not span-selective: the gain comes from rebuilding the specification.

- **Comment**: 36 pages, 7 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Whether a policy shifts domains and, by extension, continues, broadens, narrows, or reverses previous policy is of paramount importance to the study of public policy. Finally, we apply our method to China's AI policy (1980-2024) and map the direction of policy change across eras.



### Skill++: Towards Lifelong Agents via Routing-Aware Skill Refinement

- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00544v1)

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language model (LLM) agents extend their capabilities with skills, each with a name, a description, and a body. Under progressive disclosure, an LLM router selects skills by name and description alone and loads only the selected bodies. Skill libraries grow continually as users and agents add new skills. Because the router relies on this metadata, a similar new skill can displace an earlier one, breaking previously correct routing even though the earlier skill and the router are unchanged. This routing interference is aggravated for public skills, whose descriptions may not match their bodies. We formulate the problem as lifelong skill routing, a class-incremental continual learning problem. A solution must keep earlier skills selectable without suppressing new ones. As a library grows to 731 skills over ten sessions, Skill++ selects all required skills for 76.3% of queries, versus 36.4% under the original metadata. Across three benchmarks and five LLM routers, it outperforms all compared approaches, and its gains carry over to the Codex CLI agent harness.

### Conic Certification of Transformer Attention
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00585v1

- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL, math.OC
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00585v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-31 03:28:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-31 03:28:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Masoud Ataei
- **Comment**: 56 pages, 5 figures, 6 tables. Code and data: https://github.com/chimera1001/conic-attention-cert
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Attention is the computational core of large language models, yet the verifiers that bound how far an attention head's output can move under input perturbations give no guarantee on how loose their bounds are. A weak certificate therefore cannot be told apart from a fragile model. We develop a conic programming theory of attention certification that supplies this guarantee. We prove that the difficulty lies not in the softmax but in the bilinear interaction of a varying query with the keys. This interaction makes certification NP-hard over boxes and inapproximable once the attention logits can move by a constant amount. A fixed query or listed token substitutions, by contrast, make the exact worst case efficiently computable. We also show that relaxations that treat a network one operation at a time, linear bound propagation among them, pay on second-order terms a sum of per-operation maxima instead of one coupled maximum. Computational results on GPT-2 indicate that the theory carries over to a trained model. Against exact worst cases with the query fixed, the certificate attains the predicted second-order accuracy. It is tighter than linear bound propagation on every head, and the looseness of bound propagation tracks the predicted per-operation excess.
