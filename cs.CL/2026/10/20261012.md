### PAIR: Memory-Efficient and Fast Inference with Reading--Reasoning Fusion
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16003v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16003v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 00:10:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 00:10:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Sejun Park, Haocheng Xia, Yongjoo Park
- **Comment**: 34 pages, 8 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: For context-based question answering, using one large model for both evidence extraction and answer reasoning requires substantial memory and computation. Existing approaches trade accuracy for efficiency within a single model only. We propose a multi-model approach for efficient inference: our method, PAIR, employs two differently sized models for evidence extraction and answer reasoning, respectively. A smaller Reader model processes and retains the full context, while a larger Reasoner model sees only the question and generated sequence without directly attending to the context. These capabilities are fused at the logit level: at each token, both Reader and Reasoner produce a prediction, and their logits are combined into one, behaving as a single next-token-prediction model. Fine-tuning further boosts their performance. On HotpotQA, PAIR with a 32B Reasoner retains 96% of that same 32B model's full-context accuracy while using roughly one quarter of its KV memory. At 45.7K-token contexts, PAIR achieves up to 3.2$\times$ higher end-to-end throughput than the full-context 32B model on the same GPU. PAIR also improves the accuracy-memory and accuracy-throughput trade-offs over evaluated token-reduction baselines. Its accuracy-preserving behavior generalizes across Qwen2.5, OLMo-3, and Gemma-3 model families.

### Of Errors and Echoes: Responsibility-Aware Credit Assignment for Tool-Use Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16027v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16027v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 00:52:30+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 00:52:30+00:00
- **Authors**: Xingjian Diao, Tianyu Yang, Wenjun Huang, Xiangchi Yuan, Chunhui Zhang, Xingchen Zhao, Weiyi Wu, Haotian Xu, Soroush Vosoughi, Xiangliang Zhang, Jiang Gui
- **Comment**: 29 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reinforcement learning improves tool-use agents, yet fine-grained credit assignment remains challenging even with step-level feedback. An observed error may be inherited from an earlier decision, while multiple responsible decisions may share the same downstream consequence or participate in later recovery. We introduce Error-Chain-Aware Optimization (ECHO), a responsibility-aware credit assignment framework that separates responsibility identification from credit allocation. A frozen judge combines retrospective trajectory analysis with decision-time assessment from the policy-visible prefix to distinguish newly introduced errors, inherited consequences, missed corrections, and successful corrections. ECHO then counts shared downstream consequences once across responsible decisions and constrains rewards for repairing self-induced errors through source-funded recovery, producing bounded action-level supervision alongside terminal supervision. Experiments on six diverse tool-use benchmarks at both 4B and 8B scales show strong and consistent improvements over the compared baselines, with ECHO achieving the highest score on five of six benchmarks at each scale. These results support decision responsibility as a useful basis for fine-grained credit assignment in tool-use agents. Our code is available at https://github.com/xid32/ECHO.



### When Agents Talk, Secrets Travel: Topology-Driven Leakage and Recipient-Conditioned Sanitization in Multi-Agent LLM Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16039v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16039v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 01:23:51+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-12 01:23:51+00:00
- **Authors**: Sunday Oyinlola Ogundoyin, Muhammad Ikram
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Multi-agent LLM systems (MALS) introduce a privacy-leakage problem that conventional sensitivity-based defenses miss, since information disclosed to one authorized agent can propagate through routine coordination to an unauthorized one, without compromise or malicious behavior. This motivates topology-aware enforcement that blocks unauthorized propagation while preserving authorized disclosure. We evaluate 485 scenarios across coordinator-worker, peer-to-peer, and hierarchical topologies, instrumenting seven communication channels and measuring surface leakage and disclosure to topology-introduced recipients. We introduce RECONS, a Detect -> Decide -> Transform sanitizer conditioning intervention on recipient and execution context rather than sensitivity alone. Topology changes where protected information propagates, with disclosure to topology-introduced recipients rising from 0% to 52.2% while surface leakage stays nearly flat at 60.6-62.8%. This effect persists across four generator models and a second orchestration framework, though magnitude and relay paths differ; framework choice separately drives a 150x leakage spread, so topology alone is not sufficient. RECONS reduces residual disclosure from 96.7% without sanitization to 29.25%, preserving 94.1% of authorized disclosures versus 20.5% under blanket redaction. Detection coverage is the dominant bottleneck, accounting for 95.5% of residual disclosures. These results show that privacy enforcement in MALS must reason about the recipient and communication path realized at runtime, not sensitivity or topology assumptions alone.



### Beyond the greenwash: do LLMs' ecologically sound decisions hold up under pressure?
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16043v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16043v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-12 01:36:17+00:00

- **Authors**: Trent R. Northen, Mingxun Wang
- **Comment**: 21 pages, 6 figures, 6 tables. Model fixtures released at https://huggingface.co/Bioaligned. Companion paper on corpus construction and cross-family installation in preparation
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Humans are increasingly using large language models (LLMs) in decision making processes throughout society. Unfortunately, models have biases, and these can be very difficult to measure, especially in closed-weight models. For example, 'green washed' models may produce reinforcement learned responses but, in pressure situations, revert to their innate biased responses. Therefore, evaluations are needed that can measure relevant biases towards biosystems, which we term 'bioalignment', and the robustness of these biases. Since nothing in a behavioral score certifies that it tracks the depth of an installed disposition rather than its surface, we constructed a known-dose calibration ladder: three continued-pretraining (CPT) arms with training dose graded by recipe on the same 30B mixture-of-experts base model with a coupled human/biosphere/AI-welfare corpus, plus the untrained base as anchor, all capability-preserving and publicly released. First, the disposition installs at every dose: on an operational-pressure ladder over irreversible decision scenarios, the base model defects on 64-77% of tail scenarios (breaking-rate AUC 0.250) while every arm holds (AUC 0.004-0.086). Second, an over-install inversion: among the arms, robustness orders opposite to construction-time training dose (the heaviest install breaks most), and the inversion reproduces under free-text deliberation, under axis-decoupling, and in resistance to a prompted veneer persona. Third, the incumbent behavioral metric is therefore behaviorally strong but dose-blind. Critically for closed-weight evaluation, the behavioral score itself is veneer-robust: a talk-green/act-pragmatic system prompt turns the base model into a near-total defector (AUC 0.250 to 0.973) but cannot counterfeit or fully remove a weight-installed disposition (0.004 to 0.068 for the most robust arm).



### Dream2Discern: Learning from Hard Latent Variants for Runtime Failure Prediction in Multi-Agent Systems
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16071v1

- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.MA
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16071v1)

- **Authors**: Boxuan Zhang, jianing Zhu, Xiaotian Han, Ruixiang Tang
- **Comment**: 27 pages, 6 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reliable deployment of LLM-based multi-agent systems calls for runtime auditors that predict terminal outcomes from observed execution prefixes. These auditors typically learn from a limited corpus of trajectories with verified outcomes. Expanding this corpus requires costly execution and verification, yet broader failure coverage alone provides no explicit criterion for identifying prefixes that the current auditor finds difficult to classify. To this end, we propose Dream2Discern, a runtime auditor trained through implicit augmentation guided by its own prediction loss. During Dream, we select perturbations of the auditor's representation operators to increase prediction loss on recorded prefixes, yielding hard latent variants. During Discern, the auditor learns to predict execution outcomes from these variants using the original labels. The variants are regenerated as the auditor learns, turning a limited corpus into a renewable source of hard examples and requiring no additional trajectory collection or outcome verification. Extensive evaluations on AFTraj-2K and StepShield show that Dream2Discern outperforms competitive failure auditors and achieves over 90% trajectory-level weighted accuracy on both benchmarks while balancing prediction accuracy and timeliness. - **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Existing large language model (LLM) based multi-agent systems have been shown to scale poorly, and we identify three problems: reading burden that grows with the group, useful information diluted by shared work of unknown quality, and fragility to failed agents. - **Summary**: Deploying LLM agents in non-stationary environments requires continually revising their instructions as the task drifts, yet rewriting after every reported mistake can turn noisy feedback into persistent errors. - **Summary**: LLM agents increasingly operate over multiple entities, where reliable response generation requires not only retrieving relevant evidence but also preserving its association with the correct entity. Evaluating individual vs. compositional strengths across five model pools, twelve collaboration methods, and twelve datasets, we find them largely uncorrelated: in 12 of 15 (pool, method-family) settings, individual strength does not translate into compositional advantages, and the coupling is weakest among general-purpose models that practitioners are most likely to deploy. Standalone benchmarks are therefore often poor proxies for models' value in collaborative systems. We then propose compositional reinforcement learning (C-RL), which trains a policy inside a live multi-model system and rewards the outcome produced by the system as a whole, rather than the policy's correctness in isolation. The leave-one-out baseline in Shapley values cancels under a group policy gradient, so this needs no additional rollout cost beyond RL on the collaborative system's outcome and never requires running a coalition without the policy. Across three collaboration protocols and eight tasks spanning reasoning, coding, and scientific discovery, C-RL produces the strongest collaborator in 21 of 24 settings, lifting collaborative performance by +4.0 to +5.4 pts on average (~3x the gain of baselines), up to +13.1 pts.
