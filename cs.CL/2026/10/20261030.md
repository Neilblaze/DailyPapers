### MUON-RT Reusing the Teacher's Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41104v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL, stat.ML
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41104v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 00:50:49+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 00:50:49+00:00
- **Authors**: Aryan Das, Vinay Kumar Verma, Aakansha Mishra
- **Comment**: Under Submission
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Self-distillation methods for large language models with an exponential moving average (EMA) teacher already retain a history of student updates. Muon nevertheless stores a separate momentum buffer, increasing persistent training memory. We propose Muon with Reused Teacher (Muon-RT), which reuses the teacher-student displacement to eliminate momentum buffers for hidden weight matrices. We show that this displacement encodes an exponentially weighted history of applied updates and smooths temporally uncorrelated noise under a local model. Muon-RT removes its radial component, normalizes it spectrally, and mixes it with the current gradient after Newton-Schulz (NS) orthogonalization. Under explicit regularity and alignment assumptions, we establish linear contraction of an expected objective-gap bound toward a residual error level. With Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Muon-RT improves average accuracy over AdamW by 2.6 points with 42% less persistent memory, and over momentum Muon by 5.4 points with 27% less persistent memory. With Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) on Olmo-3-7B-Instruct, it improves average accuracy over AdamW by 2.0 points.



### Language Models Show a "Women-Are-Wonderful" Effect in Blame Attribution That is Not Just Leniency
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41116v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41116v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 01:17:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 01:17:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Anika Sharma, Snehalkumar 'Neil' S. Gaikwad, Snigdha Chaturvedi, Shashank Srivastava
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: This paper investigates the Women-Are-Wonderful (WaW) effect in language-model blame attribution: a favorable bias in which women receive more positive evaluations than men. Using 11,270 interpersonal conflicts from r/AmItheAsshole, we construct matched counterfactuals that vary the gender of the person being judged (the original poster) and the person affected (the counter-party). Across four language models, gender changes a minority of verdicts, but those changes consistently favor women. The effect is strongly role-dependent. Making the original poster a woman reduces the odds of blame by 9--52%, while making the counter-party a woman multiplies them by 2.7--9.2X. Counter-party WaW also varies across conflict types, strengthening in communication and relationship conflicts and weakening in family conflicts. Exploratory probes suggest that counter-party gender can shift judgments of sympathy, reasonableness, harm and protection. WaW is therefore not just leniency toward women being judged: models also judge conduct more harshly when it affects a woman.



### HyperRepair: Recovering Forgotten General Capabilities of Fine-Tuned Language Models
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41147v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41147v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 02:09:34+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 02:09:34+00:00
- **Authors**: Junrong Tang, Jianghui Wang, Hao Kong, Yang Lin, Yibo Yang
- **Comment**: 29 pages, 5 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Fine-tuning improves a language model's performance in a target domain but can weaken its general capabilities. Existing methods often face a trade-off between general capabilities and domain specialization, making it difficult to preserve both. We introduce HyperRepair, a framework for recovering general capabilities while retaining domain specialization at only inference cost. To make recovery generalizable across models sharing a pretrained backbone, HyperRepair learns a hypernetwork from repairs trained on source models. The hypernetwork uses activation fingerprints that summarize changes from the pretrained backbone to generate a tailored Repairing Side Net (RSN), whose prompt-conditioned gate applies corrections selectively. This enables recovery on models from unseen adaptation domains without target-domain examples or additional optimization. We evaluate HyperRpair across two model families, 12 LoRA adaptation datasets, and 15 general benchmarks. On the default split, HyperRepair achieves 93.4% of the base models' average general score while retaining 99.5% of the fine-tuned models' average domain score.



### Lineage Is Memory: Parent Inheritance and Feedback Disclosure in Short-Budget LLM-Guided Optimization
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41157v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.SE**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41157v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 02:31:45+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 02:31:45+00:00
- **Authors**: Shanglin Yang, Zhan Shi
- **Comment**: 17 pages, 2 figures, 10 tables. Code, pre-registrations, and 1,750 run logs at https://github.com/shangliy/lineage-is-memory
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Iterative language-model optimizers refine prompts and programs by evaluating a candidate, showing a proposer model a sample of failing cases, and keeping revisions that improve performance. Recent work attributes the limits of this loop to narrow single-round reflection, introducing full-set error clustering, targeted minibatches, or larger proposer models so the optimizer sees more failure modes per step. Yet every existing evaluation tests these mechanisms exclusively inside lineage-preserving search, where each revision edits a surviving parent, leaving the contribution of parent inheritance confounded with the breadth of per-round feedback. Across 1,750 pre-registered runs spanning nine prompt and program synthesis benchmarks, we separate parent inheritance from feedback disclosure under matched evaluation budgets. We find that greedy parent inheritance drives the vast majority of optimization progress, outperforming seed-reset resampling even when seed-reset proposals receive a clustered summary of all failures in every round or use a larger proposer model. While full-set error clustering and diverse failure selection improve single-step rewrites from the seed and accelerate the opening rounds of search, sequential parent inheritance rapidly closes this gap as solved error modes are retained in the candidate and filtered from subsequent failure lists. Moreover, decomposing token expenditure in GEPA reveals that proposer reflection accounts for only a small fraction of total cost whereas validation and minibatch gating consume nearly the entire budget, showing that greedy parent inheritance over raw failures captures the gains of complex reflective loops at a fraction of their compute.



### Context as Infrastructure: Knowledge Compilation for Retrieval in Agentic Systems with Harnesses

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41172v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-30 02:48:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-30 02:48:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhichang Yu, Baining Cao, Sbaaoui Idriss, Qi Wang, Gangqiang Xu, Ling Qin, Xiaoling Jin, An Liu, Shaohao Cai, Xiaoyang Bao, Shengle Chen, Changchang Ding, Pengfei Zhu, Jingxiang Xu, Yifeng Mao, Lu Liu, Hai Jin, Yingfeng Zhang
- **Comment**: 13 pages, 2 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented generation is becoming agentic. A search harness writes queries, reads results, reformulates, and decides when to stop. This largely solves accuracy: a harness with only BM25/grep and MiniMax M3 answers 85% of BrowseComp-Plus and 91% on multi-hop FRAMES in our runs, without compiled or vector indexes. It exposes a context problem: an undisciplined loop repeats queries, rereads documents, and fills the window with unused passages. The missing layer is knowledge compilation: a configurable ingestion pipeline where LLMs enrich documents with context no chunk contains alone--navigation hierarchies with claim-evidence leaves, entity graphs, timelines, mind maps, ontologies, wikis, and memory. Compilation is not a better retriever: under a competent harness, structural artifacts gave no detectable accuracy gain on FRAMES, and a PageIndex-style outline degraded at scale. Other artifacts--ontology, graph, wiki, timeline, mind map, memory--pay off in deployment (governance, integration, onboarding, temporal orientation, continuity) where accuracy benchmarks cannot see them. We describe RAGFlow's pipeline and harness (open source at https://github.com/infiniflow/ragflow), which turns the loop into five disciplined stations and keeps the context entering the model the smallest sufficient set; together they form an agent-native database: a store that hands the agent compiled, governed context instead of a ranked list of passages.

- **Summary**: Stickers are widely used in social media dialogues to express affect, stance, and intent beyond literal text. - **Summary**: Long-horizon LLM agents play an important role in solving complex and sustained tasks, with ever-growing interaction contexts. - **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to generate stories, but these stories struggle to reproduce engaging readerly dynamics, the evolving reader responses that shape narrative experience. To address this challenge, we propose a framework that learns readerly dynamics from high-quality human-written stories and uses them to guide story generation. Instead of directly modeling high-dimensional story content, we train a small LLM to predict future reader responses as low-dimensional trajectories. We then leverage the strong instruction-following capability of a larger, off-the-shelf writer LLM to generate story segments that follow these predicted trajectories within a given story setting. Our framework improves trajectory-based fidelity to human-written stories by 12%-35% while largely preserving story coherence.
