### Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22791v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:07:52+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:07:52+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhepei Wei, Wenlin Yao, Baolin Peng, Danqing Wang, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, Yu Meng, Jianfeng Gao
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language model (LLM) agents have shown promising potential in coding tasks, and their performance is jointly determined by the policy model and the harness that mediates their interaction with the environment. Indeed, harness and policy are mutually reinforcing and evolving only one component is suboptimal---a harness tuned for one checkpoint may not target the failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning remains limited to the trajectories elicited by a fixed harness. In light of this, we propose Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for coding agents that alternates harness and policy optimization, using the evolving policy as its own harness proposer. Specifically, in each round, the current policy first analyzes its own failures and proposes new harness candidates (H-step). The policy is then updated through reinforcement learning (RL) under the selected harness (P-step), and hard tasks identified during training form the next harness search set, keeping harness optimization targeted to the policy's evolving failure modes. Across three coding benchmarks, Self-HPO consistently outperforms harness-only and policy-only baselines, improving Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate from 57.8% to 64.8%, with gains generalizing to out-of-distribution settings. Further analysis shows that both optimized components retain gains even when paired with their base counterparts, while their joint gain exceeds the sum of their individual gains. Self-HPO also improves reliability (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k) over the base agent. Additionally, we show that with a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer, the same framework yields a further performance boost.



### Prune the Leaky Neurons: Mitigating Private Data Leakage in Fine-Tuned LLMs via Joint-Embedding-Guided Pruning
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22792v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22792v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:09:22+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:09:22+00:00
- **Authors**: Mohamed Shaaban, Mohamed Elmahallawy
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on proprietary records enables domain adaptation but introduces two challenges: (i) privacy leakage of memorized private data, such as personally identifiable information (PII), and (ii) balancing private data removal with utility preservation. Existing defenses, including data sanitization and differential privacy, often degrade performance, while privacy-neuron editing lacks a PII-free reference to distinguish identity-specific neurons from those supporting useful text. We propose JEPA-Prune, a post-hoc neuron pruning framework guided by a model trained exclusively on PII-free views. Alongside the fine-tuned private model, JEPA-Prune, trains a public model on surrogate and masked views of the same records using a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), capturing domain knowledge without identifiers. A cross-model representation probe quantifies deviations between private representations of leaked records and this PII-free reference. Integrated gradients and a scale-invariant specificity score then identify PII-responsive neurons, which are pruned without retraining. Experiments show that pruning just 1.6% of neurons reduces PII inference accuracy by up to 7.4x and PII extraction recall by up to 13.9x, while keeping unseen-text perplexity within 16% of that of the unpruned model for most LLMs. In contrast, privacy-neuron editing increases perplexity by at least 26x.



### Causal Latent Workspaces for Looped Transformers
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22796v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22796v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:16:58+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:16:58+00:00
- **Authors**: Ilia Zaznov
- **Comment**: 20 pages, 5 figures. Code and reproducibility materials: https://github.com/izaznov/causal-latent-workspace-transformer
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reusing a Transformer block across depth increases computation without replicating its parameters, but successive passes have no dedicated state through which positions can exchange compact summaries of their causal prefix. We introduce the causal latent workspace Transformer: an anchored, four-round shared-depth architecture in which each position writes gated vectors into prefix-specific latent slots and retrieves a content-addressed summary before the next shared block application. This path complements causal self-attention while the original input embedding remains accessible at every round. Across three byte-level corpora, three seeds, and two matched parameter budgets, the proposed model achieves the best mean held-out perplexity in five of six settings against five Transformer controls. At 101.71M parameters it leads on WikiText-103 raw, enwik8, and TinyStories, reaching perplexities 2.7820, 2.7843, and 1.6823; these improve upon the strongest non-workspace control on each corpus by 1.06%, 0.73%, and 1.01%. All nine seed-labelled comparisons at this budget favor the workspace. At 0.918M parameters it leads on WikiText-103 and TinyStories, whereas a conditioned loop is stronger on enwik8. Architectural ablations show that adding the workspace improves upon the anchored loop in all six settings and support refreshing its causal summaries at each round. Together, these results identify a causal, content-addressable prefix workspace as an effective structural augmentation of looped Transformers.



### Reasoning Reranking with Semantic IDs as the Sole Item Representation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22797v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.IR**, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22797v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:21:47+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:21:47+00:00
- **Authors**: Zhenwei Tang, Jintao Liu, Di Bai, Xidong Wu, Xinwu Cheng
- **Comment**: 30 pages, 3 figures. Code: https://github.com/lilv98/ReRankID ; data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/lilvjosephtang/ReRankID
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Reasoning rerankers write a rationale before ranking a candidate slate, and existing systems read item metadata to do so, even though generative recommenders increasingly represent items as compact Semantic IDs (SIDs). Ranking from SIDs alone has two appeals: SID prompts are far shorter than the matched item text, and although item codes are fixed from content, the backbone learns their token embeddings in part from user-item interaction sequences, so the tokens can carry behavioral signal that item text lacks. That training targets next-item generation, and whether it supports ranking a supplied slate from SIDs alone is untested. We build ReRankID, a controlled testbed in which models reason and rank from a history and slate shown only as SIDs, varying item rendering, history length, and slate size one at a time. On this testbed, such models often copy the input order instead of reranking, and readable metadata does not repair the failure. We compare supervised fine-tuning, rejection-sampled self-training, on-policy distillation, and verifier-based reinforcement learning as post-training methods and find that they elicit the missing behavior. The strongest post-trained model, the 8B model trained with a graded ranking reward, gives the best SID-only ranking and matches strong text-reading LLMs whose prompts are about 20x longer. It ranks above random with no history at all, where a caption-reading reference does not, and it keeps its gains at long histories, where text prompts pass 50k tokens and the caption-reading reference stops improving. In a paired rationale audit, this model argues trade-offs better than that reference but describes items less accurately. The common post-training methods we test thus elicit ranking from SIDs but not faithful item descriptions, which calls for post-training that improves the reasoning itself, not only the ranking it produces.



### Veil-Then-Reveal: Protecting PII Privacy via Reversible Pseudo-Anonymization in Zero-Trust User--LLM Interaction
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22800v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CR**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22800v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:24:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:24:42+00:00
- **Authors**: Mohamed Shaaban, Mohamed Elmahallawy, Thomas E. Carroll
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly process sensitive documents and personal information, yet sending prompts containing personally identifiable information (PII) to remote providers exposes data subjects and organizations to risks from logging, retention, model training, and unintended disclosure. Existing anonymization methods often mask or remove PII, while conventional encryption eliminates the lexical and structural cues needed for effective LLM processing. We present Veil-then-Reveal (VtR), a user-side PII pseudo-anonymization framework for privacy-preserving LLM interaction under a zero-trust threat model. VtR treats remote LLM providers as untrusted and sends only pseudo-anonymized prompts, keeping plaintext PII on the user. VtR first detects PII and replaces it with realistic, same-type, in-vocabulary surrogates before prompts leave the user's device, preserving their linguistic and structural properties. It combines format-preserving encryption (FPE) for structured PII, such as phone and credit-card numbers, with a keyed-hash dictionary and secure vault for semantic PII, such as names, locations, and organizations. Session-specific mappings provide cross-session unlinkability, while user-side restoration reversibly replaces surrogates with their original values in LLM responses. We further introduce a rank-class FPE strategy for dates that preserves format and ambiguity while reducing cipher evaluations. Evaluation on three datasets containing 11,322 annotated PII instances achieves 97.9% detection recall, 95.2% protection of detected values, and 99.9% round trip; 100% of echoed surrogates end-to-end round-trip restoration.

### Watermarkability: Measuring and Improving Language-Model Compatibility with Logit-Based Watermarks
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22810v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.CR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22810v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 01:40:39+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 01:40:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Zewei Deng, Mohamed Seif, Liyan Xie
- **Comment**: 16 pages, 2 figures, 10 tables. Code available at https://github.com/zeweid1221/LLM_Watermarkability
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Language-model watermarks can help identify model-generated text, encode provenance information, and detect post-generation edits. A broad class of logit-based watermarks increases the logits of a designated token set at each decoding step. Because a base model's native probability mass on this set varies across contexts, models, and watermark rules, the same logit bias can yield different adherence rates and increases in perplexity (PPL). We introduce watermarkability to characterize the compatibility between a model and the token sets requested by a watermark. At a target mass $τ$, we quantify this property through a watermarkability cost: the expected minimum KL divergence from the base-model distribution required to assign probability at least $τ$ to the requested set. A lower cost means that the model is more watermarkable for that request distribution. To reduce this cost, we propose a two-stage method. In the first stage, we train a target-set-conditioned low-rank adapter on unions drawn from a 16-way vocabulary partition to increase the requested set's probability mass. In the second stage, a closed-form controller applies the smallest remaining uniform logit bias needed to reach the target mass. We evaluate the method on six watermark schedules using OPT-125M and Qwen3-8B. Across the 12 model-schedule pairs, the pre-adaptation watermarkability cost correlates with the log-PPL increase under a near-hard fixed-bias baseline (pooled Pearson $r=0.929$). For every schedule, at least 99.80% of sampled tokens fall in the target set requested at their decoding step. At this reliability, the adapter and controller reduce average per-schedule prompt-conditional PPL by 46.3% on OPT-125M and 5.2% on Qwen3-8B relative to the fixed-bias baseline.

- **Summary**: Standard autoregressive language models generate text sequentially, but revision requires referring back to tokens in the input context or those already generated. Causal Edit Serialization (CES) is introduced as a framework for expressing such revisions within the same causal token stream. ### The Reliance Hypothesis: Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Rises as Language Resource Level Falls
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22841v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22841v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 02:33:51+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 02:33:51+00:00
- **Authors**: Lucas Resck, Jingyi Sun, Qianli Wang, Isabelle Augenstein, Anna Korhonen
- **Comment**: 21 pages, 10 figures, 16 tables
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) faithfulness matters for monitoring, explaining and safely deploying large language models, yet it has been studied mostly in English. Languages differ in level, the amount of text available to learn from, so models know less in lower-resource languages, which may change how much they rely on their reasoning. To our knowledge, we are the first to relate CoT faithfulness to both resource level and task type (knowledge- or reasoning-intensive). On parallel multiple-choice questions from Multilingual Massive Multitask Language Understanding (MMMLU) in eight languages, faithfulness rises as resource level falls on knowledge-intensive tasks but not on reasoning-intensive ones, a gap that points the same way across four models and several faithfulness measures. We propose, and find support for, the Reliance Hypothesis: models rely more on their reasoning when they cannot recall the answer, as in lower-resource languages on knowledge-intensive tasks. Model confidence partly explains the effect of resource level, but not the knowledge-reasoning gap. Languages in which models are less confident rely more on reasoning, yet less confident answers within a language do not, a Simpson's paradox. In lower-resource languages, models rely on their reasoning for more answers, not more strongly for each. CoT monitoring may therefore be most informative in lower-resource languages, where it is least often validated.



### Uncertainty-Aware Budget Allocation for Efficient LLM Tree Search
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22858v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22858v1)
- **Published**: 2026-10-16 03:03:28+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 03:03:28+00:00
- **Authors**: Jianguo Huang, Zhiyi Lyu, Fuxiang Zhang, Yanchen Deng, Deheng Ye, Bo An
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Tree search enhances multi-step reasoning in large language models (LLMs), yet prevailing methods expand every step with a fixed budget, irrespective of the marginal utility of expansion. We first show, across existing search algorithms, that continuations sampled from a shared intermediate state are largely duplicates and collapse to a few distinct answers. Critically, we find that the semantic dispersion of sibling actions predicts the redundancy of their downstream continuations, permitting a subtree's budget to be fixed before expansion. An information-theoretic analysis motivates this: treating the per-step budget as the Monte Carlo sample size for estimating a step's residual answer entropy, we derive a sufficient budget that shrinks with sibling dispersion. Across mathematical reasoning, logical reasoning, and planning benchmarks, UAA reduces token usage by up to $90\%$ and latency by up to $9\times$ at comparable accuracy, generalizing across four tree search algorithms and both open- and closed-source LLMs.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-16 03:38:04+00:00

- **Comment**: 10 pages, 5 figures, 50-page supplementary material appended
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Language-model agents are increasingly deployed to act on behalf of people in settings where individual decisions shape shared outcomes. Four models contributed approximately an equal share at every positive risk level, and four of the five also contributed when risk was zero, even though contributing could not improve their payoff.
