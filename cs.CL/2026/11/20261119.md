### Enhancing Understanding of Language Models with Varied Explanations
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25314v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25314v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-19 00:30:34+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-19 00:30:34+00:00
- **Authors**: Jingyi Sun
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Language models are increasingly used to support human judgement in complex reasoning tasks, yet their predictions often remain difficult to interpret and validate. Explainability techniques provide insight into model decision-making, helping expose errors, failure modes, and potential bias so users can decide whether to rely on model outputs. Explanations are simplified approximations of a language model's internal workings. The goal is to obtain explanations that reflect the underlying computation as closely as possible (faithfulness), are understandable to users (plausibility), and help them achieve their intended goals (utility). Evaluation protocols are essential for assessing these dimensions. This thesis studies highlight explanations, natural language explanations, and chain-of-thought reasoning. These forms offer complementary perspectives on model behaviour and exhibit distinct strengths and limitations across tasks. Producing explanations that satisfy multiple quality dimensions remains challenging, as does evaluating them reliably across forms and settings. This thesis aims to improve people's understanding of models' decision-making processes in complex reasoning tasks. First, it proposes two evaluation frameworks for assessing highlight explanations across multiple properties and explanation types, together with a meta-evaluation framework for examining the reliability of existing faithfulness metrics for chain-of-thought explanations. Second, it proposes two techniques that generate natural language explanations designed to be faithful to the model's prediction process while remaining concise, coherent, and helpful for understanding model decisions. Finally, a study of human-AI interaction explores the roles of evidence and explanations in helping users validate model verdicts in a fact-checking setting.



### Pro$^2$ Agent: Scientific Problem Probing for Autonomous AI R&D
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25357v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.AI**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25357v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-19 01:52:29+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-19 01:52:29+00:00
- **Authors**: Yixiong Chen, Liqian Peng, Tian Tan, Ziqian Lin, Blake Jianhang Chen, Chao Zhao, Yaojie Liu, Alec Go, Alan Yuille
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Large language model (LLM) agents can now autonomously conduct AI research by writing code and running experiments. They typically do so by iteratively optimizing a codebase, while ignoring the problem structure behind the task. This formulation is effective on simpler tasks, but on difficult problems it often gets stuck in the wrong solution region because the agent never explicitly represents its understanding of the problem. We introduce Pro$^2$ Agent, which shifts the primary state of autonomous AI research from a codebase state to a Problem State: an explicit structure of what problems prevent success and which solutions are likely to address them. Pro$^2$ Agent initializes the Problem State through comprehensive analysis of available resources and constraints. It then performs targeted research to relate the identified problems to candidate solutions. Through controlled hypothesis testing, the agent updates the importance of a suspected problem or the benefit brought by a candidate solution. On the complete MLE-Bench, Pro$^2$ Agent matches the best reported medal rate (70.7%) under the standard 24-hour budget and reaches 76.0% with a 48-hour budget, the highest medal rate reported on the complete benchmark. It also achieves competitive performance on PostTrainBench. In addition, it produces research notes for each task that make the research process transparent and reusable by human researchers.



### Incorporating argumentation frameworks for LLM decision-making: Task-specific design and evaluation

- **Authors**: Bochen Han, Anshu Xiong, Mingyang Ma
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Argumentation frameworks (AFs) provide explicit, formal mechanisms for representing and resolving conflicts and thus have been incorporated into large language model (LLM) decision-making. The benefits vary and depend on how arguments and their relations are constructed, evaluated, and incorporated into the decision process. {We abstract a workflow consisting of four stages based on existing argumentation-augmented methods} and investigate their design choices over tasks of debate-based decision-making and retrieval-based fact verification. Our evaluation with recent LLMs shows that existing methods do not consistently improve performance across the considered tasks. Motivated by this observation, we develop two task-specific methods. These findings demonstrate the value of combining formal argumentation and LLM while highlighting the importance of aligning with tasks in argumentation construction, model selection, and the role of formal semantic reasoning.

### Evolve Where It Matters: Coverage-Guided Self-Evolution of Agent Skills
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25429v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25429v1)

- **Authors**: Boyin Tan, Jiadong Zhang, Xiaosong Ma, Chun Jason Xue, Xiaowei Huang, Youcheng Sun
- **Comment**: 15 pages, 5 figures
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Agent skills package procedural knowledge as natural-language instructions, and a skill helps only when its instructions are correct and properly followed by the agent. Self-evolving agents revise their own skills from the runs they make, but existing methods judge each run as a whole, by its outcome or by where it went wrong, and cannot tell whether a failure comes from a wrong instruction or from one the agent did not follow. Instruction-level coverage was introduced to test skills and labels each instruction of a run as follow, not-follow, or not covered, which separates these causes. We leverage it for skill self-evolution with SkillPlumber, a framework in which an agent evolves its own skills where coverage shows they fall short. Across three benchmarks and three executors, SkillPlumber solves 64.7% to 70.5% of the tasks, 25.6 to 30.4 percentage points higher than the initial skill and 8.7 to 14.0 percentage points higher than the strongest baseline, and removing coverage reduces recovered failures by 20.8% on SkillsBench with GPT-5.5.



### RoCoKV: Accelerating LLM Inference Serving through Uncertainty and Redundancy Aware KV Cache Compression
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25433v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25433v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-19 03:16:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-19 03:16:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Payman Behnam, Elaheh Sobhani, Saad Amin, Yahya Hassan, Daniyal Khan, Biswadeep Chakraborty, Tanish Patwa, Souvik Kundu, Alexey Tumanov
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: KV cache compression is critical for large reasoning models, where most of the cost comes from long generated reasoning traces rather than the input prompt. These traces often contain redundant and low-utility tokens due to looping, self-verification, and corrections. However, such tokens may still receive high attention scores, making it difficult to remove them without harming the reasoning process. We propose RoCoKV, a KV cache compression method designed for large reasoning models. RoCoKV introduces two key components. First, uncertainty-guided adaptive pruning adjusts the compression strength based on the model's confidence, allocating more memory during uncertain phases and applying stronger pruning when the model is confident. Second, redundancy-aware token selection reduces the impact of tokens that are highly similar to recent reasoning content, encouraging the model to retain more informative and diverse tokens. Beyond the algorithm, we co-design RoCoKV with the serving stack: we develop fused three-phase CUDA decode kernels and integrate them into vLLM as a drop-in plugin that preserves its paged KV layout and CUDA graph capture, turning algorithmic savings into real end-to-end speedups. Extensive experiments across three reasoning benchmarks and two reasoning models demonstrate that RoCoKV consistently improves reasoning accuracy under constrained KV cache budgets, achieving higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art KV cache compression methods. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B in vLLM, RoCoKV achieves a 1.27X speedup and 38% higher throughput on AIME25, the most challenging evaluated reasoning benchmark, and a 1.43X speedup and 45% higher throughput on the long synthetic benchmark, compared with Full-KV.

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Omissions are difficult to verify because missing information leaves no output span to inspect: a summary may be factually supported while still excluding a decision, qualification, quantity, or condition needed to preserve the source's meaning. We introduce COVERAGE-TRACE, a source-side counterfactual framework that evaluates omission as dependence on a designated atomic source fact. It replaces only the target fact with type-matched alternatives, holds the candidate summary fixed, and measures the resulting change in its teacher-forced, length-normalized likelihood. A summary covering the target should respond more strongly than one omitting it, while aggregation across multiple counterfactuals reduces sensitivity to replacement choice. Multi-CF achieves 0.764--0.825 AUROC, compared with 0.640 for the strongest evaluated text-only baseline. COVERAGE-TRACE therefore provides a practical, complementary signal of target-specific source--summary dependence, but not a wording-invariant semantic coverage probability. These results establish source-side intervention as a promising approach to omission detection while identifying semantic invariance as the central remaining challenge.

- **Summary**: Recent benchmark evaluations increasingly rely on Large Language Models (LLMs) as automatic judges. This paradigm assumes LLMs evaluate based on the rubric's semantic meaning rather than its numeric scale. We question this assumption by introducing semantic faithfulness: the principle that semantically equivalent rubrics must yield consistent scores. To quantify this, we propose a scale-reversal probe that inverts score anchors without altering evaluation criteria. Furthermore, we introduce floor normalization to calibrate reversal disagreement against intrinsic model variability, enabling fair cross-model comparisons.
