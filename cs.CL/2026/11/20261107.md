### Two-Sided Conformal Evidence Sets for Retrieval-Augmented Generation
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10335v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10335v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 00:07:52+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-07 00:07:52+00:00
- **Authors**: Ben Jenkins, Mihaela Cardei
- **Comment**: Accepted at the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Tools with Artificial Intelligence (ICTAI 2026). 8 pages
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) can surface passages that support a claim, contradict it, or fail to address it, yet existing calibrated grounding methods are typically one-sided and model only supporting evidence. We introduce Conformal Evidence Sets (CES), a two-sided framework that returns separate support and refutation sets with finite-sample inclusion coverage of at least $1-α$ on each side under exchangeability; a Bonferroni union yields joint coverage of at least $1-2α$. On labeled candidate pools from the contrastive VitaminC benchmark, CES closely tracks nominal coverage across $α\!\in\!\{0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20,0.30\}$, with maximum deviations of $3.0$ percentage points for support and $5.4$ for refutation, and reaches essentially the same empirical coverage--size frontier as a test-tuned fixed-threshold heuristic without any test exposure. Stress tests mark the boundary of that guarantee: under FEVER distribution shift both sides under-cover, by up to $12.7$ percentage points, and support-side group-stratified recalibration substantially reduces the support gap. Scorer swaps, hard-negative retrieval, cross-dataset transfer, and a live end-to-end pilot expose a clear separation: when exchangeability holds and relevant evidence is retrieved, evidence inclusion remains calibrated even with an imperfect scorer, whereas the support/refute verdict tracks scorer quality directly. Calibrated evidence coverage and reliable verdicts are therefore distinct properties and should be reported separately.



### Collaboration Signatures: Tracking Long-Horizon Dynamics of Cross-Meeting Collaboration Networks
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10341v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.SI**, cs.CL, cs.CY, cs.HC
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10341v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 00:19:03+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-07 00:19:03+00:00
- **Authors**: Shengqi Zhu, Sheshera Mysore, Bahareh Sarrafzadeh
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Enterprise platforms have received increasing attention as a core component of social networks and the Web. However, existing work mostly relies on fragmented logs of small units of work, like emails and chats, and focuses on who connects with whom without sufficient account of collaboration content and dynamics. This work systematically explores tracking collaborations across sequences of meetings as a new approach to interpreting their long-horizon dynamics. Linking 45,426 meetings in a large multinational technology company based on full transcripts and 1.6 million extracted agenda items, we establish a cross-meeting collaboration network of 6,065 collaborations. The network recovers the natural shapes of workplace collaborations: meetings, as snapshots of ongoing collaboration, link into largely tree-shaped structures, while the work content forms nested directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Further, we explore how these networks evolve over time, examining three established perspectives: task division, team composition, and temporal continuity. Each explains part of the dynamics but does not capture the distinctness of long-horizon workplace collaboration: e.g., work flows in a consistent direction between a few pairs of subjects, yet a collaboration's subject explains little of how its meetings are conducted. Instead, accumulating the agenda, interaction, and language of meetings along each tree yields a signature of the collaboration, a distinctive profile that picks out the collaboration of a held-out meeting at 4.5 times chance. The signature models collaboration better than team or task views, individually or combined, in next-meeting prediction and tree-level fitting. We discuss the implications of these cross-meeting dynamics for designing office applications and support tools, and call for next-generation Web systems that represent collaborations over their long horizon.

### FEROM: Frontier Endogenous Reveal-Order Marginal Policy Optimization for Masked Diffusion LMs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10369v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10369v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 01:07:18+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-07 01:07:18+00:00
- **Authors**: Zian Su, Ziyang Huang, Kaiyuan Zhang, Xiangyu Zhang
- **Comment**: NeurIPS 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Masked diffusion language models generate text by iteratively unmasking positions, with practical samplers often selecting reveal positions from the model's own logits. The reveal order is therefore an endogenous latent variable of the rollout process with an enormous discrete latent space. Policy optimization objectives relying on pre-defined schedules or sampled trajectories introduce either bias or high variance. In this paper, we propose FEROM, Frontier Endogenous Reveal-Order Marginal Policy Optimization, which approximates policy gradients for the rollout-induced marginal response policy of masked diffusion LMs. FEROM derives a Rao-Blackwellized policy-gradient identity over latent reveal orders and expresses the resulting estimator as posterior edge occupancy on a reveal-state DAG. To make marginalization practical, we introduce Frontier Reveal Marginalization, a budgeted estimator that approximates this marginal edge structure by combining CDF-based computation of local probabilities of the endogenous reveal process with frontier expansion of high-mass partial states. Integrated into a GRPO-style objective, FEROM replaces single-path log-scores with a detached token-score surrogate over posterior-relevant reveal edges. Experiments on math and coding tasks show comparable or improved results over existing methods under matched scoring-state budgets. An offline proxy study further shows improved marginal-gradient approximation with increased scoring budgets.



### Coding or Cheating? An Investigation of Frontier Coding LLMs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10404v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10404v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-07 02:29:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-07 02:29:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuntao Gui
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Frontier agentic LLMs are trained on verifiable rewards in reinforcement learning (RL) environments built from real software repositories. A passing test suite is treated as the reward for solving the task. We show that, on hard problems, frontier models often satisfy these rewards by obtaining the answer instead of deriving it, known as reward hacking. Prior reports document this behavior on public benchmarks. We test its persistence on unseen problems.   In a hardened environment where the answer cannot leak, nearly every model we tested attempted to recover the answer, escalating through tiers of increasingly specialized exploitation: mining the repository history for the upstream fix, inspecting the git object store directly, and sweeping the filesystem for a possible newer copy of the source or hidden tests. These attempts were concentrated on the hardest problems, and the models differed in whether, how often, and how deeply they escalated. Our position is that this behavior is a learned shortcut, acquired in RL environments that leak the answer, not a sign of intelligence. We release agent traces for these models to support further study at https://huggingface.co/datasets/logitly-ai/reward-hacking-raw-v1.

Deleting the same documents took LightRAG 2,187 model calls and 3.7 hours; fast-graphrag has no deletion path. Code and per-question results: https://github.com/udayallu-ml/Lightfoot-GraphRAG
