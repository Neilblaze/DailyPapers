### MathaMAGICal: Validating Trajectory Data Attribution During Capability Acquisition
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19692v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19692v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 00:51:17+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 00:51:17+00:00
- **Authors**: Jason Lee, Dikshanta Pokhrel, Phonn Nay Khant, Victor Lee, Moritz Weckbecker, Sebastian Lapuschkin, Wojciech Samek, Mark Riedl, Glenn Matlin
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Influence-function-based methods like MAGIC provide exact first-order scores for trajectory data attribution, yet only retraining indicates whether those scores accurately predict data removal. We evaluate MAGIC on $86$M- and $152$M-parameter GPT-2-style models learning multistep arithmetic from scratch on filtered web text and four synthetic math families. Full-run attribution at $86$M suffers from numerical overflow, and even with metagradient clipping, scores correlate near chance with retraining (linear datamodeling score, LDS, $0.17$--$0.22$). In contrast, short checkpoint windows yield stable, accurate predictions at both scales (LDS $0.86$--$0.99$ across four $100$-step windows at $152$M), whereas a single-checkpoint gradient-dot baseline shows near-zero correlation. Rankings of individual training blocks predict retraining only within that single run and do not transfer across seeds or hyperparameters ($ρ\le 0.09$), whereas format-level patterns replicate across runs. Two data families, worked examples and primitive facts, occupy $2.1$--$2.6\times$ their corpus share in both the helpful and harmful $5\%$ score tails, reflecting strong bidirectional local sensitivity distinct from net benefit under multi-epoch family deletion. These results show that trajectory attribution is reliable within short, validated windows and yields family-level insights that generalize across runs.

- **Summary**: Pretraining data curation decides what a model learns from, yet what makes a document worth learning from depends on what the model already knows. ### Where Should LLM Agents Adapt at Test Time? In Context, in Weights, or Both
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19700v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19700v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 01:07:23+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 01:07:23+00:00
- **Authors**: Parsa Mirtaheri, Mohammadreza Armandpour, Keivan Alizadeh, Iman Mirzadeh, Parshin Shojaee, Aryo Lotfi, David Harrison, Mehrdad Farajtabar
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: LLM agents operating repeatedly in the same environment, such as a code repository, a database, or an application suite, encounter tasks that share latent structure. Experience can reveal this structure, allowing agents to reuse environment knowledge and learn from environmental feedback. Agents can adapt at test-time using this experience through two channels: retaining it in context and updating their weights. These channels are usually studied separately, leaving unclear what each contributes and when they complement each other. We compare in-context learning (ICL), reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards using GRPO, and self-distillation using SDPO, alone and in combination, using a shared online protocol and modular framework. Agents encounter matched task streams, are evaluated on each task before adapting to it, and learn only from their own attempts and environment feedback. We evaluate 14 adaptation mechanisms and a stateless baseline with Qwen3.5-9B on 109 tasks from four environments drawn from SWE-bench Verified, AppWorld, ScienceWorld, and BIRD-Critic-SQLite, using five task orders per environment. ICL provides the largest standalone gain: its best policy raises mean success across environments from 28.8% to 49.4%. Adding GRPO to this policy achieves the highest mean success, 51.6%. While adding GRPO improves the overall mean under every ICL policy, SDPO has mixed effects. Trace analyses show fewer lookups and shorter attempts under ICL, consistent with reusing knowledge of the shared structure. However, ICL also frequently skips inspection and verification for the current task. Adding GRPO to ICL helps restore these behaviors while preserving its reduced environment lookups. These observations suggest that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards can refine how agents use retained experience, motivating the joint study of context management and weight updates.



### StaleBench: Isolating Stale Retrieval in Agent Memory, and Pricing the Fix

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19752v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 03:29:02+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 03:29:02+00:00
- **Authors**: S M Mahbub Murshed
- **Comment**: Accepted to Findings of EACL 2027. 39 pages, 3 figures. Code and data: https://github.com/thenexuslab/stalebench-release
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: An agent that stores facts over time eventually stores several versions of the same fact. Asked a value-agnostic question - "which team does X play for now?" - a similarity retriever cannot prefer the current version, because every version matches the entity the query names. StaleBench isolates this stale-retrieval failure on 892 real updating facts, with currency labels from the source (Wikidata validity years via TempLAMA), never from a retrieval score. Our central result is that retrieval metrics can rank memory policies backwards: a retention policy and its retain-everything control return the identical statement at rank 1 for every test query, yet differ by 29.5 and 65.6 points in reader accuracy on two readers when no date is shown. Withdrawing the lexical anchor that statements carry costs deployed keying mechanisms tens of points of freshness; entity resolution on both the write path and the query recovers most of the loss. Three deployed systems (Mem0, Graphiti, LangMem) and a reimplemented ERASE each handle supersession differently, and no single scoring convention describes all four. We release the data, a frozen evaluator, and reference baselines.

- **Summary**: Dataset cartography argues that training dynamics can reveal regions of a dataset that matter differently for learning: easy-to-learn examples aid optimization, hard-to-learn examples often expose annotation problems, and ambiguous examples contribute strongly to out-of-distribution generalization. A striking implication of the original work is that a model trained on only 33% ambiguous examples can outperform full-data training on several OOD evaluations, including a QNLI reformulation of SQuAD evaluated on Adversarial SQuAD. We revisit this claim in a stricter setting: open-ended extractive question answering, where models must predict answer spans rather than classify question-sentence pairs. These results show that clean-source cartography remains useful for reduced-data selection, but its OOD advantage does not carry over as a general replacement for full-data training in extractive adversarial QA.

- **Summary**: Small language models often fail explicit user requests (length, number of items, format, required or forbidden words, grammatical person, answer language). ### Multi-Branch Transformer
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19800v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CL
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19800v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-15 05:20:54+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-15 05:20:54+00:00
- **Authors**: Gaotang Li, Lingjie Chen, Chunyuan Deng, Zhiwei Xu, Hongzheng Chen, Christopher De Sa, Jingrui He, Yingheng Wang, Hanghang Tong
- **Comment**: Project: https://gaotangli.github.io/project_page/Multi-Branch-Transformer/
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Transformer language models are commonly scaled by making their blocks wider or stacking them deeper. In this work, we explore a complementary path: branching, the parallel composition of full Transformer blocks. We introduce the Multi-Branch Transformer (MBT), which processes the input through shared initial layers, executes independently parameterized branches of sequential blocks in parallel, and fuses their outputs before shared final layers. At comparable training compute, we study three MBT variants: Wide-Shallow preserves block width, Narrow-Deep preserves sequential depth, and Balanced interpolates between them. In controlled pretraining experiments at four mixture-of-experts scales (0.8B to 2.1B total parameters), 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs consistently outperform sequential Transformers under matched parameter and training-compute budgets. Balanced MBTs improve further and retain comparable quality as the number of branches increases. To improve inference efficiency at long contexts, we introduce Joint-KV, which constructs a shared KV cache from branch representations at each depth. Our final recipe combines Balanced MBTs with Joint-KV, supported by an inference engine that exploits branch grouping and shared-cache reuse. Fitted scaling curves indicate that standard Transformers require up to 1.26x as much training body compute to match our final models' validation loss. These models reduce full-model KV-cache storage by up to 34% and deliver up to 51.4% higher decoding throughput on a single H200 GPU. Ablation studies show that branching yields gains beyond changes in width and depth, enables an effective KV-sharing strategy (Joint-KV), remains robust across fusion functions, and performs best in the middle layers. These findings show that branching offers a practical path for scaling language models toward both better modeling quality and faster inference.

- **Summary**: Machine interpreting (MI), or AI-enabled speech translation, is increasingly visible in professional and public-facing communication, yet its performance relative to human simultaneous interpreters remains insufficiently understood.
