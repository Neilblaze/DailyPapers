### Richness Is Not Evenness: Ordered Renyi Crossings under Self-Training
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08892v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08892v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-06 02:31:07+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-06 02:31:07+00:00
- **Authors**: Pranjal Srivastava
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Recursive self-training of language models, a setting studied in model collapse, can reduce vocabulary while making frequent words, mostly function words, less dominant. We fine-tune English GPT-2 on its own output for nine generations, vary the training-data sampling temperature, and measure every model with one fixed sampler. As training temperature rises, Renyi entropy changes turn from loss to gain at distinct temperatures: min-entropy first, then collision entropy, Shannon entropy and observed richness. Between the outer crossings, word types fall while the most-common word becomes less dominant. Untruncated training at T = 1 lies in this interval: at 13,000 words, generation 9 has 24% fewer types than generation 0 yet collision entropy is 0.91 bits higher. The ordering persists with a finer grid, different measurement samplers, a fixed word budget and five fresh training seeds. Min-entropy again crosses before richness in four further settings (in Hindi only at interpolated roots) and in 12 of 13 distinct measurements under eight text representations. Next-token identities and a categorical simulation isolate decoding and sampling effects; the crossing order remains empirical. Fine-tuning on human text can produce the same split while likelihood improves, and no single order's sign reliably identifies worse likelihood across the tested settings. We recommend reporting entropy profiles at stated budgets with an independent quality measure.

- **Summary**: As language agents tackle increasingly complex tasks, they increasingly rely on multiple agents to coordinate specialized work, requiring decisions not only about what each subagent should do but also which tools and workspace resources it should access. ### On the Incompleteness of do-calculus for Universal Identifiability
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08916v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.LG, math.PR
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08916v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-06 02:59:26+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-06 02:59:26+00:00
- **Authors**: Yaroslav Kivva, Aliaksandra Novik, Negar Kiyavash
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: We study the problem of causal effect identification from an arbitrary set of observational and/or interventional distributions, which we call Universal Identifiability (uID). The uID problem generalizes existing classical identifiability (ID) and general identifiability (gID) problems, which considered the identification problem from either mere observational distributions or a specific type of interventional distributions. Prior research has established graphical criteria to determine when a causal effect is identifiable in ID and gID problems. Moreover, algorithms that leverage do-calculus and basic probability manipulations have been proposed to compute these effects. Consequently, it has been shown that do-calculus is both sound and complete in these problems. Despite these well-established results, a formal definition of what it means for the do-calculus to be complete does not exist. We propose a rigorous formalization for the completeness of the do-calculus and show that the completeness results in previous work remain valid. However, for more general problems, such as uID, the rules of do-calculus are no longer complete. This is perhaps the reason why the uID problem has remained open thus far.

- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Quantization-aware training (QAT) is the most reliable way to obtain accurate low-bit large language models (LLMs), but training with AdamW keeps two dense FP32 states for every weight matrix. We find that low-rank memory-efficient optimizers such as GaLore and LDAdam degrade substantially in QAT. A considerable part of the QAT momentum lies outside its top-r singular subspace, and projection-based methods discard this part at every step. It reduces optimizer-state memory by 71% to 74% relative to AdamW, stays close to AdamW in most settings, and lowers training throughput by less than 2%.

- **Summary**: Multi-agent LLM systems increasingly aggregate judgments across model instances, but reasoning about other agents can itself introduce failure. - **Comment**: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. The source code and trained weights are available at \url{https://github.com/visresearch/FPRT}
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Training smaller language models on reasoning solutions generated by a larger teacher model is the prevailing approach for transferring mathematical reasoning capabilities. However, the student tends to overfit the output distribution of the teacher and generalizes poorly when its own behavior at inference time deviates from the training data. Recent methods attempt to bridge this gap by incorporating error data, yet the corrective trajectories still reflect the reasoning patterns of the teacher, which the student cannot reliably reproduce, leaving the distribution mismatch largely unresolved. We propose a method, Failure Profiling and Reachable Trajectory Selection (FPRT), built on the insight that effective training data should be jointly determined by what the student fails on and what it can realistically learn. Our method first profiles the failure behavior of the student on each problem through repeated sampling, characterizing it in terms of error rate and error consistency to reveal qualitatively distinct failure modes. Among the correct candidates, our method selects the one whose initial reasoning steps after the point of divergence are most reproducible by the student. These first corrective steps constitute the primary bottleneck, while the remaining steps follow an already corrected context and can therefore be continued more readily. This contrasts with standard reranking approaches, which score the trajectory as a whole and thereby dilute the signal at the critical reasoning transition. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves the SOTA performance across the five benchmarks on both student backbones.
