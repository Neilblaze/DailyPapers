- **Summary**: Applications use Typed Decision Models to route and moderate text in many languages, and they often act only on decisions above a confidence threshold. ### Information-Theoretic Design of Sparse Autoencoder-Based Steering
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01378v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01378v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-01 00:33:01+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-01 00:33:01+00:00
- **Authors**: Hirofumi Morise
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Sparse autoencoder (SAE)-based activation steering is a lightweight approach for modifying the behavior of large language models (LLMs) by adding small vectors to hidden states during inference. However, designing effective steering interventions remains challenging. In this work, we develop an information-theoretic framework for analyzing SAE activations through empirical feature-firing distributions. The central object of the framework is the Jensen--Shannon divergence (JSD) between such firing distributions, which quantifies how far a generated or intervened state deviates from a dataset-induced target distribution. Based on an analytic divergence response for virtually shifted states, we construct steering vectors toward the target firing distribution. The same divergence can also be used to monitor deviation from another empirical distribution, such as a general valid-language distribution. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the steering in a question-mode generation task study. We further use teacher-forced pulse experiments to show that local interventions induce structured downstream JSD responses in later layers, and apply the framework to a safety-related target--contrast response setting for feature analysis.

- **Summary**: Activation steering controls large language models at inference time by adding a vector to their internal representations. Most work asks how to build that vector, and far less attention has gone to where to add it, even though steering at a poorly chosen layer can be worse than not steering at all. Prior selectors choose a layer without ever steering the model to test it: they score how separable the target behavior is at that layer, and how consistently the individual examples point along the steering direction. - **Summary**: Scaling laws predict the outcome of large, expensive training runs by extrapolating trends from smaller, cheaper runs.
