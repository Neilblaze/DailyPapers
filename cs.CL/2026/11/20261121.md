### Muted Distillation: Sealing Reasoning from Teacher Unlocks Token-Efficient Agents
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28445v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28445v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 00:50:35+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 00:50:35+00:00
- **Authors**: Yuwei Zhang, Ian Miao, Wentao Zhang, Chen Qu, Bill Bi, Jingbo Shang, Donna Schut
- **Comment**: Ongoning work
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: On-policy distillation (OPD) is commonly adopted as one of the components in post-training of large language models (LLMs), but in multi-turn agentic tasks we find that the student model increases their reasoning lengths after training. Our empirical analysis shows that the reasoning token lengths is affected by how much the student can distract the teacher's scores. We introduce Muted Distillation (MD), a simple variant of OPD that hides the student's reasoning from the teacher, so the teacher judges what the student does without the interference of student's reasonings. MD takes a few lines of code and adds no extra data or hyperparameters. On the knowledge-work benchmarks OfficeQA Pro and SpreadsheetBench, it reduces output tokens per episode by 57\% and 53\% compared with standard OPD, making the student more concise than it was before training. At the same time, it matches OPD's accuracy on OfficeQA Pro and improves pass@1 by 4.8 points on SpreadsheetBench.



### Digital Knockoffs
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28482v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.LG
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28482v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 01:59:39+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 01:59:39+00:00
- **Authors**: Niuniu Zhang
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Social simulations often use language model agents to represent people who differ on several measurable traits. However, describing a trait profile in a prompt does not guarantee the agent will express each trait at its intended level. We propose a method that calibrates agents to multidimensional profiles using activation steering: (i) extract steering directions for each trait, (ii) learn a shared mapping from steering strengths to measured trait scores, and (iii) search for a steering intervention for each target. We use Bayesian optimization for this search and evaluate the method on the Big Five personality scores of 500 OpenPsychometrics respondents. In each of five rounds, the assigned interventions yield lower mean absolute error across respondents than the prompting and steering baselines. Further, the model's responses to a given intervention yield the same trait profile regardless of whose target motivated it, so a poor match for one respondent can be the best match for another. When we match each respondent to the closest profile found across all tested interventions, Bayesian optimization yields 16.6% lower mean absolute error than uniform random search at the same evaluation budget. Our method thus offers a way to build social agents whose measured traits dovetail with those of the people they seek to represent. Whether this closer fit translates to improved predictions of downstream behavior remains an open question.

### Digital Twins for Multi-Agent Systems: When to Trust, What to Change
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28517v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.AI, cs.MA
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28517v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 03:09:42+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 03:09:42+00:00
- **Authors**: Truc Nguyen Thi Le, Phuoc Nguyen T. H
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Digital twins of agent networks need to flag unreliable forecasts and help users choose interventions. Both decisions depend on what happens over several steps: an error can spread through the network, and an edit whose trajectory is easy to predict may still yield a poor collective outcome. We ask when information about error propagation improves failure detection and whether a learned twin can select effective network edits despite imperfect forecasts. We propose Propagation-Aware Validation for Networked Digital Twins (PAV-Twin). Its monitor combines Jacobian-based propagation features with recent errors and ensemble disagreement to estimate the risk that a forecast exceeds an error threshold. Its planner simulates each candidate edit and ranks the edits by their predicted collective outcomes. In simulations, the monitor gained 0.13 in area under the precision-recall curve on larger oscillator networks with familiar topologies and 0.09 on larger networks with held-out topologies, relative to a graph baseline without propagation features. Gains were small in contractive consensus. Giving the graph baseline the same features narrowed the gap. In a separate planning study, ensemble simulation recovered about 99% of the possible improvement over random intervention selection in oscillators and nearly all of it in consensus. With a simplified oscillator twin, selecting by predicted outcome also gave lower regret than selecting by trajectory fidelity. These results show when propagation features help flag unreliable forecasts and why the quality of intervention choices should be measured directly.

- **Summary**: Strong results in multi-hop retrieval-augmented question answering come from graph indexes read by models of 70B parameters or more, and building such an index calls a large language model over the whole corpus. We present SupportChain, which splits a question into hops, binds each hop's answer to a sentence quoted from a retrieved passage, estimates how reliable each execution is from its own trace, and merges several executions by the support of their answers, the pooled reliability of the executions that agree, stopping once support is high enough. The reliability model needs only 100 answered training questions.



### DisSparse: Pipelined Top-$p$ Sparse Attention for Long-Context LLM Serving
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28522v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.LG**, cs.CL, cs.DC
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28522v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-21 03:18:37+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-21 03:18:37+00:00
- **Authors**: Nurlan Nazaraliyev, Elaheh Sadredini, Nael Abu-Ghazaleh
- **Comment**: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/nnurlan008/DisSparse
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Long-context LLM inference is bottlenecked by the memory demands of the key-value (KV) cache, whose size scales linearly with context length. Dynamic sparse attention reduces this bottleneck by fetching and attending only to a critical subset of tokens. The dominant primitive in dynamic sparse attention research and deployment is fixed-budget top-$k$ selection, which is favored because its deterministic block count aligns with the static memory allocation that systems like vLLM rely on. Top-$k$ uses a fixed budget regardless of how attention mass is distributed across heads, layers, and decode steps, leading to either wasted bandwidth on heads with peaky attention or dropped context on heads with diffuse attention. Top-$p$ (nucleus) selection adapts naturally to this variation but has seen limited adoption in production serving systems due to two system-level challenges: variable block counts that break deterministic block allocation and computationally expensive selection kernels that exceed the cost of attention itself. We present DisSparse, a serving system that integrates top-$p$ sparse attention into vLLM end-to-end. DisSparse decouples block selection from the scheduler's critical path, hides selection latency behind layer computation via a two-stream pipeline, and introduces a single-pass histogram-based top-$p$ kernel. We further observe intra-block sparsity, the inflation of selection budgets caused by mixing high- and low-importance tokens within fixed-size KV cache blocks, and address it with a token relocation kernel guided by accumulated prefill attention scores. Across long- and medium-context benchmarks on multiple models, DisSparse achieves 1.17x to 1.80x speedup in sparsity analysis (block selection) over state-of-the-art top-$p$ methods with negligible accuracy loss and supports up to 8x larger maximum batch sizes than dense vLLM under the same memory budget.

- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A risky choice can be described by what will be saved or what will be lost, and people often choose differently depending on that wording. We test whether six large language models (LLMs) show this gain-loss framing effect, and whether it shrinks when they can first read more about the options. GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen3 235B and Llama 4 Maverick made 11,519 choices across four risk scenarios, each in its original wording, two paraphrases and a mirrored version in the opposite wording. Part of the drop from 0 to 2 cards may reflect a change in prompt format, and most card content counted against the risky option, which may favor the safe choice. With these cards, in the balanced comparison 17 of 18 card gaps were below the model's no-card gap, but few were within the threshold.

- **Summary**: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) increasingly use external visual tools to acquire evidence during reasoning, yet learning when to invoke these tools remains challenging. - **Summary**: Self-rewarding optimization (SRO) promises to improve language models by enabling them to generate their own reward signals. We quantify document evolution using lightweight linguistic and structural signals grounded in prior work on procedural language, technical communication, and document organization. Correlation analysis shows mixed patterns of association, including both positive and negative relationships, with effect sizes ranging from negligible to moderate and varying across corpora. Temporal summaries further distinguish comparatively stable signals from revision-sensitive ones, supporting monitoring of document maintenance workflows rather than one-shot quality judgments. An illustrative analysis on public GitHub product documentation shows that signal behavior differs across corpora, reinforcing the need for context-aware interpretation. These results identify which metric classes provide stable anchors and which provide edit-sensitive indicators for managing operational documentation as an engineered document resource.
