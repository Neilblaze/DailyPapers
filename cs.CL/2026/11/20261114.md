- **Summary**: Surveys help researchers keep up with fast-moving fields, but they take weeks to months to write. - **Comment**: Submitted to ACL ARR 2026
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Instruction-guided text-to-speech (ITTS) lets users specify voice properties in natural language. Applications from conversational AI character voices to synthetic training data for downstream speech models depend on the system producing many distinct voices when the same broad category is requested repeatedly. No prior work audits whether ITTS outputs are in fact diverse, either compared to real speech or across attribute values. These results show that diversity and controllability capture complementary weaknesses, neither of which alone is sufficient to evaluate ITTS. - **Summary**: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to design, optimize, and operate the infrastructure behind AI training and serving, yet the literature remains fragmented across hardware design, machine learning systems, electronic design automation, and high-performance computing. We present a comprehensive survey of LLM-assisted AI infrastructure across its lifecycle, from accelerator design and kernel engineering to compilation, deployment, operations, data management, and energy optimization. We organize the literature by the primary resource the LLM manages: artifact-centric methods for computational artifacts, operational-centric methods for deployment context and system state, and resource-centric methods for data and energy. Together, the taxonomy, lifecycle perspective, and quantitative evaluation provide an evidence-grounded reference for LLM-driven engineering of AI infrastructure.

- **Summary**: Generative models play a rapidly growing role in the design and simulation of molecules. ### The Geometry of Conceptors in Neural-Activation Spaces
- **Arxiv ID**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19075v1
- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19075v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 01:25:00+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 01:25:00+00:00
- **Authors**: Ilias Triantafyllopoulos, Young Min Cho, Ren Tao, Miranda Muqing Miao, Sunny Rai, Lyle Ungar, Sharath Chandra Guntuku, Sunghye Cho, Tyler Moore, Neville Ryant, João Sedoc
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: Conceptors represent concepts as graded collections of directions in neural-activation space rather than as single vectors. We develop a geometric account of how these representations depend on the contrast used to define a concept: opposing values in bipolar fitting, or presence and absence in unipolar fitting. We derive an exact decomposition of a centered pooled conceptor into an average pole filter, a mean-separation update, and a covariance-shape update. Experiments across five language models on sentiment, political leaning and stance, and depression expression show that both updates improve reconstruction of held-out contrasts. Additional directions preserve variation beyond the mean-difference line, and changing the comparator changes the benefit of these directions. Shape, size, and overlap each show up downstream: in amplification experiments, bipolar fits raise evidence for both poles more consistently than unipolar fits; conceptor size (the quota) correlates positively with layerwise probe performance; and cross-concept overlap quantifies interference under subspace removal, linking shared directions to Boolean composition. In an additional generation evaluation, conceptor filters achieve higher target-expression win rates against an unsteered reference than three mean-based controls. Together, these findings explain how fitting choices shape conceptor geometry and support bipolar fitting when the objective is to amplify both opposing poles.

- **Summary**: Cuneiform recorded typologically unrelated languages for more than three millennia, raising a natural question: which of its statistical regularities are properties of the shared writing system, and which belong to individual languages? We release CUNEI-TOOLS and a three-language benchmark.



### EEGHyena2: Fixation-Inspired Joint Lexical-Grammatical Conditional Random Field for Interpretable EEG-to-Text Decoding

- **DOI**: None
- **Categories**: **cs.CL**, cs.ET
- **Links**: [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19104v1)
- **Published**: 2026-11-14 02:29:26+00:00
- **Updated**: 2026-11-14 02:29:26+00:00
- **Authors**: Xiaoshan Zhou, Chenlan Liu
- **Comment**: None
- **Journal**: None
- **Summary**: A sentence is built from words that carry lexical meaning and grammatical roles, and brain activity during reading reflects both. Current EEG-to-text decoders, however, let a large language model (LLM) propose and order the words, so fluent output can reflect the LLM's prior more than the recorded EEG. In this study, we asked whether fixation-linked EEG carries lexical and grammatical evidence during natural reading and how a decoder can use such evidence. Fixation-related potential analyses showed that content and function words differ reliably in group-averaged EEG but only weakly in single words. We therefore propose EEGHyena2, a structured decoder in which a bidirectional encoder inspired by StripedHyena 2 feeds a semantic head that ranks a fixed lexicon and a part-of-speech (POS) head that predicts grammatical categories; a joint word-POS conditional random field (CRF) scores a restricted candidate lattice, and a frozen Llama-2 scores the candidates in context. The CRF learned transitions that mirror English syntax, and the encoder drew local context from its convolutions and distant context from attention. Decoders can thus pool weak word-level EEG evidence across the sentence through explicit lexical and grammatical structure, with the language model adjudicating among candidates rather than generating them, which lowers decoding cost and keeps decisions inspectable.

- **Summary**: Model Context Protocol (MCP) gateways manage tool catalogs for language model agents using two primary paradigms: code mode where agents write scripts against tool stubs and decision layers which are cheap routers that preselect tool subsets.
